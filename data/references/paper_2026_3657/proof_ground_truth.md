# Reference Proof

## Proof Structure and Obligations

The proof must establish: (i) applicability of the fixed-point transportation-information inequality to every update; (ii) a componentwise one-block Wasserstein bound; (iii) invariance of the local product ball, including every intermediate sequential state and every random-scan history; (iv) the three schedule recurrences; (v) spectral radius below one for the random-scan and sequential matrices; and (vi) exponential bounds for the deterministic norms and the expected random-scan norm. All these obligations are closed below.

## Complete Proof

Write e(mu)_i=W_p(mu^i,mu_*^i), and let B be the product set defined by e(mu)_i<=c delta_i for all i. By hypothesis B is contained in U.

### One-block bound and invariant region

For any mu in U, fixed-center Fisher-smoothness and the fixed-point identity give

I(Gamma_i[mu_{-i}]||mu_*^i)^(1/2)
 <= sum_{j!=i} L_ij W_p(mu^j,mu_*^j)<infinity.

The right side is finite because all inputs and fixed-point marginals belong to P_p. By the definition of I, the update is absolutely continuous with respect to mu_*^i and has finite relative Fisher information. Thus the assumed transportation-information inequality applies and yields

lambda_i W_p(Gamma_i[mu_{-i}],mu_*^i)
 <= I(Gamma_i[mu_{-i}]||mu_*^i)^(1/2)
 <= sum_{j!=i} L_ij W_p(mu^j,mu_*^j).

Dividing by lambda_i gives the one-block bound

W_p(Gamma_i[mu_{-i}],mu_*^i) <= (R e(mu))_i.  (1)

If mu belongs to B, then

(R e(mu))_i <= c(R delta)_i <= c q delta_i < c delta_i.

Consequently, replacing any one coordinate by its update preserves B. Updating every coordinate from the same old state also preserves B. This proves invariance for the parallel and random schedules and, by induction over coordinates, for every intermediate state of a sequential sweep. Induction over iterations then ensures that all applications of (1) take place within U.

The positive sub-eigenvector is given in the task. Its existence requires no irreducibility: for q>rho(R), the convergent series delta=(qI-R)^(-1)1=sum_{n>=0}q^(-n-1)R^n1 has positive entries and satisfies R delta=q delta-1<q delta. A sufficiently small c exists because U is a product neighborhood of the fixed point.

### Parallel updates

Applying (1) to each coordinate of the parallel update gives

e_{k+1}<=R e_k.

Since R is nonnegative, induction gives e_k<=R^k e_0.

### Uniform random scan

Let F_k be the sigma-field generated by the first k coordinate choices. The initialization is fixed, the update maps are deterministic, and at each finite time the state is a function of finitely many finite-valued choices. Thus all variables used here are measurable. Invariance also gives the deterministic bound 0<=e_k<=c delta, so they are integrable.

Conditioned on F_k, the next coordinate is uniform and independent of the past. Coordinate i is updated with probability 1/M, in which case its new error is at most (R e_k)_i. Otherwise its error remains (e_k)_i. Therefore

E[e_{k+1}|F_k] <= (R/M+(M-1)I_M/M)e_k=A e_k.

Since e_k is F_k-measurable, the tower property also gives E[e_{k+1}|e_k]<=A e_k. Taking total expectations and using nonnegativity of A gives

E e_k<=A^k e_0.

For completeness, rho(A)=(M-1+rho(R))/M. Every eigenvalue of A is (M-1+z)/M for an eigenvalue z of R, and hence its modulus is at most (M-1+rho(R))/M. The Perron-Frobenius fact needed for the reverse inequality is that the spectral radius of a finite nonnegative matrix is itself an eigenvalue; its hypothesis holds because R is entrywise nonnegative. Thus equality holds, and rho(A)<1.

### Systematic sequential updates

Consider one sweep. Before updating coordinate i, all lower-index coordinates have their new values and all higher-index coordinates have their old values. The invariant-region argument above justifies applying (1) at this intermediate state. Hence

(e_{k+1})_i <= sum_{j<i}R_ij(e_{k+1})_j + sum_{j>i}R_ij(e_k)_j.

After all coordinates have been updated, these inequalities give

(I_M-R_L)e_{k+1}<=R_U e_k.

Since R_L is strictly lower triangular, R_L^M=0 and

H=(I_M-R_L)^(-1)=I_M+R_L+...+R_L^(M-1)

is entrywise nonnegative. Multiplication by H preserves componentwise order, so e_{k+1}<=G e_k, where G=H R_U. Iteration yields e_k<=G^k e_0.

The relevant Stein-Rosenberg implication is: if R is a finite nonnegative matrix with zero diagonal, R=R_L+R_U is its strict triangular splitting, and rho(R)<1, then rho((I_M-R_L)^(-1)R_U)<1. All these hypotheses hold here. The implication can also be verified directly in this setting, closing the spectral obligation without relying on an unstated version of that theorem. Indeed,

delta-G delta=H(delta-R delta)>0,

because delta-R delta>0 and H is nonnegative with identity as one of its summands. Thus beta=max_i (G delta)_i/delta_i<1. The weighted maximum norm ||v||_delta=max_i |v_i|/delta_i satisfies ||Gv||_delta<=beta||v||_delta. In particular rho(G)<=beta<1. This argument includes reducible matrices and rho(R)=0.

### Exponential bounds and asymptotic upper rates

For a finite matrix B and any r>rho(B), there is a finite K_{B,r} such that ||B^k||_1<=K_{B,r}r^k for all k>=0. Here is the elementary matrix-power justification. In complex Jordan form, powers of a block zI+N, with N nilpotent, are finite sums of binomial(k,l) z^(k-l)N^l. If z is nonzero, each such term is bounded by a constant times r^k because |z|<r and the binomial factor grows only polynomially. A zero-eigenvalue block has only finitely many nonzero powers, which can be absorbed into the constant. Conjugating back proves the assertion. This also justifies the convergent resolvent series used above.

Apply this bound to R and G. The error vectors are nonnegative, and therefore componentwise domination implies domination of their l1 norms. Consequently, for each schedule and each q' strictly between its spectral radius and one,

||e_k||_1 <= K_{B,q'}||e_0||_1(q')^k,

where B=R or G.

For random scan, nonnegativity gives the essential identity

E||e_k||_1=sum_i E(e_k)_i=||E e_k||_1.

Hence

E||e_k||_1 <= ||A^k e_0||_1 <= K_{A,q'}||e_0||_1(q')^k

for every q' in (rho(A),1). For any other fixed norm on R^M, an upper comparison with the l1 norm gives the same conclusions with a changed constant. These constants are independent of k and, for random scan, of the realized scan history.

Taking k-th roots and then allowing q' to decrease to the relevant spectral radius proves the stated limsup upper bounds. No equality between a trajectory's actual rate and the matrix spectral radius is required. The argument includes zero initial error and zero spectral radius. All three claims follow.
