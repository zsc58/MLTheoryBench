# Reference Proof

## Obligations and proof structure

The proof must establish: (1) well-defined risk-set summaries and high-probability invertibility; (2) the observed counting-process martingales and cancellation of each site's baseline; (3) convergence of the information matrices and an exact score identity; (4) sitewise bracket limits and a justified martingale CLT, followed by the correct site-weight aggregation; and (5) consistency of the empirical event covariance summaries and the weighted sandwich. All of these obligations are closed below.

The independent background results used are: the counting-process compensator formula under conditional independent censoring; the iid law of large numbers and its bounded marked-threshold uniform version; the martingale CLT for square-integrable vector martingales with deterministic continuous limiting brackets and vanishing uniformly bounded jumps; Slutsky's theorem; and continuity of matrix inversion at a nonsingular matrix. No specialized additive-risk theorem is assumed.

## 1. Risk-set laws and information matrices

Let B be the common finite bound on the covariate norm. For site k put
\[
r_k(t)=E[R_{1k}(t)],\qquad b_k(t)=E[R_{1k}(t)X_{1k}],
\]
and let r_{k,n} and b_{k,n} be their empirical averages. Since R(t)=1{Y>=t},
\[
\inf_{0\le t\le\tau}r_k(t)=r_k(\tau)>0.
\]
Only this unconditional denominator bound is needed; a uniform conditional lower bound in X is not needed.

For iid pairs (Y_i,W_i) with bounded scalar W_i, the marked-threshold uniform law states
\[
\sup_t\left|m^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^m W_i1\{Y_i\ge t\}-E[W1\{Y\ge t\}]\right|\to_p0.
\]
One obtains this from the iid law of large numbers on finitely many brackets for the monotone marked tail measure. For nonnegative marks, separate its large atoms and partition the remainder into intervals of arbitrarily small measure; monotonicity controls every threshold between partition points. Decompose signed marks into positive and negative parts. Thus this law applies to W=1 and to every coordinate of X, since the observations are iid within each site and the marks are bounded.

Consequently, uniformly on [0,tau], r_{k,n}->r_k and b_{k,n}->b_k in probability. Ratio continuity with the positive population denominator gives
\[
e_{k,n}:=\sup_{t\le\tau}\|\bar X_k(t)-\mu_k(t)\|\to_p0.
\]
In particular, all empirical risk denominators are positive throughout the interval with probability tending to one. The zero convention makes the summaries defined on the remaining outcomes. It also gives ||bar X_k||<=B everywhere, and ||mu_k||<=B.

For vectors a,b with norms at most 2B,
\[
\|aa^T-bb^T\|\le(\|a\|+\|b\|)\|a-b\|.
\]
It follows that replacing bar X_k by mu_k in Ahat_k costs at most 4B tau e_{k,n} in operator norm. The resulting expression is an average of iid bounded random matrices
\[
\int_0^\tau R_{ik}(t)(X_{ik}-\mu_k(t))^{\otimes2}dt.
\]
The iid law of large numbers therefore proves Ahat_k->p A_k. Since K is fixed and p_{k,n}->p_k,
\[
\widehat A_S\to_p A_S=\sum_kp_kA_k.
\]
For every nonzero v, v^T A_S v=sum_k p_k v^T A_k v>0. Hence A_S is positive definite, P(Ahat_S is invertible)->1, and Ahat_S^{-1}->p A_S^{-1} on those events.

The source also states positive definiteness of pooled information. This is redundant for this chain. Indeed, for the mixture having site probabilities p_k, define r=sum_k p_k r_k and mu=sum_k p_k r_k mu_k/r. Its pooled information equals
\[
A_{\rm pool}=A_S+\int_0^\tau\sum_kp_kr_k(t)(\mu_k(t)-\mu(t))^{\otimes2}dt\succeq A_S.
\]
Thus the inherited pooled condition is satisfied without introducing an additional public assumption or target.

## 2. Martingales, baseline cancellation, and exact linearization

Use the filtration generated by all baseline covariates and the observed event and censoring histories up to time t, with its usual augmentation. Future event or censoring times are not initially revealed. The at-risk processes and the empirical risk-set means, including their empty-set convention, are predictable.

Write h_{ik}(t)=lambda_{0k}(t)+beta_0^T X_{ik}. The continuous-time hazard model and conditional independent censoring give
\[
M_{ik}(t)=N_{ik}(t)-\int_0^tR_{ik}(s)h_{ik}(s)ds
\]
as subject martingales. Their predictable brackets are the displayed compensators. Define
\[
g_k(t)=\lambda_{0k}(t)+B\|\beta_0\|,\qquad H_k=\int_0^\tau g_k(t)dt<\infty.
\]
The hazard is nonnegative on the model's covariate support and bounded above by g_k. Consequently the compensators are bounded by H_k, and the martingales are square-integrable. Independent subjects with continuous failure-time hazards have no simultaneous failure jumps almost surely; their martingales are orthogonal. These facts hold in the joint observed-history filtration because subjects are independent.

Define
\[
S_k(\beta)=\sum_i\int_0^\tau (X_{ik}-\bar X_k(t))\{dN_{ik}(t)-\beta^TX_{ik}R_{ik}(t)dt\},\qquad S(\beta)=\sum_kS_k(\beta).
\]
The risk-set identity
\[
\sum_iR_{ik}(t)(X_{ik}-\bar X_k(t))=0
\]
holds even when the risk set is empty. It cancels each site's baseline contribution, giving
\[
S_k(\beta_0)=\sum_i\int_0^\tau(X_{ik}-\bar X_k(t))dM_{ik}(t).
\]
The same identity shows that
\[
\sum_i R_{ik}(t)(X_{ik}-\bar X_k(t))X_{ik}^T
=\sum_iR_{ik}(t)(X_{ik}-\bar X_k(t))^{\otimes2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
S_k(\beta)=n_k(D_k-\widehat A_k\beta),\qquad
S(\beta)=S(\beta_0)-n\widehat A_S(\beta-\beta_0).
\]
This is exact, with derivative independent of beta and no Taylor remainder. On the invertibility event, the estimator is the unique root and
\[
\sqrt n(\widehat\beta_S-\beta_0)=\widehat A_S^{-1}n^{-1/2}S(\beta_0).
\]

## 3. Sitewise predictable brackets and the CLT

For 0<=t<=tau, let
\[
Z_{k,n}(t)=n_k^{-1/2}\sum_i\int_0^t(X_{ik}-\bar X_k(s))dM_{ik}(s).
\]
Predictability and square integrability have been verified. Orthogonality gives its matrix bracket
\[
\langle Z_{k,n}\rangle(t)=\frac1{n_k}\sum_i\int_0^t
(X_{ik}-\bar X_k(s))^{\otimes2}R_{ik}(s)h_{ik}(s)ds.
\]
Replacing bar X_k by mu_k changes this bracket, uniformly in t, by at most 4B H_k e_{k,n}, which is o_p(1). At each fixed t, the replacement is an iid average of bounded random matrices and converges to
\[
Q_k(t)=E\left[\int_0^t(X_{1k}-\mu_k(s))^{\otimes2}R_{1k}(s)h_{1k}(s)ds\right].
\]
The convergence is uniform in t: empirical and population increments between u and v have norm at most 4B^2 integral_u^v g_k(s)ds. A finite partition on which these integrals are uniformly small, combined with the iid law at its endpoints, proves the uniform assertion. This also shows that Q_k is deterministic and continuous, with Q_k(0)=0.

By the compensator identity for the bounded predictable integrand (X-mu_k)^{tensor 2},
\[
Q_k(\tau)=E\left[\int_0^\tau(X_{1k}-\mu_k(t))^{\otimes2}dN_{1k}(t)\right]=\Sigma_k.
\]
There are almost surely no simultaneous subject failure jumps. A jump of Z_{k,n} therefore has norm at most 2B/sqrt(n_k), a deterministic bound tending to zero. This verifies the small-jump/Lindeberg condition, not merely bracket convergence. The martingale CLT now gives
\[
n_k^{-1/2}S_k(\beta_0)\Rightarrow N(0,\Sigma_k).
\]
The theorem allows singular Sigma_k; the corresponding normal law is then degenerate.

The K site scores are independent because each uses only its own site's observations. Their characteristic functions factor, so their marginal limits give joint convergence to independent Gaussian vectors. Since n_k/n->p_k,
\[
n^{-1/2}S(\beta_0)=\sum_k\sqrt{p_{k,n}}\,n_k^{-1/2}S_k(\beta_0)
\Rightarrow N(0,\Sigma_S),\qquad \Sigma_S=\sum_kp_k\Sigma_k.
\]
This establishes both the correct weights and the normalized score's tightness.

## 4. Estimator limit and consistency

Combine the exact score identity, Ahat_S^{-1}->p A_S^{-1}, and the score CLT. Slutsky's theorem yields
\[
\sqrt n(\widehat\beta_S-\beta_0)\Rightarrow
N(0,A_S^{-1}\Sigma_S A_S^{-1}).
\]
The transpose on the rightmost inverse can be omitted because A_S is symmetric. The same identity gives beta_hat_S-beta_0=O_p(n^{-1/2}) and hence consistency. No prior consistency assertion is needed to justify linearization.

These conclusions are unaffected by setting the estimator to zero on singular-information outcomes, since their probabilities tend to zero.

## 5. Empirical covariance and sandwich consistency

For each site, replacing bar X_k by mu_k in Sigmahat_k costs at most
\[
4B e_{k,n}\frac1{n_k}\sum_i N_{ik}(\tau)\le4B e_{k,n}=o_p(1).
\]
After replacement, the summands are iid bounded matrices
\[
\int_0^\tau(X_{ik}-\mu_k(t))^{\otimes2}dN_{ik}(t),
\]
each with norm at most 4B^2, since a subject has at most one observed failure. Their expectation is Sigma_k. Thus Sigmahat_k->p Sigma_k. Together with the information convergence already proved,
\[
\widehat A_S\to_pA_S,\qquad \widehat\Sigma_S\to_p\Sigma_S.
\]
Continuity of inversion and multiplication gives
\[
\widehat V_S\to_p V_S:=A_S^{-1}\Sigma_S A_S^{-1}.
\]
The zero extension on singular outcomes again has no effect. Consequently, if Chat_S=n^{-1}Vhat_S, then n Chat_S->p V_S: Chat_S is the estimator-scale asymptotic covariance estimate. This does not assert existence or convergence of the actual finite-sample covariance of beta_hat_S; such a moment assertion does not follow from weak convergence alone.
