# Reference Proof

Let K=(K_1,K_2), a_i=a+bK_i, and let H be the set of gains with |a_i|<=1-delta/2. All constants below are selected before m. Classical scalar discounted LQR gives P*>0, K*=-gamma*a*b*P*/(r+gamma*b^2*P*), and |a+bK*|<=|a|. Thus K^o=(K*,K*) belongs to H.

For K in H, put f_i=q+rK_i^2. Define the 2-by-2 nonnegative matrix A(K) by placing a_i^2 in row i and in the column of the half-line reached from side i; its other row entry is zero. At a_i=0 the row is zero, independently of the assigned next side. Each row sum is at most (1-delta/2)^2. The Bellman equations give P(K)=f(K)+gamma*A(K)*P(K), so P(K)=(I-gamma*A(K))^(-1)f(K). The inverse is its convergent Neumann series. The cost-to-go is P_i(K)*x^2 on half-line i. Write h=(sigma_rho^2/2,sigma_rho^2/2) as a row vector. Balanced initial second moments yield J_rho(K)=h*P(K). In particular q<=P_i(K)<=P_max, 0<=Delta(K):=J_rho(K)-J_rho(K^o)<=Delta_max, and K and P range over bounded sets. The entries a_i^2*1_{a_i>=0} and a_i^2*1_{a_i<0} have Lipschitz first derivatives, including at zero. Differentiating the bounded resolvent therefore shows that J_rho has an L_mu-Lipschitz gain gradient on the convex set H and a finite uniform gradient bound G_max. The constants may be enlarged while retaining these properties.

We derive the gain-gradient and performance identities explicitly. For each integer t>=0, [h*A(K)^t]_i is E_rho[x_t^2*1_{x_t on side i}]: each row of A propagates the squared state along its deterministic next half-line, and h supplies the two initial second moments. This remains true if a multiplier is zero, since that trajectory thereafter contributes zero. Thus the discounted, unnormalized second-moment occupancy is the row vector d(K):=h*(I-gamma*A(K))^(-1), with components d_i(K)=sum_{t>=0}gamma^t*E_rho[x_t^2*1_{x_t on side i}]. If c_i(K) denotes the normalized occupancy, c_i(K)=(1-gamma)*d_i(K). Its time-zero term gives d_i(K)>=sigma_rho^2/2.

For fixed P=P(K), define F_i(z)=q+r*z^2+gamma*P_{next(i,z)}*(a+b*z)^2, taking the last term as zero when a+b*z=0. On side 1 the last term is gamma*[P_1*(a+b*z)_+^2+P_2*(a+b*z)_-^2]; on side 2 the coefficients P_1 and P_2 are interchanged. Therefore F_i is continuously differentiable and 2r-strongly convex on the whole real line. At z=K_i its derivative is F_i'(K_i)=2r*K_i+2gamma*b*a_i*P_{next(i,K_i)}; the second summand is zero if a_i=0. Only row i of A and component i of f depend on K_i. Differentiating (I-gamma*A(K))*P(K)=f(K) gives partial_i P(K)=(I-gamma*A(K))^(-1)*e_i*[2r*K_i+2gamma*b*a_i*P_{next(i,K_i)}]. Indeed, the derivative of the sole nonzero entry in row i, after multiplication by P, is 2b*a_i*P_{next(i,K_i)}; both one-sided derivatives are zero at a_i=0. Multiplying by h proves, without an interchange of infinite sums and derivatives, that g_i:=partial_i J_rho(K)=d_i(K)*F_i'(K_i)=c_i(K)*F_i'(K_i)/(1-gamma).

For the second identity, abbreviate A^o=A(K^o), f^o=f(K^o), P=P(K), and P^o=P(K^o)=(P*,P*). Subtract the two Bellman equations, arranging them with the transition matrix of K^o on the left:

(I-gamma*A^o)*(P-P^o)=f(K)+gamma*A(K)*P-f^o-gamma*A^o*P.

The right-hand component i is exactly F_i(K_i)-F_i(K*), with F_i defined using the fixed vector P(K), not P^o. Multiplication by h*(I-gamma*A^o)^(-1)=d(K^o) therefore gives the exact identity Delta(K)=sum_i d_i(K^o)*[F_i(K_i)-F_i(K*)]. This is the switched-half-line performance calculation required for PL. Strong convexity gives F_i(K_i)-F_i(K*)<=F_i'(K_i)*(K_i-K*)-r*(K_i-K*)^2<=F_i'(K_i)^2/(4r). Because |a+bK*|<1, |x_t|<=|x_0| along K^o, and hence d_i(K^o)<=sigma_rho^2/[2*(1-gamma)]. Combining this with d_i(K)>=sigma_rho^2/2 and g_i=d_i(K)*F_i'(K_i) yields Delta(K)<=||g||_2^2/[2*r*sigma_rho^2*(1-gamma)]. Thus (1/2)||g||_2^2>=alpha_mu*Delta(K) for the valid choice alpha_mu=r*sigma_rho^2*(1-gamma). No specialized external policy-gradient corollary is needed.

For quadratic growth, instead arrange the same Bellman equations with A(K) on the left: (I-gamma*A(K))*(P(K)-P^o)=f(K)+gamma*A(K)*P^o-P^o. The scalar optimality equation for P* and completion of squares show that its component i equals (r+gamma*b^2*P*)*(K_i-K*)^2, regardless of the side reached, because both components of P^o equal P*. Multiplying by h*(I-gamma*A(K))^(-1)=d(K) proves Delta(K)=(r+gamma*b^2*P*)*sum_i d_i(K)*(K_i-K*)^2. In particular Delta(K)>=kappa*||K-K^o||_2^2, where kappa=(r+gamma*b^2*P*)*sigma_rho^2/2. Since K^o is interior to H and its gradient is zero, smoothness gives ||g||_2^2<=C_grad*Delta(K), with C_grad=L_mu^2/kappa. This closes both cost-gap estimates and the gain-gradient estimates used below.

Take independent standard normal Z_j,U_j and write w_{j,0}=sqrt(beta/m)*Z_j, v_{j,0}=sqrt(alpha/m)*U_j. Products contributing to each initial gain are independent across j, centered, and subexponential at scale O(1/m). The standard Bernstein inequality for sums of independent centered subexponentials gives an exponential-in-m tail for a fixed positive deviation. With delta<delta_0, it puts K(theta_0) in K_delta with failure probability exp(-Omega(m)). Standard chi-square upper tails give total initial squared mass at most R_*^2 with the same form of bound. On each side the backbone membership indicators are independent Bernoulli variables with mean p=(Phi(S)-Phi(s))*(2*Phi(V*beta)-1)>c. A binomial Chernoff lower tail gives at least c*m members per side. Each bad-set mass is a sum of m independent, nonnegative, Gaussian-square-dominated terms of size 1/m. Bernstein's inequality and the strict inequalities defining M_w,M_v bound both masses by their thresholds with failure probability exp(-Omega(m)). A union bound, with a smaller C_0 and larger m_0 if necessary, produces the asserted good event. These concentration statements apply to fixed beta and fixed deterministic thresholds, independently of m.

Use the zero-ReLU derivative convention in the task. At a neuron with w=0 both selected weight gradients are zero; it stays zero and contributes nothing to either effective gain. For every other neuron on side i, its update is w'=w-eta_m*g_i*v and v'=v-eta_m*g_i*w. Multiplication yields w'v'=wv-eta_m*g_i*(w^2+v^2)+eta_m^2*g_i^2*wv. Summing over the current sign groups, excluding any frozen zero neurons, gives K_{k+1}=K_k-eta_m*D_k*g_k+eta_m^2*h_k+e_k. Here D_k is diagonal with entries equal to the sums of w^2+v^2 over active neurons in each group, h_{i,k}=K_{i,k}*g_{i,k}^2, and e_k reallocates post-update products of crossing neurons. Frozen zero neurons change none of these identities.

If eta_m*G_max<=1, a crossing neuron obeys |w'|<=eta_m*|g_i|*|v| and |v'|<=2|v|. Its reallocated product therefore has Euclidean gain-error norm at most 2*sqrt(2)*eta_m*|g_i|*v^2. Hence ||e_k||_2<=2*sqrt(2)*eta_m*||g_k||_2*Xi_k, with Xi_k the sum of v_{j,k}^2 over crossing neurons. Each active neuron update has operator norm at most 1+eta_m*||g_k||_2; a frozen zero neuron is unchanged. If eta_m*sum_{t=0}^k||g_t||_2<=B, all neuron radii through time k+1 are at most e^B times their initial radii and their first-layer displacements are at most e^B*B times those radii. The inequality e^B*B<1 and the definition of tau_w imply that a neuron initially outside the two bad sets cannot cross zero. On the good event this proves Xi_t<=Xi_* for every step t whose cumulative gradient path is at most B.

We close the induction without assuming a future crossing bound. At time zero the good event supplies stability, total mass, and the two backbone counts. The bounds ||g_0||^2<=C_grad*Delta_max and eta_m*lambda_* *alpha_mu<=1 give eta_m*||g_0||<=B. Suppose stability and Delta_t<=rho_m^t*Delta_0 hold through t=k. Then ||g_t||<=sqrt(C_grad*Delta_max)*rho_m^(t/2) and, because 0<=rho_m=1-eta_m*lambda_* *alpha_mu<1, eta_m*sum_{t=0}^k||g_t||<=2*sqrt(C_grad*Delta_max)/(lambda_* *alpha_mu)=B. Radius and displacement bounds now apply through k+1, and the crossing-mass bound applies to step k. In particular total squared weight mass through k+1 is at most G_*. For an initial backbone neuron its radius is at most R_c/sqrt(m); e^B*B*R_c<=tau/2 keeps its initial sign and gives |w_{j,k+1}|>=tau/(2*sqrt(m)). Thus both diagonal entries of D_k are at least c*tau^2/4=lambda_*, and at most G_*.

The elementary inequality 2|wv|<=w^2+v^2 gives ||h_k||_2<=(G_*/2)*||g_k||_2^2. Together with the crossing bound and eta_m*G_max<=1, this yields ||K_{k+1}-K_k||_2<=eta_m*L_* *||g_k||_2. Summing from initialization gives ||K_{k+1}-K_0||_2<=L_* *B. Since K_0 is in K_delta and |b|*L_* *B<=delta/2, K_{k+1} is in K_{delta/2}. Smoothness on this convex safe set is therefore applicable to the completed step. It gives Delta_{k+1}<=Delta_k-eta_m*(lambda_*-2*sqrt(2)*Xi_*-eta_m*C_*)*||g_k||_2^2. The two benign-regime absorption inequalities make the parenthesis at least lambda_*/2. The PL bound then gives Delta_{k+1}<=rho_m*Delta_k. This closes the induction for every k; at the next induction step the newly established gap bound supplies the cumulative path needed to control Xi_{k+1}. The good event has probability at least 1-exp(-C_0*m).

Every safe multiplier has absolute value below one, so every iterate is stabilizing. Iterating the gap inequality proves the stated geometric bound. Finally the quadratic-growth bound with coefficient kappa converts it to the stated bound on (K_1-K*)^2+(K_2-K*)^2.
