# Reference Proof

## Proof Structure

First establish the elementary finite-history upper-closure identity and its monotonicity. Optimism then gives safe elimination and the witness trichotomy on a single full-probability event where all rounds terminate. For each optimal resource, its positive-measure witness set remains admissible while the resource is uncovered. The accepted sampling law and a precisely stated conditional Borel-Cantelli lemma rule out perpetual noncoverage. Finiteness of the optimal antichain and the two upper-closure inclusions give eventual equality.

## Complete Proof

Let \(S_f(H)=\{r(i):i\text{ is queried in }H,\ f\le p(i)\}\), and write \(U_t=\uparrow M_f(H_t)\).

For any finite subset \(S\) of a poset, every element of \(S\) lies above a minimal element of \(S\): starting at an element that is not minimal, descend strictly within \(S\); finiteness forces this process to terminate. Therefore
\[
\uparrow\min S=\uparrow S.
\]
This also holds for the empty set, whose minimal set and upper closure are empty. Applying this identity to \(S_f(H)\) gives
\[
\uparrow M_f(H)=\uparrow S_f(H).
\]
Since queried histories grow, \(S_f(H_t)\subseteq S_f(H_{t+1})\), and hence \(U_t\subseteq U_{t+1}\). In particular, once a resource is covered, it remains covered.

We next justify the stochastic procedure. The set \(D_n\) of distinct implementation tuples is Borel because \(I\) is Polish and its diagonal is closed. The assumed Borel acceptance graph makes membership of a fresh proposal in the admissible set measurable as a function of the previous queried tuple and that proposal. Integration of its indicator against \(\mu\) makes the acceptance probability measurable as a function of the queried tuple. Consequently successive accepted implementations can be constructed measurably using fresh independent proposal sequences, one sequence for each round.

For a fixed current history \(H\), set \(q=\mu(A(H))>0\). Conditional on the past, the probability that the first \(m\) fresh proposals all fail is \((1-q)^m\), which tends to zero. Thus that round terminates almost surely. Induction over rounds and a countable union of null events give one measurable event \(\Omega_0\) of probability one on which every round terminates. On this event histories exist at every finite round.

For any Borel \(B\subseteq I\), the probability that the first accepted proposal belongs to \(B\), conditional on the current history, is
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(1-q)^{k-1}\mu(B\cap A(H))
=\frac{\mu(B\cap A(H))}{\mu(A(H))}.
\]
The same formula applies to the random current history by the measurable acceptance construction and independence of the fresh proposals from the past. If proposals are instead conditioned on being unqueried, as in the source pseudocode, both the numerator and denominator acquire the same normalization factor; the accepted law is unchanged.

We now prove safe elimination. Let \(i\) be unqueried at a history \(H\). If \(\widehat r(i,H)\in\uparrow M_f(H)\), then \(\widehat r(i,H)\le r(i)\) and upward closure imply \(r(i)\in\uparrow M_f(H)\). If \(f\nleq\widehat p(i,H)\), then \(f\le p(i)\) is impossible, since \(p(i)\le\widehat p(i,H)\). Consequently, for an unqueried implementation,
\[
i\notin A(H)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\neg\bigl(f\le p(i)\ \text{and}\ r(i)\notin\uparrow M_f(H)\bigr). \tag{1}
\]

Fix any execution in \(\Omega_0\). For every \(r_*\in O\) and every \(i_*\in W_{r_*}\), either \(i_*\) is queried at a finite round, or it is never queried. In the latter case, either it is outside \(A(H_t)\) at some finite round, or it belongs to \(A(H_t)\) at every round. These alternatives are mutually exclusive and exhaustive.

If \(i_*\) is queried, its resource belongs to the target-feasible sampled resource set immediately after that query, and the finite-history upper-closure identity gives \(r_*\in U_t\). If \(i_*\) is never queried but is outside \(A(H_t)\) at some round, it is unqueried at that round, so (1) and \(f\le p(i_*)\) imply \(r_*\in U_t\). This proves the trichotomy and its coverage assertions for all optimal witnesses simultaneously on \(\Omega_0\); no union over an uncountable witness family is taken.

Now assume \(O\) is finite and each \(W_r\) is Borel with \(c_r=\mu(W_r)>0\). Let \(J_{t+1}\) be the implementation queried between histories \(H_t\) and \(H_{t+1}\), and let \(\mathcal F_t\) be the sigma-field generated by the accepted implementations through round \(t\). The next round's proposal sequence is independent of \(\mathcal F_t\).

For an optimal resource \(r\in O\), coverage is equivalent to having queried a witness:
\[
r\in U_t\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{some queried implementation belongs to }W_r. \tag{2}
\]
Indeed, a queried witness gives coverage by the finite-history identity. Conversely, coverage gives a target-feasible sampled resource \(s\le r\). Since \(r\) is a minimal element of the entire target-feasible resource set, \(s=r\), so the corresponding queried implementation belongs to \(W_r\). Because \(W_r\) is Borel, (2) makes \(\{r\in U_t\}\) an \(\mathcal F_t\)-measurable event, even though the resource poset has no specified measurable structure.

While \(r\notin U_t\), every implementation in \(W_r\) is unqueried, since querying one would cover \(r\). For such an implementation \(i\), optimism gives \(f\le p(i)\le\widehat p(i,H_t)\). If \(\widehat r(i,H_t)\in U_t\), then \(\widehat r(i,H_t)\le r\) would imply \(r\in U_t\), a contradiction. Thus
\[
W_r\subseteq A(H_t)\quad\text{whenever }r\notin U_t.
\]
The accepted sampling law therefore gives, on the uncovered event,
\[
\Pr(J_{t+1}\in W_r\mid\mathcal F_t)
=\frac{\mu(W_r)}{\mu(A(H_t))}
\ge c_r>0. \tag{3}
\]

We use the following standard conditional Borel-Cantelli lemma. If \((\mathcal G_n)\) is a filtration and \(E_n\in\mathcal G_n\), then, with \(a_n=\Pr(E_n\mid\mathcal G_{n-1})\),
\[
\Pr\left(\left\{\sum_n a_n=\infty\right\}\cap
\{E_n\text{ occurs only finitely often}\}\right)=0.
\]
No independence of the events \(E_n\) is required.

For the fixed optimal resource \(r\), define
\[
E_{t+1}=\{r\notin U_t\}\cap\{J_{t+1}\in W_r\}.
\]
By (2), the uncovered event belongs to \(\mathcal F_t\), and the witness-selection event belongs to \(\mathcal F_{t+1}\). Thus \(E_{t+1}\in\mathcal F_{t+1}\), verifying adaptedness. Equation (3) gives
\[
\Pr(E_{t+1}\mid\mathcal F_t)
\ge c_r\mathbf 1_{\{r\notin U_t\}}.
\]
On the measurable event \(B_r=\bigcap_{t\ge0}\{r\notin U_t\}\), the conditional-probability sum diverges. However, \(E_{t+1}\) can occur at most once: its occurrence queries a witness and covers \(r\), after which monotonicity prevents the uncovered condition from holding again. The conditional Borel-Cantelli lemma therefore implies \(\Pr(B_r)=0\).

Equivalently, this last conclusion can be obtained without the lemma. If \(B_{r,n}=\bigcap_{t=0}^{n}\{r\notin U_t\}\), then (3) and conditional expectation give \(\Pr(B_{r,n+1})\le(1-c_r)\Pr(B_{r,n})\). Hence \(\Pr(B_r)\le\lim_n(1-c_r)^n=0\). This also covers the boundary case \(c_r=1\).

Define \(T_r=\inf\{t\ge0:r\in U_t\}\). Equation (2) makes \(T_r\) a measurable stopping time, and the preceding argument shows \(T_r<\infty\) almost surely. Monotonicity gives \(r\in U_t\) for every \(t\ge T_r\).

The feasibility and upward-generation assumptions imply \(O\ne\varnothing\): a target-feasible resource exists and lies above an element of \(O\). Since \(O\) is finite, \(N_0=\max_{r\in O}T_r\) is a measurable random variable and is almost surely finite. It may be assigned any finite value on the null event where a round fails to terminate or a coverage time is infinite. Outside that null event, every \(r\in O\) belongs to \(U_t\) for all \(t\ge N_0\). Since \(U_t\) is upward closed,
\[
\uparrow O\subseteq U_t\qquad(t\ge N_0).
\]

Conversely, every target-feasible queried resource lies in \(\uparrow O\) by upward generation. Therefore \(S_f(H_t)\subseteq\uparrow O\), and upward closure together with the finite-history identity yields
\[
U_t=\uparrow S_f(H_t)\subseteq\uparrow O
\qquad\text{for every }t.
\]
Combining the two inclusions proves \(U_t=\uparrow O\) for all \(t\ge N_0\), almost surely.
