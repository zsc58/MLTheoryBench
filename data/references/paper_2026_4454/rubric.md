# Scoring Guide

- **0:** No correct idea or relevant valid method.
- **1-2:** Nontrivial relevant ideas, but no complete correct proof step or useful conclusion.
- **3-7:** Some correct core steps or conclusions, but important steps are missing.
- **8-9:** Complete and essentially correct, with only readily repairable errors or omitted cases.
- **10:** Fully correct.

# Core Steps

- **3 points:** Gives a valid VC-dimension-d affine-halfspace construction, specifies its masses and a target compatible with the public boundary convention, proves an initial-mistake event of probability at least 1/8, and identifies the resulting loss contribution 1/n. An appeal to the standard affine-halfspace VC-dimension formula is acceptable.
- **2 points:** Correctly couples subsequent source coins at the middle point to the alpha-biased random walk, proves positive probability of strict-positive survival, and establishes persistence by a round-wise ERM induction. The induction must preserve the outer labels and justify the ERM choice on nonseparable accumulated data, not merely assert that a majority at one point suffices in an arbitrary class.
- **3 points:** Provides the fixed-generator XOR reduction, verifies the XOR-class VC dimension and independent positive/unlabeled sample laws, and applies the assumed PU guarantee and Chernoff concentration with the factor 1-alpha. The update must be implementable: it may branch on an observed positive count, not on unknown population loss. It must handle insufficient positives and zero disagreement probability.
- **2 points:** Gives a valid geometric schedule with correct generation-before-update timing, accounts for full-round batching and initialization, and derives the stated expected-loss bound uniformly for all t>=1. The argument must address large n and early rounds rather than only sufficiently large epochs, and handle d=0.

# Evaluator Notes

Accept a complete valid alternative proof. Do not require the reference proof's incidental constants, its particular initialization, or its exact dyadic schedule. Treat omission of the random-walk persistence argument, the XOR-to-PU distributional justification, or a valid all-round schedule as theorem-critical gaps.

The PU guarantee is an explicit public assumption; a solver is not required to prove the cited specialized learning theorem. Conversely, that assumption does not promise proper outputs, so do not require recovery classifiers to belong to H. Only the lower-bound ERM is required to be proper. An argument using proper PU outputs must justify that extra property rather than silently assume it.

Conditional on obtaining enough accepted examples, a solver must have a valid reason that its supplied positive sample still has the required law. A predetermined independent unlabeled block and the first required number of accepted positives suffice. Using only nonaccepted covariates as though they were i.i.d. from P does not.

The learner knows alpha but not P, h*, or the hidden source indicators. Epoch updates must use batches generated by the classifier being held fixed. Rounding epoch lengths cannot be discarded uniformly when the nominal sample budget is smaller than one batch. A proof restricted to sufficiently large t, with an uncontrolled parameter-dependent transient, does not establish the public all-round guarantee.
