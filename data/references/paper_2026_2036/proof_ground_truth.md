# Reference Proof

## Proof Structure

Use the intercept-inclusive truncation of the true additive quantile as a feasible comparator for the coefficient-ball projection. Expand the unprojected squared error. Conditional on the preceding history, integrate the conditional density between the prediction and the true quantile to control the cross-term. Expand the conditional second moment of the batch average and use orthonormality and Bessel's inequality for its diagonal and off-diagonal parts. Finally, unroll the resulting recursion and evaluate the weighted sum in cumulative sample size.

## Complete Proof

Write \(q=m_\tau\), \(\|f\|_2^2=\int_{[0,1]^p}f(x)^2\,dx\), and let \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\) be the sigma-algebra generated by all batches before time \(t\). The schedules are deterministic, and the current batch is independent of \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\). All function inner products below use Lebesgue measure; coefficient norms are Euclidean unless otherwise specified.

### 1. Orthonormal coordinates, truncation, and projection

The functions \(1\) and \(\psi_{k,j}(x^{(k)})\) form an orthonormal system on \([0,1]^p\). Within a coordinate this follows from univariate orthonormality, between coordinates from centering and product integration, and for the intercept from centering. In particular, the coefficient-to-function map in each retained space is an isometry.

Let \(S_t\) be the span of the coordinates of \(\Psi_t\), and define
\[
\check q_t(x)=\alpha+\sum_{k=1}^p\sum_{j=1}^{J_t}\beta_{k,j}\psi_{k,j}(x^{(k)}),\qquad r_t=\check q_t-q.
\]
The discarded tail is orthogonal to \(S_t\), and
\[
\delta_t:=\|r_t\|_2^2
=\sum_{k=1}^p\sum_{j>J_t}\beta_{k,j}^2
\le pQ^2J_t^{-2s}. \tag{1}
\]
The coefficient assumption makes \(\check q_t\) feasible for the full coefficient \(\ell_1(R)\)-ball.

We use the following standard closed-convex projection fact. If \(v\) minimizes \(\|u-z\|^2\) over a nonempty closed convex set \(C\) in a Hilbert space, then
\[
\langle u-v,z-v\rangle\le0\quad(z\in C),\qquad
\|v-z\|^2\le\|u-z\|^2.
\]
The first inequality follows by differentiating the squared distance along \(v+\varepsilon(z-v)\), \(0\le\varepsilon\le1\), at zero; expanding \(\|u-z\|^2\) proves the second. Here the feasible set is the isometric image of a finite-dimensional closed coefficient ball, so it is nonempty, closed, and convex. It is not a linear subspace.

Put \(\widetilde q_t=\widetilde\theta_t^\top\Psi_t\). Applying this fact with comparator \(\check q_t\), and then adding the orthogonal tail, gives
\[
\|\widehat q_t-q\|_2^2
=\|\widehat q_t-\check q_t\|_2^2+\delta_t
\le\|\widetilde q_t-\check q_t\|_2^2+\delta_t
=\|\widetilde q_t-q\|_2^2. \tag{2}
\]
Zero-padding preserves the previous fitted function. Thus, with \(\widehat q_0=0\),
\[
\widetilde q_t=\widehat q_{t-1}+\gamma_t H_t,
\qquad H_t(x)=G_t^\top\Psi_t(x).
\]
Define \(e_t=\mathbb E\|\widehat q_t-q\|_2^2\). Equation (2) yields
\[
e_t\le e_{t-1}+2\gamma_t\mathbb E\langle\widehat q_{t-1}-q,H_t\rangle
+\gamma_t^2\mathbb E\|G_t\|_{\mathrm{Euclidean}}^2. \tag{3}
\]
The last identification uses the coefficient isometry.

The coefficient bound and common basis bound imply \(|q|\le B\) and \(|\widehat q_t|\le B\), including \(t=0\). Indeed, the true coefficient series is absolutely summable, and its expansion is uniformly absolutely convergent. Consequently all the squared errors considered here are finite, and \(e_t\le4B^2\). No moment assumption on \(Y\) is needed: the score is bounded.

### 2. Conditional-CDF contraction

Fix the preceding history and write
\[
a_t(x)=\widehat q_{t-1}(x),\quad u_t=a_t-q=h_t+r_t,
\quad h_t=\widehat q_{t-1}-\check q_t\in S_t.
\]
The conditional CDF is continuous, and the quantile is finite, so \(F_x(q(x))=\tau\), where \(F_x=F_{Y\mid X=x}\). Absolute continuity gives
\[
F_x(a_t(x))-\tau
=\int_{q(x)}^{a_t(x)}p_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)\,dy
=\lambda_t(x)u_t(x), \tag{4}
\]
where \(\lambda_t\) is the secant slope when \(u_t\ne0\), and we set \(\lambda_t=c_1\) otherwise. Since both endpoints belong to \([-B,B]\),
\[
c_1\le\lambda_t(x)\le c_2.
\]
This argument requires no continuity of the density and no choice of an intermediate point.

Let \(a=c_1C_1>0\) and \(L=c_2C_2\). Conditional expectation of a batch score equals \(-\lambda_t(X)u_t(X)\). Orthonormality gives
\(\langle u_t,\Psi_t(X)^\top\Psi_t(\cdot)\rangle=h_t(X)\).
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[\langle u_t,H_t\rangle\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
&=-\int\lambda_t(x)p_X(x)(h_t(x)+r_t(x))h_t(x)\,dx\\
&\le-a\|h_t\|_2^2+L\|h_t\|_2\|r_t\|_2\\
&\le-\frac a2\|h_t\|_2^2+\frac{L^2}{2a}\delta_t.
\end{aligned} \tag{5}
\]
The last inequality follows from \(Lvw\le av^2/2+L^2w^2/(2a)\). Since \(\|u_t\|_2^2=\|h_t\|_2^2+\delta_t\), taking expectations gives
\[
2\gamma_t\mathbb E\langle u_t,H_t\rangle
\le-a\gamma_t e_{t-1}
+\left(a+\frac{L^2}{a}\right)\gamma_t\delta_t. \tag{6}
\]

### 3. Conditional batch second moment

Let
\[
Z_{t,i}=\bigl(\tau-\mathbf1\{Y_{t,i}\le a_t(X_{t,i})\}\bigr)\Psi_t(X_{t,i}),
\qquad \mu_t=\mathbb E[Z_{t,1}\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}].
\]
Given \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\), these vectors are iid and \(G_t=n_t^{-1}\sum_iZ_{t,i}\). Expanding the squared norm and using conditional independence for distinct indices gives the exact identity
\[
\mathbb E[\|G_t\|^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
=\frac1{n_t}\mathbb E[\|Z_{t,1}\|^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
+\frac{n_t-1}{n_t}\|\mu_t\|^2. \tag{7}
\]
This also holds when \(n_t=1\), with the second term zero.

The absolute score is at most one. There are \(d_t=1+pJ_t\) orthonormal coordinates, so
\[
\mathbb E[\|Z_{t,1}\|^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
\le\int\|\Psi_t(x)\|^2p_X(x)\,dx
\le C_2d_t. \tag{8}
\]
By (4), the coordinates of \(\mu_t\) are the retained coefficients of the function
\[
w_t(x)=-\lambda_t(x)u_t(x)p_X(x).
\]
This function belongs to \(L^2\), since \(|w_t|\le L|u_t|\). For a finite orthonormal system \(\{\phi_\ell\}\), Bessel's inequality states
\(\sum_\ell|\langle w,\phi_\ell\rangle|^2\le\|w\|_2^2\); it follows by expanding the nonnegative squared norm of \(w-\sum_\ell\langle w,\phi_\ell\rangle\phi_\ell\). Its hypotheses hold for the full retained system, including the intercept. Hence
\[
\|\mu_t\|^2\le\|w_t\|_2^2\le L^2\|u_t\|_2^2. \tag{9}
\]
Combining (7)-(9) and taking expectations yields
\[
\mathbb E\|G_t\|^2
\le\frac{C_2(1+pJ_t)}{n_t}+L^2e_{t-1}. \tag{10}
\]
All these conditional integrations and finite sums are justified by bounded scores and finite-dimensional square integrability.

### 4. Recursion

Substituting (1), (6), and (10) into (3) gives
\[
e_t\le(1-a\gamma_t+L^2\gamma_t^2)e_{t-1}
+D_1\gamma_tJ_t^{-2s}
+D_2\frac{J_t}{n_t}\gamma_t^2, \tag{11}
\]
where one may take
\(D_1=(a+L^2/a)pQ^2\) and \(D_2=C_2(p+1)\), because \(J_t\ge1\).

Put \(b=a/2\), and fix \(A\) large enough that \(bA\ge1\). Since \(\gamma_t=A n_t/N_t\to0\), choose a deterministic \(t_0\ge2\) such that, for every \(t\ge t_0\),
\[
L^2\gamma_t\le a/2,\qquad 0\le1-b\gamma_t\le1.
\]
It follows that
\[
e_t\le(1-b\gamma_t)e_{t-1}
+D_1\gamma_tJ_t^{-2s}
+D_2\frac{J_t}{n_t}\gamma_t^2. \tag{12}
\]
All earlier iterates are covered by the finite bound on \(e_{t_0-1}\); no small-step condition is needed at the initial iterations.

### 5. Unrolling and cumulative-sample summation

Let \(\beta=2s/(2s+1)\in(0,1)\). Since \(N_t\ge1\), the prescribed ceiling satisfies
\[
N_t^{1/(2s+1)}\le J_t\le2N_t^{1/(2s+1)}.
\]
Thus the forcing term in (12) is at most
\[
D_A n_tN_t^{-1-\beta} \tag{13}
\]
for a fixed finite \(D_A\). For \(t\ge t_0\), the contraction factor is nonnegative and, using \(bA\ge1\), satisfies
\[
1-b\gamma_t=1-bA\frac{n_t}{N_t}
\le1-\frac{n_t}{N_t}=\frac{N_{t-1}}{N_t}. \tag{14}
\]
Repeated substitution into (12), valid also for zero errors or forcing terms, gives
\[
e_t\le e_{t_0-1}\prod_{k=t_0}^t(1-b\gamma_k)
+\sum_{j=t_0}^tD_A n_jN_j^{-1-\beta}\prod_{k=j+1}^t(1-b\gamma_k).
\]
Using (14) and telescoping,
\[
e_t\le e_{t_0-1}\frac{N_{t_0-1}}{N_t}
+\frac{D_A}{N_t}\sum_{j=t_0}^t n_jN_j^{-\beta}. \tag{15}
\]
For every \(j\), monotonicity of \(v^{-\beta}\) gives
\[
n_jN_j^{-\beta}\le\int_{N_{j-1}}^{N_j}v^{-\beta}\,dv.
\]
Here \(N_{t_0-1}>0\). Consequently
\[
\sum_{j=t_0}^t n_jN_j^{-\beta}
\le\int_{N_{t_0-1}}^{N_t}v^{-\beta}\,dv
\le\frac{N_t^{1-\beta}}{1-\beta}.
\]
Equation (15) is therefore \(O(N_t^{-1})+O(N_t^{-\beta})=O(N_t^{-\beta})\). Since positive integer batch sizes imply \(N_t\to\infty\), this proves
\[
\mathbb E\|\widehat q_t-m_\tau\|_{L^2([0,1]^p)}^2
=O\!\left(N_t^{-2s/(2s+1)}\right).
\]
The threshold for \(A\) can be chosen using \(c_1C_1\); the eventual starting index and the final implicit constant may also depend on the fixed batch schedule. No uniformity over schedules is asserted.
