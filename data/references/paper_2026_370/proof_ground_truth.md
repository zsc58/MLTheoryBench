# Reference Proof

## Proof Structure

Derive the operator/nuclear descent inequality, the polar identities, the Frobenius gradient-gap bound, and conditional mini-batch estimates. Normalize the momentum and separate its signal lag from its martingale-noise component. For the vector update, retain the negative momentum-norm term in descent and absorb the squared lag against it. For the polar update, obtain nuclear-norm descent directly, sum the first-moment lag bound, and give explicit parameter choices. Handle capped tuning and degenerate parameters separately, then deduce iteration bounds and compare rank coefficients.

## Complete Proof

Let \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\) be the sigma-field generated by the batches before iteration \(t\). The initial point and all tuning parameters are deterministic. Write
\[
g_t=\nabla f(X_{t-1}),\qquad \zeta_t=\widehat G_t-g_t,\qquad v=\sigma/\sqrt B.
\]
The uniform oracle assumptions and independent iid batches give
\[
\mathbb E[\zeta_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]=0,
\qquad \mathbb E[\|\zeta_t\|_F^2\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]\le v^2.
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, conditional on the past, the iterate is fixed and the centered sample gradients are independent; expanding the squared norm cancels all cross terms and divides the single-sample variance by \(B\). Errors at different times need not be independent. Their cross inner products have zero expectation by conditional centering.

### Basic inequalities

For an SVD \(A=\sum_i s_i u_i v_i^\top\),
\[
|\langle A,D\rangle_F|
\le\sum_i s_i|u_i^\top Dv_i|
\le\|A\|_*\|D\|_{\rm op}.
\]
Cauchy–Schwarz on the singular values and their nonnegativity give
\[
\|A\|_*\le\sqrt d\,\|A\|_F,
\qquad \|A\|_{\rm op}\le\|A\|_F\le\|A\|_*.
\tag{2}
\]
The compact SVD uses only positive singular values. Consequently
\[
\|\mathcal P(A)\|_{\rm op}\le1,
\qquad \langle A,\mathcal P(A)\rangle_F=\|A\|_*,
\qquad \mathcal P(cA)=\mathcal P(A)\quad(c>0).
\tag{3}
\]
These statements also hold at zero with the stipulated convention.

Integrating the gradient along the segment from \(X\) to \(Y\), applying duality and smoothness to the remainder, yields
\[
f(Y)\le f(X)+\langle\nabla f(X),Y-X\rangle_F
       +\frac L2\|Y-X\|_{\rm op}^2.
\tag{4}
\]
Specifically, the remainder is at most
\(\int_0^1 Ls\|Y-X\|_{\rm op}^2\,ds\).
Applying (4) to \(Y=X-\nabla f(X)/L\), and then using (2), gives
\[
f_*\le f(Y)\le f(X)-\frac{\|\nabla f(X)\|_F^2}{2L}.
\]
Thus
\[
\|\nabla f(X)\|_F^2\le2L(f(X)-f_*),
\qquad \|g_1\|_*\le\sqrt{2dL\Delta}.
\tag{5}
\]

All expectations used below are finite. For the polar method, movement is deterministically bounded over a finite horizon. For the vector method, smoothness implies Frobenius Lipschitz continuity of the gradient, hence at most linear gradient growth; (1) and induction through the linear momentum update give finite second moments of the iterates and momentum. Integrating smoothness also gives at most quadratic absolute growth of the objective, so its expectations and the telescoping operations below are well defined.

### Normalized momentum and noise

For \(q=1-\rho\in(0,1]\), put
\[
N_t=qV_t,\qquad
H_t=\rho H_{t-1}+qg_t,\qquad
Z_t=\rho Z_{t-1}+q\zeta_t,
\]
with all three initial values zero. Then
\[
N_t=H_t+Z_t,\qquad N_t=\rho N_{t-1}+q\widehat G_t.
\]
Unrolling \(Z_t\) and using martingale orthogonality gives
\[
\mathbb E\|Z_t\|_F^2
=q^2\sum_{j=1}^t\rho^{2(t-j)}\mathbb E\|\zeta_j\|_F^2
\le\frac{q}{1+\rho}v^2\le qv^2.
\tag{6}
\]
In particular,
\[
\mathbb E\|Z_t\|_*\le\sqrt{dq}\,v.
\tag{7}
\]
No independence between the signal lag and \(Z_t\) is assumed.

Let \(A_t=H_t-g_t\). Its correctly indexed boundary and recursion are
\[
A_1=-\rho g_1,\qquad
A_t=\rho A_{t-1}+\rho(g_{t-1}-g_t)\quad(t\ge2).
\tag{8}
\]
Although called a signal lag, \(A_t\) is generally random because the trajectory is random.

### Vector-style momentum

First suppose \(\Delta>0\). For the prescribed tuning, \(q\le1\) implies
\[
\alpha=\frac{q^2}{4L},\qquad s:=\frac\alpha q=\frac q{4L},
\qquad X_t-X_{t-1}=-sN_t.
\tag{9}
\]
By (4), norm monotonicity, and the inner-product identity,
\[
f(X_t)\le f(X_{t-1})
-\frac s2\|g_t\|_F^2
-\frac s2(1-Ls)\|N_t\|_F^2
+\frac s2\|N_t-g_t\|_F^2.
\tag{10}
\]
The negative \(N_t\)-term must be retained.

For \(0<q<1\), weighted Young's inequality applied to (8) gives
\[
\|A_t\|_F^2
\le\rho\|A_{t-1}\|_F^2
  +\frac{\rho^2}{q}\|g_{t-1}-g_t\|_F^2.
\]
Smoothness and (9) imply
\[
\|g_{t-1}-g_t\|_F\le Ls\|N_{t-1}\|_F.
\]
Summing the recurrence from \(t=2\) to \(T\), using \(A_1=-\rho g_1\), and discarding a nonpositive terminal term yields
\[
\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb E\|A_t\|_F^2
\le\frac{\rho^2}{q}\|g_1\|_F^2
 +\frac{\rho^2L^2s^2}{q^2}
   \sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb E\|N_t\|_F^2.
\tag{11}
\]
For \(q=1\), \(\rho=0\) and \(A_t=0\), so (11) holds directly without dividing by \(\rho\).

Using \(N_t-g_t=A_t+Z_t\), the elementary inequality \(\|A+Z\|_F^2\le2\|A\|_F^2+2\|Z\|_F^2\), and (6), gives
\[
\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb E\|N_t-g_t\|_F^2
\le\frac{2\rho^2}{q}\|g_1\|_F^2
 +\frac{2\rho^2L^2s^2}{q^2}
   \sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb E\|N_t\|_F^2
 +2Tqv^2.
\tag{12}
\]
Telescoping (10) and using \(f(X_T)\ge f_*\), we obtain
\[
\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb E\|g_t\|_F^2
 +(1-Ls)\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb E\|N_t\|_F^2
\le\frac{2\Delta}{s}
 +\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb E\|N_t-g_t\|_F^2.
\]
Under (9),
\[
1-Ls\ge\frac34,
\qquad \frac{2\rho^2L^2s^2}{q^2}=\frac{\rho^2}{8}\le\frac18.
\]
Absorb the movement-dependent term of (12), discard the remaining nonnegative momentum term, and apply (5). This gives the explicit estimate
\[
\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb E\|g_t\|_F^2
\le\frac{12L\Delta}{qT}+2qv^2.
\tag{13}
\]
This absorption is the local repair of the source proof's tuning-endpoint problem. It uses the negative term already present in the source descent inequality and does not change the prescribed stepsize.

Put \(a=\sqrt{L\Delta/T}\). When \(v>0\), the prescribed momentum is \(q=\min\{a/v,1\}\). If \(q=1\), then \(v\le a\), and (13) is at most \(14a^2\). If \(q<1\), its right side is \(14av\). If \(v=0\), the stipulated \(q=1\) gives at most \(12a^2\). Jensen's inequality over both randomness and iteration index therefore gives
\[
\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb E\|g_t\|_F
\le\sqrt{14}\left[
\sqrt{\frac{L\Delta}{T}}+
\left(\frac{\sigma^2L\Delta}{BT}\right)^{1/4}
\right].
\tag{14}
\]
Multiplying by \(\sqrt d\) using (2) proves
\[
\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb E\|g_t\|_*
\le\sqrt{14}\left[
\sqrt{\frac{dL\Delta}{T}}+
\left(\frac{d^2\sigma^2L\Delta}{BT}\right)^{1/4}
\right].
\tag{15}
\]
If \(\Delta=0\), (5) gives \(g_1=0\). The public zero-gap convention sets \(\alpha=0\), so every iterate equals \(X_0\), irrespective of oracle noise. Both sides of the vector bound are then zero.

### Polar momentum

For \(q>0\), scale invariance gives \(\mathcal P(V_t)=\mathcal P(N_t)\). By (2)–(3) and the triangle inequality,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle g_t,\mathcal P(N_t)\rangle_F
&=\|N_t\|_*+\langle g_t-N_t,\mathcal P(N_t)\rangle_F\\
&\ge\|g_t\|_*-2\|N_t-g_t\|_*.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently (4) gives
\[
f(X_t)\le f(X_{t-1})-\alpha\|g_t\|_*
  +2\alpha\|N_t-g_t\|_*+\frac{L\alpha^2}{2}.
\tag{16}
\]
This is descent in the required nuclear metric, not a Frobenius bound later multiplied by \(\sqrt d\).

The polar movement satisfies \(\|X_{t-1}-X_{t-2}\|_{\rm op}\le\alpha\). Thus (8) yields, pathwise,
\[
\|A_t\|_*\le\rho\|A_{t-1}\|_*+\rho L\alpha
\quad(t\ge2),
\qquad \|A_1\|_*=\rho\|g_1\|_*.
\]
Unrolling,
\[
\|A_t\|_*\le\rho^t\|g_1\|_*+\frac{\rho L\alpha}{q}.
\tag{17}
\]
Together with (7), this gives
\[
\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb E\|N_t-g_t\|_*
\le\frac{\rho\|g_1\|_*}{Tq}
 +\frac{\rho L\alpha}{q}+v\sqrt{dq}.
\tag{18}
\]
The initial contribution vanishes exactly when \(\rho=0\); this fact is important in the capped case.

Summing (16), applying (18) and (5), gives the explicit scalar estimate
\[
\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb E\|g_t\|_*
\le\frac{\Delta}{T\alpha}
 +\frac{L\alpha}{2}
 +\frac{2\rho L\alpha}{q}
 +\frac{2\rho\sqrt{2dL\Delta}}{Tq}
 +2v\sqrt{dq}.
\tag{19}
\]

Suppose first that \(\Delta>0\). Choose
\[
q=\begin{cases}
\min\{\sqrt{L\Delta}/(v\sqrt{dT}),1\},&v>0,\\
1,&v=0,
\end{cases}
\qquad \rho=1-q,
\qquad \alpha=\sqrt{\frac{q\Delta}{LT}}>0.
\tag{20}
\]
Again put \(a=\sqrt{L\Delta/T}\). The first three terms in (19) are at most
\[
\frac{a}{\sqrt q}+\frac{a\sqrt q}{2}
 +\frac{2\rho a}{\sqrt q}
\le\frac{7a}{2\sqrt q}.
\]
If \(q=1\), then \(\rho=0\) and \(v\sqrt d\le a\) when \(v>0\). The boundary term is zero and the last term is at most \(2a\); hence (19) is at most \(11a/2\). This also covers \(v=0\).

If \(q<1\), then \(q=a/(v\sqrt d)\), so
\[
\frac{a}{\sqrt q}=v\sqrt{dq}
=\left(\frac{d\sigma^2L\Delta}{BT}\right)^{1/4},
\]
and
\[
\frac{2\rho\sqrt{2dL\Delta}}{Tq}
=2\sqrt2\,\rho\,\frac{vd}{\sqrt T}
\le2\sqrt2\,\frac{\sigma d}{\sqrt{BT}}.
\]
Thus a universal constant, for example \(6\), gives
\[
\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb E\|g_t\|_*
\le6\left[
\sqrt{\frac{L\Delta}{T}}+
\frac{\sigma d}{\sqrt{BT}}+
\left(\frac{d\sigma^2L\Delta}{BT}\right)^{1/4}
\right].
\tag{21}
\]

It remains to supply positive polar stepsizes when \(\Delta=0\), rather than substituting zero into (20). By (5), \(\nabla f(X_0)=0\). If \(v=0\), choose \(\rho=0\) and any positive \(\alpha\), for instance \(1/L\). Conditional zero variance makes every sampled gradient equal the true gradient almost surely. Induction gives \(V_t=0\), \(X_t=X_0\), and a zero stationarity average.

If \(\Delta=0\) and \(v>0\), choose
\[
\rho=0,\qquad \alpha=\frac{vd}{LT\sqrt T}>0.
\]
Irrespective of the noise, bounded polar movement and smoothness give
\[
\|g_t\|_*\le L\|X_{t-1}-X_0\|_{\rm op}
\le L\alpha(t-1).
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb E\|g_t\|_*
\le\frac{L\alpha(T-1)}2
\le\frac{vd}{2\sqrt T},
\]
which is bounded by the surviving second term in (21). This closes all zero-gap cases without relaxing the requirement \(\alpha>0\) for the polar method.

Taking \(C=6\) proves both public rate bounds.

### Iteration bounds and rank comparison

For each displayed rate, make every nonzero summand at most \(\varepsilon/(3C)\); using three terms also suffices for the two-term vector bound. With a sufficiently large universal constant \(K\), positive integers satisfying
\[
T\ge\max\left\{1,\left\lceil K\left(
\frac{dL\Delta}{\varepsilon^2}
+\frac{d^2\sigma^2L\Delta}{B\varepsilon^4}
\right)\right\rceil\right\}
\]
and, respectively,
\[
T\ge\max\left\{1,\left\lceil K\left(
\frac{L\Delta}{\varepsilon^2}
+\frac{d^2\sigma^2}{B\varepsilon^2}
+\frac{d\sigma^2L\Delta}{B\varepsilon^4}
\right)\right\rceil\right\}
\]
are sufficient. These are the stated big-O bounds with the public integer-rounding convention. When all coefficients vanish, one iteration suffices.

Holding \(L,\Delta,\sigma,B\) fixed, the deterministic rate coefficient changes from \(d^{1/2}\) to \(1\), and the \(T^{-1/4}\) coefficient changes from \(d^{1/2}\) to \(d^{1/4}\). The corresponding iteration coefficients change from \(d\) to \(1\), and from \(d^2\) to \(d\). The polar bound additionally contains \(d\sigma/\sqrt{BT}\), corresponding to \(d^2\sigma^2/(B\varepsilon^2)\). Accordingly these bounds do not assert that the polar upper bound is smaller for every horizon and parameter choice, nor do upper bounds alone prove a lower bound or actual speedup for the vector method. They establish precisely the improved deterministic and leading small-tolerance stochastic rank dependence under the common nuclear stationarity metric.

## Obligation Closure

The norm facts, descent lemma, gradient-gap bound, and mini-batch estimates are derived above. The adaptive-noise cross terms are closed by conditional centering. The vector mismatch, absorption at the prescribed stepsize, capped tuning, and norm conversion are explicit. The polar mismatch, initial boundary term, admissible parameter choices, capped tuning, and all degenerate cases are explicit. Both iteration deductions and the qualified rank comparison are explicit. No external result or finite-Newton–Schulz residual theorem is required, and no private proof obligation remains unresolved.
