# Reference Proof

## Proof Structure

The recipient-specific blocks form a mutually independent array. A remote quantized model received by recipient j is a measurable function of its designated off-diagonal block, independent of j's complete diagonal PU block. Permuting diagonal calibration and true-null test observations leaves the local PU input and local learned function unchanged. Joint invariance with the alternative scores and received models gives the requested conditional score exchangeability. A finite-rank argument then proves null p-value validity and local PRDS directly. Each local p-value vector depends only on its recipient's column of blocks, so different local vectors are independent. The increasing-cross-section argument proves global PRDS. The standard BH-under-PRDS theorem and the definition of FastLSU yield global FDR control.

## Complete Proof

### 1. Independent recipient-specific inputs and measurable remote models

For each source a, its blocks B^(a->1),...,B^(a->K) are mutually independent. The complete local collections are independent across source agents. Consequently the entire array (B^(a->j))_(a,j) is mutually independent.

Fix recipient j. For a!=j, H^(a->j) is constructed exclusively from B^(a->j), and the received parameter is theta_hat^(a->j)=q(A(H^(a->j))). Measurability of q composed with A makes this a measurable random parameter. Since f is measurable, F^(a->j)(z)=f(z;theta_hat^(a->j)) is a measurable random evaluation for each z. The family of received models, interpreted through the sigma-field generated by their evaluations, is a measurable function of the off-diagonal blocks in column j. That family is independent of the complete diagonal block B^(j->j), including the data used to learn its local score. This establishes the remote-independence and measurability obligation.

### 2. Conditional exchangeability of the relevant null scores

Write U for the vector of calibration observations and true-null test observations in B^(j->j), T for its first k_j labeled observations, and V for its alternative test observations. Let pi be any permutation of the coordinates of U, leaving T and V fixed.

The source-level conditional exchangeability assumption implies invariance of the joint law of (T,U,V) under this permutation. Indeed, extend pi to the full raw null sequence of Agent j by fixing every other coordinate. The conditional law given all of that agent's alternatives is invariant; integrating out the other blocks and other alternative observations gives the claimed diagonal-block equality.

The input H^(j->j) consists of T as an ordered labeled portion and all remaining observations as an unordered unlabeled portion. Thus the permitted permutation leaves H^(j->j) unchanged. The local learned function g^(j)(.;H^(j->j)) is also unchanged by the assumed permutation invariance. This is the reason that this local input can be included in a joint distributional equality. One may not instead condition on an arbitrary ordered collection of calibration and test observations.

Adjoin the received models to the equality in law. Their independence from the diagonal block preserves the equality, and the permutation leaves those models unchanged. Applying the common deterministic score evaluation and pointwise maximum therefore permutes exactly the coordinates of the null-score vector, while leaving both the alternative-score vector and the received models unchanged.

Let Q be the vector of composite scores on calibration and true-null test observations, A_alt the vector of alternative composite scores, and F_j the collection of received models. We have established

(Q,A_alt,F_j) has the same law as (Q_pi,A_alt,F_j)

for every permitted pi. Equivalently, for every bounded measurable h,

E[h(Q) | A_alt,F_j] = E[h(Q_pi) | A_alt,F_j] almost surely.

This is the requested conditional exchangeability. Taking conditional expectations again also gives exchangeability conditional on A_alt alone. No exchangeability of scores at the fixed labeled training positions is needed or asserted.

### 3. Direct derivation of local p-value validity and PRDS

We prove the implication rather than merely invoking the specialized inherited conformal theorem.

Fix a true-null test coordinate i in recipient j's block. Put L=ell_j+1. The L scores consisting of the calibration scores and the score of test i are distinct almost surely. List their decreasing order statistics as

t_1 > t_2 > ... > t_L.

Let W consist of this sorted list, all other test scores, and the received models. In particular, W includes all alternative scores. The joint permutation invariance established above remains valid for permutations of these L null coordinates that fix the other null test coordinates. Conditional on W, the position R of test i in this sorted list is therefore uniform on {1,...,L}. To see this explicitly, the L events specifying the focal coordinate's position are carried into one another by these permutations, while W is unchanged. Their conditional probabilities are equal and sum to one.

Thus R is independent of W and

p_i^(j)=R/L.

For 0<=u<=1,

P(p_i^(j)<=u)=floor(Lu)/L<=u.

This closes the null-validity obligation.

For another test h!=i, let s_h denote its score, which is included in W. Its calibration set contains all t_b except t_R, so

p_h^(j) = [1 + sum_(b=1)^L 1{t_b>=s_h} - 1{t_R>=s_h}]/L.

As r increases, t_r decreases, so 1{t_r>=s_h} is nonincreasing. Hence p_h^(j) is nondecreasing in r. The focal p-value r/L is also increasing in r. The complete local p-value vector can therefore be written P^(j)=Phi(R,W), where Phi(r,W) is coordinatewise nondecreasing in r for every W outside a null set.

For any increasing measurable set D and r in {1,...,L}, independence of R and W gives

P(P^(j) in D | p_i^(j)=r/L) = E[1{Phi(r,W) in D}].

The right-hand side is nondecreasing in r. This proves PRDS on every true-null coordinate, including dependence with alternative p-values.

If ell_j=0, every local p-value equals 1, and validity and PRDS follow directly; the same rank argument has L=1. If I_0^(j) is empty, PRDS on that subset is vacuous. An empty local test vector causes no difficulty.

### 4. Independence of the local p-value vectors

For each j, the local p-value vector is a measurable function only of the recipient-specific column

(B^(1->j),...,B^(K->j)).

Its local learned score uses B^(j->j); each remote model uses the unique block B^(a->j); and calibration and test evaluation use B^(j->j). Different recipients' columns have no blocks in common. Because the full block array is mutually independent, these columns, and hence the local p-value vectors, are mutually independent. Merely knowing that each remote model is independent of its recipient's evaluation data would not suffice for this step.

### 5. Global PRDS

For two independent PRDS vectors X and Y, take an increasing measurable set D in their product space. For each y, its section D_y={x:(x,y) in D} is increasing. If i is a true-null coordinate of X, independence gives

P((X,Y) in D | X_i=x) = integral P(X in D_y | X_i=x) dP_Y(y).

For every y, the integrand is nondecreasing in x by PRDS of X. The integral is therefore nondecreasing. The symmetric argument applies to true-null coordinates of Y. Induction establishes PRDS of the concatenated vector P on the tagged global true-null set I_0. Null validity for the concatenated vector follows from Step 3.

### 6. BH/FastLSU and the global FDR bound

The standard independent background result used here is the following: for a finite p-value vector with values in [0,1], marginal null validity P(p_i<=u)<=u for all u in [0,1], and PRDS on the true-null subset, the Benjamini-Hochberg step-up procedure at any fixed alpha in (0,1) satisfies E[V/(R vee 1)]<=alpha.

For M>0, let p_(1)<=...<=p_(M) and

r_star=max({0} union {r in {1,...,M}:p_(r)<=alpha r/M}).

BH rejects exactly the p-values at most alpha r_star/M. The preceding steps verify every hypothesis of the stated standard result. By definition, FastLSU computes this same pooled BH rejection set through local counts N(t)=sum_j #{i:p_i^(j)<=t}. Thus V=sum_j V^(j) and R=sum_j R^(j), and

E[(sum_j V^(j))/((sum_j R^(j)) vee 1)]<=alpha.

If M=0, FastLSU rejects nothing and the FDR is zero. If I_0 is empty, V is identically zero. These conventions also retain the stated denominator when there are no rejections.

## Obligation Audit

All theorem-critical obligations are closed: measurable remote-model independence; joint local permutation invariance with alternative scores and received models; conditional null-score exchangeability; null p-value validity; local PRDS by direct rank coupling; independence through disjoint recipient columns; global PRDS by increasing sections; and the BH/FastLSU FDR conclusion. The only invoked external theorem is the explicitly stated standard BH-under-PRDS result. The specialized inherited conformal result is proved to the extent needed here, not accepted as a completed citation. No unresolved GT obligation remains.
