# Reference Proof

## Theorem-critical obligations

1. Establish that the true weighted empirical law is measurable with respect to the unordered multiset and is the conditional law of the final pair.
2. Prove known-weight membership by inserting the realized response.
3. Transfer each of the three finite-law calibration properties. In particular, justify isotonic transfer using sigma-lattice projection inequalities rather than an ordinary tower property.
4. Prove robust event inclusion under the inherited candidate-response independence assumption, including zero coordinates and empty feasible boxes.
5. Derive the finite-sample and asymptotic confidence conclusions.

All five obligations are closed below. The imported exchangeable predictive-system theorem is not used as a black box. The finite-law calibration properties are explicit assumptions, not conclusions delegated to a citation.

## Standard background and its applicability

Regular conditional distributions exist here because the pair space and the space of finite unordered multisets are standard Borel. The final pair belongs to the realized multiset almost surely. Conditional distributions may therefore be represented by probabilities on its finitely many distinct atoms. This does not require the joint-density assumption used for the source's optional explicit permutation formula.

For a sigma-lattice A, let C_A be the square-integrable real random variables H such that {H>t} belongs to A modulo null sets for every real t. This is a nonempty closed convex subset of L2: constants belong to it; upper level sets of positive linear combinations are obtained by countable unions and intersections of upper level sets; and an L2-convergent sequence has an almost-surely convergent subsequence whose limit is lattice-measurable. The Hilbert-space projection theorem states that every nonempty closed convex subset of a Hilbert space has a unique nearest point. Thus the least-squares definition of E[T|A] is well-defined for the bounded indicators used here. Its particular variational consequences will be derived explicitly, not assumed as a specialized calibration theorem.

## 1. Conditional weighted law

Write E for the unordered multiset and

M = sum_{i=1}^{n+1} w_i(Z_1,...,Z_{n+1}) delta_{Z_i}.

The weights sum to one. Their permutation equivariance shows that M depends only on E: permuting an enumeration permutes the coefficients together with their atoms and leaves the measure unchanged. Hence M is E-measurable.

For almost every realized multiset e, take a distinct-atom enumeration z_1,...,z_{n+1}. The definition of the true weights gives, for every bounded measurable h,

E[h(Z_{n+1}) | E=e] = sum_i w_i(z_1,...,z_{n+1}) h(z_i) = integral h dM(e).

Consequently L(Z_{n+1}|E)=M. All later conditional arguments apply the finite-law assumptions to this fixed law M(e), not to an ordering-dependent random law inside the conditional experiment.

At the realized candidate response, tilde_mu^{Y_{n+1}}=M. Therefore F*=G[M,X_{n+1}]. Null-multiset extensions and the choices made for repeated candidate tuples do not change this almost-sure identity.

## 2. Known-weight band membership

For every real threshold y, the candidate y'=Y_{n+1} is included in the infimum and supremum defining L*(y) and U*(y). Thus

L*(y) <= G[P_{tilde_mu^{Y_{n+1}}},X_{n+1}](y) = F*(y) <= U*(y).

This is a pointwise statement about entire functions for each realized sample on the full-probability set where the weight definitions hold. It establishes F* in Pi*[X_{n+1}] almost surely without an uncountable union of exceptional events.

## 3. Probabilistic calibration

Fix u in (0,1). Conditional on E=e, the pair (X_{n+1},Y_{n+1}) has the finite-support law M(e), and its forecast is G[M(e),X_{n+1}]. Applying in-sample probabilistic calibration gives

P(F*(Y_{n+1})<=u | E) <= u,

P(F*(Y_{n+1}-)<u | E) >= u.

Ordinary expectation of these inequalities yields the claimed unconditional inequalities.

## 4. Auto-calibration

Fix y in R and put T=1{Y_{n+1}>y}, Q=1-F*(y). In-sample auto-calibration under the conditional finite law gives

E[T | E,F*] = Q.

Here conditioning is on the ordinary sigma-field generated by E and the complete random CDF F*. Since Q is sigma(F*)-measurable, the ordinary tower property gives

E[T | sigma(F*)] = Q.

This proves the auto-calibration conclusion.

## 5. Isotonic calibration

Fix y in R and again put T=1{Y_{n+1}>y}, Q=1-F*(y), and A=A(F*). Because survival probabilities increase under the stochastic order, Q is A-measurable and bounded.

Fix any global event A0 in A. By the definition of A, it is the inverse image under F* of a Borel upper set U of CDFs, modulo a null set. Under E=e, its section is the event {G[M(e),X] in U}; this belongs to the sigma-lattice generated by the finite-law forecast G[M(e),X].

Under that finite law, in-sample isotonic calibration says that Q is the least-squares projection of T onto the corresponding lattice-measurable functions. For an admissible perturbation H, expansion along Q+t(H-Q), with t decreasing to zero through positive values, gives

E[(T-Q)(H-Q) | E=e] <= 0.

The choices H=Q+1 and H=Q-1 are both admissible, so

E[T-Q | E=e] = 0.

The choice H=Q+1_{A0} is admissible, giving

E[(T-Q)1_{A0} | E=e] <= 0.

The choices H=0 and H=2Q are admissible and give

E[(T-Q)Q | E=e] = 0.

These statements hold for almost every e for each fixed test event. Averaging yields

E[T-Q]=0,

E[(T-Q)1_{A0}]<=0 for every A0 in A,

E[(T-Q)Q]=0.

Every bounded A-measurable real H can be approximated by simple functions consisting of a constant plus a finite sum of nonnegative multiples of indicators of upper level sets of H. The first two relations consequently imply E[(T-Q)H]<=0. Truncation and Cauchy-Schwarz extend this inequality to every H in C_A. Combining it with E[(T-Q)Q]=0 gives

E[(T-Q)(H-Q)]<=0 for every H in C_A.

Finally,

E[(T-H)^2]-E[(T-Q)^2]
= E[(H-Q)^2]-2E[(T-Q)(H-Q)]
>= E[(H-Q)^2] >= 0.

Thus Q is the unique least-squares minimizer over C_A. By the public definition of sigma-lattice conditional expectation,

E[1{Y_{n+1}>y} | A(F*)] = 1-F*(y).

This proves isotonic calibration. Neither equality on every event in sigma(F*) nor an ordinary tower property for lattice conditioning was used.

## 6. Robust membership and confidence

For this part the source's inherited assumption makes w*(y') independent of y'; denote the vector by w*. Define A_alpha={w* in W_alpha}. The true vector has total mass one, so exclusion of zero-total-mass vectors does not change this coverage event.

For every candidate response, tilde_mu^{y'}=mu_{w*}^{y'}. Hence L_{w*}=L* and U_{w*}=U*. On A_alpha, w* is an admissible vector in the outer optimization, and therefore

L^{WU,alpha} <= L_{w*}=L* <= F* <= U*=U_{w*} <= U^{WU,alpha}

at every real threshold. Equivalently, one can use just the admissible pair (w*,Y_{n+1}) to obtain the required containment directly. Thus

A_alpha is a subset of {F* in Pi^{WU,alpha}}.

A feasible vector with zero insertion coordinate causes no difficulty: its positive total mass still defines the augmented probability law. If W_alpha is empty, the robust band is empty under the stated convention, and A_alpha is also empty. Thus the event inclusion remains valid in every case. No independence assumption about estimated weights or box bounds is needed.

For each n,

P(F* in Pi^{WU,alpha}) >= P(A_alpha).

Finite-sample coverage gives the bound 1-alpha. Under asymptotic coverage, taking liminf of this inequality gives

liminf_{n->infinity} P(F* in Pi^{WU,alpha}) >= liminf_{n->infinity} P(A_alpha) >= 1-alpha.
