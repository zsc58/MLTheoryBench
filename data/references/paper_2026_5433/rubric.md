# Scoring Guide

- **0:** No correct idea or relevant valid method.
- **1-2:** Nontrivial relevant ideas, but no complete correct proof step or useful conclusion.
- **3-7:** Some correct core steps or conclusions, but important steps are missing.
- **8-9:** Complete and essentially correct, with only readily repairable errors or omitted cases.
- **10:** Fully correct.

# Core Steps

1. **Randomized joint construction and causal setup (2 points):** Correctly uses the supplied product experiment on the full population space, with independent treatment assignment of law p_T and the attribute kernel K. Distinguishes this experiment from the observational law and identifies the two APO quantities. A product-integral formula is acceptable but not mandatory. The experiment itself is a supplied definition, not an additional conclusion the solver must establish from coordinate-family independence.
2. **Conditional-independence collapse (4 points):** Derives that the randomized conditional outcome law or mean given both T and T' depends only on T, and that its mean is g(T). Uses the product assignment mechanism, consistency, and the attribute's dependence only on T. An equivalent direct integral argument earns full credit; introducing the auxiliary symbol g(t,t') is not required. Does not assume the desired collapse or identify a finite-sample estimator with a population mean.
3. **Projection identity and definition domain (2 points):** Obtains the conditional average of g(T), proves it is finite on the asserted portion of D, and gives the correct p_{T'}-almost-everywhere conclusion. Handles local conditional integrability without silently assuming global integrability. Nonnegative-part arguments, integrable localization, or equivalent correct conditional-law reasoning are acceptable. Recognizes that deterministic attributes are included and that positive-mass atoms cannot be exceptional null points.
4. **Monte Carlo projection (2 points):** Identifies the ratio as the empirical conditional average with fitted values substituted for g. In the oracle case, verifies integrability of the indicator-weighted numerator, uses i.i.d. sampling and positive attribute mass, and proves convergence of the ratio while handling possible finite-sample zero counts. Either convergence in probability or almost-sure convergence receives full credit. Does not claim consistency for arbitrary fitted predictors without additional justified hypotheses.

# Evaluator Notes

Accept a complete valid alternative proof. Do not require the reference proof's auxiliary kernels, positive-part notation, or incidental presentation choices. Do not require a derivation of standard probability theorems, but require their applicable hypotheses to be verified.

The primary mathematical step is the randomized conditional-independence collapse; the public definition of g' alone is not a proof of the projection identity. Conversely, a valid integral derivation need not explicitly name a tower-property step.

The public experiment uses the full original population probability space and fresh assignment coordinates. It is not specified merely by independence from the σ-field generated by fixed-index potential outcomes. A proof may invoke the supplied product law directly. No additional separability or measurability with respect to the coordinate-generated σ-field is required. A counterexample that instead sets the randomized treatment equal to a population coordinate must also satisfy the specified product experiment; coordinate-family independence alone does not do so.

Do not demand a pointwise statement at all continuous attribute values. Regular conditional laws are determined only almost everywhere; mere existence of versions at a null point does not force the desired pointwise identity there. At every positive-mass atom in D the identity does hold.

Do not accept the assertion that positive attribute mass guarantees a positive denominator for every finite n. Eventual positivity, or a suitable zero-count extension with a vanishing zero-count probability, is sufficient. The exact formula for that probability is optional.

The setup does not assume E*[|Y|]<∞ globally. A proof using global signed conditional expectations must justify the relevant integrability or localize its argument. The defined finite-mean domain D supplies enough local integrability for the claimed result.

No convergence theorem for a general learned ĝ is requested. Do not impose a training-independence or consistency assumption absent from the task, and do not infer such independence merely because a predictor is fitted or revealed before its evaluations. Distinguish Monte Carlo approximation of a fitted function from accuracy of that fitted function for g.

The source's corrected auxiliary joint-treatment estimator is private audit context, not an additional solver obligation. No credit depends on reproducing that estimator, its correction, or any source-specific notation. All proof obligations for the public task are closed in the reference proof; there is no unresolved private dependency.
