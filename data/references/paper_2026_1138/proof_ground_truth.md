# Reference Proof

## Proof structure and obligations

1. Identify the one-batch covariance by conditional independence and total covariance.
2. Obtain the martingale-difference and conditional-covariance identities for the actual fresh-batch filtration.
3. Localize with a stopping time, prove the fluid limit, and obtain a tight fluctuation-scale bound without requiring moments of the initial fluctuation.
4. Bound the accumulated Taylor and ODE discretization errors.
5. Verify the martingale FCLT hypotheses and joint convergence with the initial fluctuation.
6. Control interpolation, pass through the linear integral equation, and establish uniqueness of the limiting law.

All six obligations are closed below. Batch size b and horizon T are fixed throughout; constants may depend on these and the local compact neighborhood, but not on eta or the iteration index.

## 1. One-batch covariance

Fix theta in U. The fourth-gradient-moment assumption implies all second moments used here are finite. Conditional on mu, the b coordinates of a batch are independent with common gradient mean m_mu(theta) and covariance G_mu(theta). Consequently

    E[g_B(theta) | mu] = m_mu(theta),
    Cov(g_B(theta) | mu) = G_mu(theta)/b.

Total covariance therefore gives

    Cov(g_B(theta)) = E[G_mu(theta)]/b + Cov(m_mu(theta)).

The invariant conditional mean is h(theta), so the last term is zero. Since Pi_theta is deterministic at fixed theta,

    Cov(Pi_theta g_B(theta)) = Pi_theta G(theta) Pi_theta^T / b
                            = G*(theta)/b.

This argument uses the supplied conditional-i.i.d. representation, so no representation theorem for an unspecified class of measurable spaces is needed. The first-b-coordinates formulation also covers b=1. A data-independent simple random subset of distinct coordinates has the same conditional law, which is the sampling formulation used in the source.

## 2. The iteration filtration

Let F_k^eta be the sigma-field generated by theta_0^eta and batches 1 through k. The iterate theta_k^eta is F_k^eta-measurable. The next batch is independent of this entire sigma-field and has the specified fixed batch law. Applying the preceding mean and covariance calculation at the measurable parameter theta_k^eta gives

    E[xi_{k+1}^eta | F_k^eta] = 0,
    E[xi_{k+1}^eta (xi_{k+1}^eta)^T | F_k^eta]
        = G*(theta_k^eta)/b.

The projector is a measurable function of H(theta); continuity of H suffices for measurability of its range projector, without requiring constant rank or continuity of Pi itself. These conditional identities use independent batch copies. The unconditional one-batch calculation alone would not justify them for batches sharing a random directing measure across iterations.

## 3. Stopping, the fluid limit, and the fluctuation-scale bound

Put n_eta=floor(T/eta) and let tau_eta be the first index k with theta_k^eta outside K. Define

    chi_k = 1{k < tau_eta},
    N_m^eta = eta sum_{k=0}^{m-1} chi_k xi_{k+1}^eta,
    M_m^eta = -sqrt(b/eta) N_m^eta.

These are stopped martingales, including when m=n_eta+1. Since chi_k is F_k^eta-measurable, the assumed conditional fourth-moment bound gives

    E[||chi_k xi_{k+1}^eta||^2 | F_k^eta] <= C_K^(1/2).

Thus Doob's L2 inequality and orthogonality of martingale increments yield

    E max_{m<=n_eta+1} ||N_m^eta||^2 <= C eta,
    E max_{m<=n_eta+1} ||M_m^eta||^2 <= C.

This stopping argument does not condition on the future event that all iterates stay in K. That event need not preserve martingale differences. Write E_eta={tau_eta>n_eta}; localization gives P(E_eta)->1.

For k<=n_eta set t_k=k eta, e_k=theta_k^eta-bar theta(t_k), and V_k=sqrt(b/eta)e_k. For k<n_eta, the ODE local truncation error

    rho_{k+1}=bar theta(t_{k+1})-bar theta(t_k)+eta h(bar theta(t_k))

satisfies ||rho_{k+1}||<=C eta^2. Local C1 regularity on a neighborhood of K gives a finite Lipschitz bound for h between points of K. Subtracting the ODE increment from the recursion, summing, and applying discrete Gronwall on E_eta gives

    max_{k<=n_eta} ||e_k||
      <= exp(CT) (||e_0|| + max_{m<=n_eta}||N_m^eta|| + C eta).

The right side tends to zero in probability. Since P(E_eta^c)->0, the grid-point fluid limit follows without conditioning on E_eta.

Multiplying the same inequality by sqrt(b/eta) gives

    max_{k<=n_eta} ||V_k||
      <= exp(CT) (||U_0^eta|| + max_m||M_m^eta|| + C sqrt(b eta))

on E_eta. Weak convergence of U_0^eta implies its tightness. Consequently max_k||V_k||=O_P(1). No uniform second or fourth moment of U_0^eta is assumed or needed.

## 4. Linearization and accumulated remainders

Let A(t)=D h(bar theta(t)). Uniform fluid convergence and K compactly contained in U ensure that, with probability tending to one, the segments joining theta_k^eta and bar theta(t_k) lie in a fixed compact neighborhood in U. Local Lipschitz continuity of D h then gives

    h(theta_k^eta)-h(bar theta(t_k)) = A(t_k)e_k + q_k,
    ||q_k|| <= C ||e_k||^2.

The rescaled grid recursion is

    V_{k+1}=V_k-eta A(t_k)V_k-sqrt(eta b)xi_{k+1}^eta
             -sqrt(eta b)q_k-sqrt(b/eta)rho_{k+1}.

Uniformly over partial sums through n_eta, the absolute accumulated Taylor remainder is at most

    C T sqrt(eta/b) max_{k<=n_eta}||V_k||^2 = o_P(1).

The accumulated ODE truncation remainder is bounded by C T sqrt(b eta)=o(1). These estimates explicitly use the fluctuation-scale bound from the preceding step, not merely the unscaled fluid limit.

Define the step process V^eta(t)=V_floor(t/eta) and the stopped step martingale M^eta(t)=M_floor(t/eta)^eta. Replacing completed-grid sums by integrals introduces an incomplete-cell error bounded by C eta max_k||V_k||. Since A is uniformly continuous, replacing A(eta floor(s/eta)) by A(s) adds another o_P(1) term. On E_eta the stopped and original increments agree. Hence, on the whole probability space,

    V^eta(t)=U_0^eta-integral_0^t A(s)V^eta(s)ds+M^eta(t)+r^eta(t),
    sup_{t<=T}||r^eta(t)|| -> 0 in probability.

Here and below, identities established on events of probability tending to one have residuals defined by the displayed difference off those events; this suffices for convergence in probability and does not require moment bounds off the localization event.

## 5. Martingale FCLT, including the initial value

The predictable covariance of M^eta is

    <M^eta>(t)=eta sum_{k<floor(t/eta)} chi_k G*(theta_k^eta).

On E_eta, chi_k=1 for all relevant k. Continuity of G* on K, the fluid limit, and deterministic Riemann-sum convergence imply, uniformly in t in [0,T],

    <M^eta>(t) -> V(t):=integral_0^t G*(bar theta(s))ds

in probability. V is deterministic, continuous, starts at zero, and has positive-semidefinite increments. For every epsilon>0, conditional Lindeberg is verified by

    sum_{k<n_eta} E[||Delta M_{k+1}^eta||^2
                    1{||Delta M_{k+1}^eta||>epsilon} | F_k^eta]
      <= epsilon^(-2)(eta b)^2 (n_eta+1) C_K -> 0.

The same fourth-moment estimate shows that the sum of unconditional fourth moments of the increments is O(eta), so their maximum tends to zero in probability. Total conditional second moments are uniformly bounded.

We use the standard vector martingale functional CLT: for square-integrable martingale arrays starting at zero, convergence of predictable covariance uniformly in probability to a deterministic continuous covariance function, together with conditional Lindeberg and the associated tightness/jump bounds, gives convergence in D with the J1 topology to the continuous centered Gaussian martingale with that covariance function and independent increments. All its hypotheses have just been verified. Therefore M^eta converges to a process with the law of

    M(t)=integral_0^t C*(bar theta(s))dW_s,

where C* is the symmetric positive-semidefinite square root. Continuity of G* makes this deterministic integrand continuous and bounded along the ODE path.

Joint convergence with U_0^eta, with independence in the limit, also follows here. To see this explicitly, let f be any bounded nonnegative continuous function with E f(U_0)>0. Change probability by the F_0^eta-measurable density f(U_0^eta)/E f(U_0^eta). Under the changed probability the stopped increments remain martingale differences with the same conditional moments. Convergence-in-probability assertions remain valid because these densities are uniformly bounded for small eta. The same FCLT therefore has the same limiting law for M^eta under this change of probability. Testing against bounded continuous functions of M^eta proves factorization of the limiting mixed expectations. By tightness and decomposition of bounded continuous functions into nonnegative parts, this identifies

    (U_0^eta,M^eta) => (U_0,M)

with M independent of U_0. We may represent M using a standard d-dimensional Brownian motion W independent of U_0.

## 6. Interpolation and the limiting equation

The solution map for

    v(t)=u+m(t)-integral_0^t A(s)v(s)ds

is unique and uniformly continuous on bounded time intervals with respect to the uniform norms of u and m: subtract two equations and apply Gronwall. The same estimate makes an additive uniform o_P(1) residual negligible. J1 convergence to a continuous path implies uniform convergence in a convergent-path representation. The joint convergence and residual equation therefore give

    V^eta => U,
    U(t)=U_0-integral_0^t A(s)U(s)ds+integral_0^t C*(bar theta(s))dW_s.

Existence follows from this linear integral equation, and Gronwall gives pathwise uniqueness for a fixed initial value and driving path. Independence of W and U_0 specifies the unique solution law.

It remains to check the interpolation used in the task, including the possible final partial grid interval. On E_eta, for every k<=n_eta, h(theta_k^eta) is bounded and

    sqrt(b/eta)||theta_{k+1}^eta-theta_k^eta||
       <= sqrt(b eta)(C+||xi_{k+1}^eta||).

For any epsilon>0, the union bound and the stopped fourth-moment estimate give

    P(max_{k<=n_eta} sqrt(b eta)||chi_k xi_{k+1}^eta||>epsilon)
       <= epsilon^(-4)(b eta)^2(n_eta+1)C_K -> 0.

Thus the piecewise-linear and step iterate interpolations differ by o_P(sqrt(eta/b)) uniformly, including the final partial interval. Also

    sup_{t<=T} sqrt(b/eta)
       ||bar theta(t)-bar theta(eta floor(t/eta))|| <= C sqrt(b eta)->0.

Consequently the public fluctuation U^eta is uniformly o_P(1) away from V^eta. It has the same limit in D. Since U^eta has continuous paths and the limit is continuous, convergence also holds in C with the uniform topology. The grid fluid limit and the same interpolation estimates establish uniform convergence in probability of hat theta^eta to bar theta. This proves both requested limits and the mini-batch covariance identity.
