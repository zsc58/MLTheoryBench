# Reference Proof

## Obligations and proof structure

The proof must close: parameter and degenerate cases; adaptive self-normalized estimator confidence; the effective-dimension determinant estimate; posterior confidence and Gaussian optimism; saturation and the conditional one-step regret inequality; an adapted confidence-truncated super-martingale with a valid concentration bound; cumulative design-norm summation; and the final probability allocation. Every obligation is derived below. The specialized prediction-confidence and determinant results are not taken as unexplained citations.

All parameters and the graph are fixed. Let F_{t-1} be the sigma-algebra generated by observations and algorithm randomization through round t-1. The current posterior draw is fresh conditional on this history. The chosen arm is known before the reward noise is generated, and the noise satisfies the stated conditional moment-generating-function bound in that pre-reward filtration. Thus the selected feature is predictable for self-normalized concentration. Histories after each reward give an adapted filtration for the regret process.

Write
A=diag(eta_1,...,eta_N)+lambda I_N,
h=log(1+T/lambda),
k=log(1/delta),
l=R sqrt(2d(h+2 log T+k))+C,
z=sqrt(4 log(TN)).
Then v=R sqrt(6d(h+k))+C and g=vz+l. Put s_{i,t}=||b_i||_{B_t^{-1}} and K=11g/p.

## 1. Elementary bounds and deterministic cases

The rows of Q are orthonormal, so ||b_i||_2=1 and ||b_i-b_j||_2=sqrt(2) for distinct arms. Since A>=lambda I_N,
||mu||_2<=C/sqrt(lambda),
0<=Delta_i<=sqrt(2)C/sqrt(lambda),
and also Delta_i<=2 by the bounded means. Every B_t>=lambda I_N, so s_{i,t}<=1/sqrt(lambda).

If C=0, positive definiteness of A implies mu=0 and every regret is zero. This includes the zero-variance posterior case R=C=0. The requested inequality holds immediately.

Assume C>0. The first term of the requested bound, denoted H_1, satisfies, using g>=C, d>=1 and log(1+x)>=x/(1+x),
H_1=(11g/p)sqrt(4(1+lambda)dT h/lambda)
   >=(22C/p)(T/sqrt(lambda))sqrt(d(1+lambda)/(lambda+T)).
If lambda>=T, the final square-root factor is at least 1/sqrt(2). Thus H_1>=sqrt(2)CT/sqrt(lambda)>=R(T).
If 1<=T<=4, then (1+lambda)/(lambda+T)>=1/T, and
H_1>=(22C/p)sqrt(T/lambda)>=sqrt(2)CT/sqrt(lambda)>=R(T).
These cases therefore satisfy the full target deterministically.

It remains to prove the result for C>0, T>=5 and 0<lambda<T. In particular h>log 2 and v>0. No restriction on the public theorem's regularization regime has been introduced.

## 2. Determinant identity and effective dimension

For each rank-one update, the determinant identity gives
 det(B_{t+1})/det(B_t)=1+b_{a(t)}^T B_t^{-1}b_{a(t)}=1+s_{a(t),t}^2.
Consequently
 log(det(B_t)/det A)=sum_{s<t}log(1+s_{a(s),s}^2).
In particular, the telescope for all T pulls ends at B_{T+1}.

For any t<=T+1, let S_t=sum_{s<t}b_{a(s)}b_{a(s)}^T. This matrix is positive semidefinite and tr(S_t)=t-1<=T. In the coordinates in which A is diagonal, Hadamard's determinant inequality gives
 log(det(A+S_t)/det A)
 <=sum_{j=1}^N log(1+(S_t)_{jj}/(eta_j+lambda)).
Each diagonal entry of S_t is nonnegative and at most T, so the first d summands total at most d h.

If d<N, maximality in the definition of d gives
 d eta_{d+1}>T/h.
Using log(1+x)<=x and eta_j>=eta_{d+1} for j>d, the remaining summands total at most
 sum_{j>d}(S_t)_{jj}/eta_{d+1}<=T/eta_{d+1}<d h.
If d=N there are no remaining summands. Hence in all cases
 log(det(B_t)/det A)<=2d h,  t<=T+1.
This explicitly closes the effective-dimension dependency.

## 3. Time-uniform estimator confidence

The standard self-normalized inequality used here is the following. For a fixed positive definite initial matrix A, predictable features, and conditionally mean-zero R-sub-Gaussian noise, if
 xi_t=sum_{s<t}epsilon_s b_{a(s)},
then, for alpha in (0,1), with probability at least 1-alpha simultaneously for all t,
 ||xi_t||_{B_t^{-1}}^2
 <=2R^2 log(det(B_t)^{1/2}/(alpha det(A)^{1/2})).
Its hypotheses hold by the sequential feedback model and A>0. If R=0, the noise is zero almost surely and the same conclusion holds directly.

The estimator update gives
 B_t hat_mu(t)=(B_t-A)mu+xi_t,
so
 hat_mu(t)-mu=B_t^{-1}(xi_t-A mu).
For any vector b, Cauchy-Schwarz therefore gives
 |b^T(hat_mu(t)-mu)|
 <=||b||_{B_t^{-1}}(||xi_t||_{B_t^{-1}}+||A mu||_{B_t^{-1}}).
Because B_t>=A, inversion reverses the positive-definite order and
 A B_t^{-1}A<=A.
Thus ||A mu||_{B_t^{-1}}<=||mu||_A<=C. This derives the prediction-confidence result rather than citing its specialized version.

Apply the self-normalized inequality once with alpha=delta/2. The determinant estimate gives, simultaneously for all relevant t and all arms,
 |b_i^T(hat_mu(t)-mu)|<=beta s_{i,t},
where
 beta=R sqrt(2d h+2k+2 log 2)+C.
Let E_t be this all-arm beta-confidence event and let E be their intersection over t=1,...,T. Then P(E)>=1-delta/2. Each E_t is F_{t-1}-measurable.

The radius comparisons required below are explicit. Since T>=5 and d>=1,
2d h+2k+2 log 2<=2d h+2d k+4d log T,
so beta<=l. Also, since h>log 2,
6d(h+k)-(2d h+2k+2 log 2)
=4d h+(6d-2)k-2 log 2>0,
so beta<=v. The source's unrestricted assertion l<=v is neither assumed nor used.

## 4. Posterior confidence and Gaussian optimism

For a standard Gaussian Z, integration by parts gives the Mills bounds
 phi(x)x/(x^2+1)<=P(Z>x)<=phi(x)/x,  x>0,
where phi(x)=exp(-x^2/2)/sqrt(2pi). For x>=1, these imply
 (1/(2sqrt(pi)x))exp(-x^2/2)
 <=P(|Z|>x)
 <=(sqrt(2/pi)/x)exp(-x^2/2).
The upper coefficient differs from the incorrect source coefficient.

Conditional on F_{t-1}, each
 b_i^T(tilde_mu(t)-hat_mu(t))/(v s_{i,t})
is standard Gaussian. Here v>0 and s_{i,t}>0. Let P_t be the event that all posterior deviations have absolute value at most vz s_{i,t}. A union bound gives
 q_t:=P(P_t^c|F_{t-1})
 <=N(sqrt(2/pi)/z)exp(-z^2/2)
 =sqrt(2/pi)/(T^2 N z)
 <=1/(2T^2).
Indeed N>=2 and TN>=2 imply N z>=2sqrt(4 log 2)>2sqrt(2/pi). Dependence among projected Gaussian variables does not affect the union bound.

On E_t, the standardized optimal-arm threshold is
 u_t=b_{a*}^T(mu-hat_mu(t))/(v s_{a*,t})<=beta/v<=1.
Therefore, by monotonicity, symmetry, and the Gaussian lower bound at x=1,
 P(b_{a*}^T tilde_mu(t)>b_{a*}^T mu|F_{t-1})
 =P(Z>u_t)
 >=P(Z>1)
 >=exp(-1/2)/(4sqrt(pi))
 >=1/(4e sqrt(pi))=p.
This calculation covers negative and zero thresholds without dividing by the threshold or applying a tail formula outside its domain.

## 5. Saturation and one-step expected regret

Define the saturated set
 C_t={i:Delta_i>g s_{i,t}}.
The optimal arm is unsaturated, so the unsaturated set is nonempty. On E_t intersect P_t, beta<=l and g=vz+l imply
 |b_i^T(tilde_mu(t)-mu)|<=g s_{i,t}
for every arm. Every saturated arm j then satisfies
 b_j^T tilde_mu(t)<=b_j^T mu+g s_{j,t}<b_{a*}^T mu.
Thus optimal-arm optimism together with P_t forces the chosen maximizer to be unsaturated. On an E_t history,
 P(a(t) notin C_t|F_{t-1})>=p-q_t.
The subtracted event is posterior-confidence failure, not estimator-confidence failure.

Let bar a_t be a minimum-norm unsaturated arm, selected by a fixed tie rule. It is fixed by the conditioned history. Writing m_t=E[s_{a(t),t}|F_{t-1}], the preceding probability gives
 m_t>=s_{bar a_t,t}(p-q_t).
On E_t intersect P_t, comparison with the sampled maximizer gives
 Delta_{a(t)}
 <=Delta_{bar a_t}+g s_{bar a_t,t}+g s_{a(t),t}
 <=2g s_{bar a_t,t}+g s_{a(t),t}.
On P_t^c use Delta_{a(t)}<=2. Consequently, on an E_t history,
 E[Delta_{a(t)}|F_{t-1}]
 <=2g s_{bar a_t,t}+g m_t+2q_t
 <=(2g/(p-q_t)+g)m_t+1/T^2.
For T>=5,
 p-q_t>=p-1/T^2>=p/5,
because T^2>=25>5e sqrt(pi). Since p<1,
 2g/(p-q_t)+g<=10g/p+g<=11g/p.
Therefore
 E[Delta_{a(t)}|F_{t-1}]<=K E[s_{a(t),t}|F_{t-1}]+1/T^2
on E_t. This closes the one-step obligation with the target's original constants.

## 6. Truncation and concentration without a lower bound on g

Set
 Delta'_t=Delta_{a(t)}1_{E_t},
 X_t=Delta'_t-K s_{a(t),t}-1/T^2,
 Y_t=sum_{s<=t}X_s.
These variables are adapted and integrable. Since E_t is determined by F_{t-1}, the one-step inequality proves E[X_t|F_{t-1}]<=0 when E_t holds. When it fails, truncated regret is zero and the same conclusion is immediate. Thus Y_t is a super-martingale.

A false absolute-increment estimate is unnecessary. Put
 M=K/sqrt(lambda),
 D=sqrt(2)C/sqrt(lambda).
Since g>=C and 11/p>sqrt(2), D<=M. Each increment lies in the deterministic interval
 [-M-1/T^2, D-1/T^2],
whose length is at most 2M.

The conditional-range form of Azuma-Hoeffding states that adapted increments with conditional mean at most zero and conditional interval length at most 2M satisfy
 P(sum_t X_t>=M sqrt(2T log(1/alpha)))<=alpha.
It follows from the conditional Hoeffding estimate
 E[exp(theta X_t)|F_{t-1}]<=exp(theta^2 M^2/2), theta>=0,
and multiplication of these conditional estimates. The nonpositive conditional means and bounded interval lengths verified above are precisely its hypotheses; no assumption that g/p>=1 is needed.

With alpha=delta/2, therefore, with probability at least 1-delta/2,
 sum_t Delta'_t
 <=K sum_t s_{a(t),t}+1/T
   +(11g/(p sqrt(lambda)))sqrt(2T log(2/delta)).
The last term is no greater than
 (g/p)(11/sqrt(lambda)+2)sqrt(2T log(2/delta)).

## 7. Cumulative norms and final assembly

For u=s_{a(t),t}^2, we have 0<=u<=1/lambda. The elementary inequality x<=2log(1+x) for x in [0,1] gives:
- if lambda>=1, u<=2log(1+u);
- if 0<lambda<1, lambda u<=2log(1+lambda u), hence u<=(2/lambda)log(1+u).
Thus, for all relevant norms and all positive lambda,
 s_{a(t),t}^2<=(2+2/lambda)log(1+s_{a(t),t}^2).
This inequality is only asserted on its verified domain, not for arbitrary real x.

Cauchy-Schwarz, the determinant telescope through the last pull, and the determinant estimate give
 sum_{t=1}^T s_{a(t),t}
 <=sqrt(T sum_{t=1}^T s_{a(t),t}^2)
 <=sqrt(T(2+2/lambda)log(det(B_{T+1})/det A))
 <=sqrt(((4+4lambda)/lambda)dT log((lambda+T)/lambda)).
On E, actual and truncated regret agree for every round. Its failure probability is at most delta/2, as is the concentration-event failure probability. A union bound therefore proves, with probability at least 1-delta,
 R(T)<= (11g/p)sqrt(((4+4lambda)/lambda)dT log((lambda+T)/lambda))
        +1/T
        +(g/p)(11/sqrt(lambda)+2)sqrt(2T log(2/delta)).
Together with the deterministic cases, this proves the complete public parameter regime. Substituting g and the fixed constant p gives leading dependence d sqrt(T log N), up to the other logarithmic factors and problem-dependent constants.
