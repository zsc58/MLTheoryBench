# Reference Proof

## Obligation ledger

The following obligations are closed explicitly below: (i) well-definedness and the TD matrix-moment inequalities; (ii) the stochastic bias/variance reduction, including multiplicative noise; (iii) the exact finite power sum and its normalization; (iv) the convergent resolvent representation and all three integration regions; (v) substitution, including k=1 and gamma=0; and (vi) an admissible fixed sharpness instance and the factor-11 comparison. The contextual classical Kreiss theorem is not used to discharge the specialized power-sum obligation.

## 1. Reduction and matrix inequalities

Put \(t=1-\gamma>0\), \(z_j=\theta_j-\theta^*\), and
\[
 H=\Sigma-\gamma C,\quad S=(H+H^\top)/2,\quad
 h_j=\phi(X_j)(\phi(X_j)-\gamma\phi(X'_j))^\top,
\]
\[
 \xi_j=\phi(X_j)\{R_j+\gamma\phi(X'_j)^\top\theta^*-\phi(X_j)^\top\theta^*\}.
\]
Then \(\mathbb Eh_j=H\), \(\mathbb E\xi_j=0\), and
\[
 z_j=(I-\alpha h_j)z_{j-1}+\alpha\xi_j.
\]
All these variables have the required finite second moments. The reward assumption gives a finite right-hand side in the equation for \(\theta^*\). Linear independence makes \(\Sigma\) positive definite, and the inequalities below make \(H\) invertible. Bounded features and the residual assumption then give square-integrable iterates by induction. Also
\[
 \|V_{\bar\theta_k}-V_{\theta^*}\|_\pi^2
 =\|\bar\theta_k-\theta^*\|_\Sigma^2,
 \qquad \|v\|_\Sigma^2=v^\top\Sigma v.
\]

Write \(U=\Sigma^{-1/2}C\Sigma^{-1/2}\). Its contraction property implies
\[
 t\Sigma\preceq S\preceq(1+\gamma)\Sigma,
 \qquad \Sigma+(C+C^\top)/2\succeq0.
\]
In particular \(S\succeq t\omega I\), so \(H\) is invertible. The feature bound and stationarity of both state marginals imply
\[
 \mathbb E[h_jh_j^\top]
 =\mathbb E[\|\phi(X)-\gamma\phi(X')\|^2\phi(X)\phi(X)^\top]
 \preceq(1+\gamma)^2\Sigma,
\]
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E[h_j^\top h_j]
 &\preceq\mathbb E[(\phi(X)-\gamma\phi(X'))(\phi(X)-\gamma\phi(X'))^\top]\
 &=(1+\gamma^2)\Sigma-\gamma(C+C^\top)\
 &=(1+\gamma)S-\gamma t\{\Sigma+(C+C^\top)/2\}
 \preceq(1+\gamma)S.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Conditioning on \(X\) gives \(\mathbb E[\xi_j\xi_j^\top]\preceq\sigma^2\Sigma\). Thus we may use
\[
 c_\Sigma=t^{-1},\qquad \beta=(1+\gamma)^2,\qquad \beta_1=1+\gamma,
 \qquad D=2-\alpha\beta c_\Sigma>0,\quad D_1=2-\alpha\beta_1>0.
\]
These are the source's LSA constants. At \(\gamma=0\), \(a_0=a_1=2\); strict inequality between these two thresholds is not needed.

## 2. Averaged stochastic recursion

Split \(z_j=w_j+v_j\), where
\[
 w_0=z_0,\quad w_j=(I-\alpha h_j)w_{j-1},
 \qquad v_0=0,\quad v_j=(I-\alpha h_j)v_{j-1}+\alpha\xi_j.
\]
Independence of the current sample from the past yields the Euclidean energy descent
\[
 \mathbb E\|w_j\|^2
 \leq\mathbb E\|w_{j-1}\|^2-\alpha D_1\mathbb E\|w_{j-1}\|_S^2.
\]
Summing and using convexity of the squared norm gives
\[
 \mathbb E\|\bar w_k\|_\Sigma^2
 \leq\frac{c_\Sigma\|z_0\|^2}{\alpha D_1 k}.
\]

For the noise recursion, \(\mathbb Ev_j=0\). Define
\[
 m=\frac{\alpha\sigma^2c_\Sigma}{D}.
\]
We claim \(M_j:=\mathbb E[v_jv_j^\top]\preceq mI\). It holds at zero. The cross terms in its next-step expansion vanish because the current sample is independent of \(v_{j-1}\) and \(\mathbb Ev_{j-1}=0\), even though \(h_j\) and \(\xi_j\) may be correlated. Consequently,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 M_j
 &\preceq m\mathbb E[(I-\alpha h_j)(I-\alpha h_j)^\top]+\alpha^2\sigma^2\Sigma\
 &\preceq m(I-\alpha D S)+\alpha^2\sigma^2c_\Sigma S=mI.
 \end{aligned}
\]
This proves the claim by induction.

To average without assuming independent additive noise, set \(T=I-\alpha H\) and
\[
 \eta_j=\xi_j-(h_j-H)v_{j-1},\qquad v_j=Tv_{j-1}+\alpha\eta_j.
\]
Relative to the filtration generated by the first \(j\) samples, \((\eta_j)\) is a martingale-difference sequence. Its unconditional covariance obeys
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E[\eta_j\eta_j^\top]
 &\preceq\sigma^2\Sigma+m\mathbb E[(h_j-H)(h_j-H)^\top]\
 &\preceq(\sigma^2+m\beta)\Sigma
 =\frac{2\sigma^2}{D}\Sigma.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Here the cross terms again vanish using \(\mathbb Ev_{j-1}=0\); no independence between \(h_j\) and \(\xi_j\) is asserted. This combined innovation is the sum of the centered source terms in the source's orthogonal-order expansion. Expanding by the number of distinct centered sample factors gives that expansion directly: products from different orders have an independent time index occurring once, so their expectations vanish. The combined martingale representation suffices for the calculation below.

Unrolling and interchanging finite sums gives
\[
 \bar v_k=\frac1k\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(I-T^{k-i})H^{-1}\eta_i.
\]
Cross terms with distinct time indices vanish by the martingale-difference property and deterministic propagation matrices. Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E\|\bar v_k\|_\Sigma^2
 \leq\frac{2\sigma^2}{Dk}\mathcal S_k,
\]
where, explicitly,
\[
 \mathcal S_k=\frac1k\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}
 \|\Sigma^{1/2}(I-T^j)H^{-1}\Sigma^{1/2}\|_F^2.
\]
In particular \(\mathcal S_1=0\). Combining the bias and noise by Cauchy--Schwarz in \(L^2\) yields
\[
 \mathbb E\|\bar z_k\|_\Sigma^2
 \leq\left(
 \sqrt{\frac{\|z_0\|^2}{2\alpha t(1-\alpha/a_1)k}}
 +\sqrt{\frac{\sigma^2\mathcal S_k}{(1-\alpha/a_0)k}}
 \right)^2. \tag{A}
\]

For completeness, the additional inequality in the source's LSA proposition is also justified. For general \(\Sigma\preceq c_\Sigma S\), the symmetric part of \(\Sigma^{-1/2}H\Sigma^{-1/2}\) is at least \(c_\Sigma^{-1}I\). Cauchy--Schwarz applied to its quadratic form gives
\(\|\Sigma^{1/2}H^{-1}\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{\rm op}\leq c_\Sigma\).
Moreover \(H^\top H\preceq\mathbb E[h_j^\top h_j]\preceq\beta_1S\), so \(T^\top T\preceq I-\alpha D_1S\preceq I\). With \(Q=\Sigma^{1/2}T\Sigma^{-1/2}\), this implies \(|\operatorname{tr}Q^j|=|\operatorname{tr}T^j|\leq d\). It follows that
\[
 \mathcal S_k\leq c_\Sigma^2\left(3d+\frac1k\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\|Q^j\|_F^2\right).
\]
The source's related inverse inequality uses \((H^{-1}+H^{-\top})/2\preceq S^{-1}\), not equality. It follows directly by writing \(H=S^{1/2}(I+K)S^{1/2}\), \(K^\top=-K\): the normalized symmetric part of the inverse is \((I-K^2)^{-1}\preceq I\).

## 3. Exact resolvent representation

For TD, put \(A=I-\gamma U\). Then
\[
 H=\Sigma^{1/2}A\Sigma^{1/2},\qquad
 Q=I-\alpha\Sigma A=\Sigma^{1/2}T\Sigma^{-1/2},\qquad
 \mathcal S_k=\frac1k\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\|(I-Q^j)A^{-1}\|_F^2.
\]
We have \(\|A^{-1}\|_{\rm op}\leq t^{-1}\). Also \(\Sigma\preceq I\), since \(\operatorname{tr}\Sigma=\mathbb E\|\phi(X)\|^2\leq1\). The strict contraction bound \(T^\top T\preceq I-\alpha D_1t\omega I\) proves that \(Q\) has spectral radius below one. Similarity to \(T\) also gives a geometric power bound sufficient to justify all ensuing series and integrals; the similarity constant is used only for convergence, not in the final estimates.

For \(k\geq2\), set \(c=\sqrt{1-1/k}\) and \(\widetilde Q=cQ\). On \(|z|=1\), define
\[
 F(z)=\{(z-c)^{-1}I-(zI-cQ)^{-1}\}A^{-1}.
\]
The uniformly convergent expansion is
\[
 F(z)=\sum_{j\geq0}c^jz^{-j-1}(I-Q^j)A^{-1}.
\]
Entrywise integration of the squared modulus uses
\(\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{i(m-n)\psi}\,d\psi=2\pi\mathbf1_{\{m=n\}}\), and gives
\[
 \frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\|F(e^{i\psi})\|_F^2\,d\psi
 =\sum_{j\geq0}c^{2j}\|(I-Q^j)A^{-1}\|_F^2.
\]
Thus, since \(c^{-2(k-1)}\leq e\),
\[
 \mathcal S_k\leq\frac{e}{2\pi k}
 \int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\|F(e^{i\psi})\|_F^2\,d\psi. \tag{B}
\]
The square on the Frobenius norm and the diagonal-index restriction in the integration identity are essential.

Set \(\psi_\gamma=\arccos(1-t^2)\). We bound the three pieces of the integral including the factor \(e/(2\pi)\), denoting them by \(I_1,I_2,I_3\).

## 4. First arc

We first establish the auxiliary bound used here. If \(D_0\succeq0\), \(|\zeta|=1\), and \(\operatorname{Re}\zeta\geq-t/\sqrt2\), then
\[
 \|(I-(I+\zeta D_0A)^{-1})A^{-1}\|_{\rm op}
 \leq\frac2{1-\gamma^2}. \tag{C}
\]
To prove it without assuming \(D_0\) invertible, consider \(x=(I+\zeta AD_0)u\) and \(r=D_0u\). If \(r\ne0\), then
\[
 a=\frac{r^*Ar}{\|r\|^2}\in\overline B(1,\gamma),\qquad
 q=\frac{r^*u}{\|r\|^2}=\frac{u^*D_0u}{\|r\|^2}\geq0.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \frac{|r^*x|}{\|r\|^2}=|a+\bar\zeta q|
 \geq\operatorname{dist}(\overline B(1,\gamma),-\bar\zeta\mathbb R_+)
 \geq\frac{1-\gamma^2}{2}.
\]
For nonnegative real part the distance is \(1-\gamma\). Otherwise it is \(\sqrt{1-(\operatorname{Re}\zeta)^2}-\gamma\), at least \(1-t^2/2-\gamma=(1-\gamma^2)/2\). Cauchy--Schwarz proves \(\|r\|\leq2\|x\|/(1-\gamma^2)\). The same calculation proves injectivity of \(I+\zeta AD_0\): for \(x=0\) it forces \(r=0\), and then \(u=0\). The other inverse exists by similarity through \(A\). Finally,
\[
 (I-(I+\zeta D_0A)^{-1})A^{-1}
 =\zeta D_0(I+\zeta AD_0)^{-1},
\]
which proves (C), including the case \(r=0\).

On \(|\psi|\leq\psi_\gamma\), let \(z=e^{i\psi}\),
\[
 \zeta=\frac{\bar z-c}{|z-c|},\qquad D_0=\frac{c\alpha}{|z-c|}\Sigma.
\]
Then \(zI-cQ=(z-c)(I+\zeta D_0A)\). Furthermore
\[
 -\operatorname{Re}\zeta
 \leq\sqrt{\frac{1-\operatorname{Re}z}{2}}\leq\frac t{\sqrt2},
\]
with \(z=1\) checked directly. The first inequality follows from monotonicity of \(u/\sqrt{u^2+b^2}\) for fixed nonzero \(b\), with the endpoints handled directly. Therefore
\[
 \|F(z)\|_F^2\leq\frac{4d}{(1-\gamma^2)^2|z-c|^2}.
\]
Expanding \((z-c)^{-1}\) into its geometric series and integrating gives \(\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|e^{i\psi}-c|^{-2}d\psi=2\pi/(1-c^2)\). Hence
\[
 I_1\leq\frac{4dek}{(1-\gamma^2)^2}
 \leq\frac{dek}{t^2}(4-3\gamma).
\]
The last inequality is \(4/(1+\gamma)^2\leq4-3\gamma\), whose difference after multiplication by \((1+\gamma)^2\) is \(\gamma(1-\gamma)(5+3\gamma)\geq0\).

## 5. Middle arc

Here \(|\sin\psi|\geq t\). The source's estimate in this region is needed only for real right-hand sides. Let \(s=\sin\psi\), \(G=(\cos\psi-c)I+c\alpha\Sigma A\), take \(x\in\mathbb R^d\), and write
\(y=(G+isI)^{-1}x=y_1+iy_2\), with real \(y_1,y_2\). The imaginary part gives \(Gy_2=-sy_1\). Expanding the squared norm and using \(\|\Sigma U-U^\top\Sigma\|_{\rm op}\leq2\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|x\|^2
 &=\|Gy_1\|^2+s^2(2\|y_1\|^2+\|y_2\|^2)
 -2c\alpha\gamma s\,y_1^\top(\Sigma U-U^\top\Sigma)y_2\
 &\geq s^2\left(2\|y_1\|^2+\|y_2\|^2
 -\frac{8\gamma}{(1+\gamma)^2}\|y_1\|\|y_2\|\right)\
 &\geq\frac{s^2}{3}\|y\|^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
We used \(\alpha<2t/(1+\gamma)^2\), \(|s|\geq t\), \(4\gamma\leq(1+\gamma)^2\), and \(2ab\leq(3/2)a^2+(2/3)b^2\). Thus the resolvent maps a real input \(x\) to a vector of norm at most \(\sqrt3\|x\|/|s|\). Each column of \(A^{-1}\) is real, so applying this bound columnwise, rather than asserting an unrestricted complex operator bound, yields
\[
 \|F(z)\|_F^2
 \leq\frac{(1+\sqrt3)^2}{s^2}\|A^{-1}\|_F^2
 \leq\frac{8d}{t^2\sin^2\psi}.
\]
For \(\gamma>0\), integration gives
\[
 I_2\leq\frac{16de}{\pi t^2}\cot\psi_\gamma
 =\frac{16de}{\pi t^3}\frac{\gamma(2-\gamma)}{\sqrt{1+2\gamma-\gamma^2}}
 \leq\frac{4de(1+t)}{t^3}.
\]
Here \(\gamma/\sqrt{1+2\gamma-\gamma^2}\leq1/\sqrt2\) and \(16/(\sqrt2\pi)<4\). For \(\gamma=0\), the middle arc is empty and its contribution is zero.

## 6. Last arc: repaired integration

Let \(\Delta=2-(1+\gamma)\alpha>0\) and \(R_z=(zI-cQ)^{-1}\). On the last arc, \(\operatorname{Re}z\leq0\), and
\[
 \|F(z)\|_F^2\leq\frac{2d}{t^2}
 \left(|z-c|^{-2}+\|R_z\|_{\rm op}^2\right). \tag{D}
\]
One must integrate the squared resolvent; replacing it by the source's unsquared pointwise bound is invalid.

The normalized integral of \(|z-c|^{-2}\) over any subset of the left semicircle is at most \(1/2\), because \(|z-c|\geq1\). To bound the other term, first suppose \(\alpha\leq1\). The eigenvalues of \(c(I-\alpha\Sigma)\) are nonnegative, so
\[
 \|(zI-cQ)y\|\geq(1-c\alpha\gamma)\|y\|\geq\frac34\|y\|.
\]
Indeed \(\alpha\gamma<2\gamma(1-\gamma)/(1+\gamma)^2\leq1/4\), the last inequality being equivalent to \((1-3\gamma)^2\geq0\). Hence the normalized integral of \(\|R_z\|_{\rm op}^2\) over the left semicircle is at most \(8/9\).

Now suppose \(\alpha>1\). Put
\[
 r=c(\alpha-1)\in(0,1),\quad b=1-r,\quad v=c\alpha\gamma,\quad
 \delta=b-v=1-c+c\Delta.
\]
The step-size restriction implies \(\alpha(1+3\gamma)\leq2\), and hence \(\alpha\gamma\leq\Delta/2\). Thus \(v\leq\delta/2\), \(b\leq3\delta/2\), and \(\delta\geq\Delta\), since \(\Delta<1\) in this case.

Parameterize the left semicircle by \(z=-\cos\theta+i\sin\theta\), \(|\theta|\leq\pi/2\). The spectrum of the real symmetric matrix \(c(I-\alpha\Sigma)\) lies in \([-r,c]\). Its distance from \(z\), denoted \(m(z)\), satisfies
\[
 m(z)^2\geq b^2+r\sin^2\theta.
\]
To verify this, if \(\cos\theta\geq r\), the squared distance to the interval is \(b^2+2r(1-\cos\theta)\), which is at least the displayed expression. If \(\cos\theta<r\), the distance is \(|\sin\theta|\); subtracting the displayed expression gives \((1-r)(r-\cos^2\theta)\geq0\).

The remaining matrix perturbation has norm at most \(v\). Since \(m(z)\geq b\),
\[
 \|R_z\|_{\rm op}^2\leq\frac1{(m(z)-v)^2}
 \leq\frac{b^2}{\delta^2}\frac1{b^2+r\sin^2\theta}.
\]
The substitution \(u=\tan\theta\) gives
\[
 \int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{d\theta}{b^2+r\sin^2\theta}
 =\frac{\pi}{2b\sqrt{b^2+r}}.
\]
Because \(b^2+r=b^2-b+1\geq3/4\), we conclude
\[
 \frac1{2\pi}\int_{\operatorname{Re}z\leq0}\|R_z\|_{\rm op}^2\,d\psi
 \leq\frac{b}{2\delta^2\sqrt{b^2+r}}
 \leq\frac{\sqrt3}{2\delta}\leq\frac1\Delta.
\]
Both cases, combined with (D), imply the required unchanged budget
\[
 I_3\leq\frac{2de}{t^2}\left(3+\frac1\Delta\right).
\]
This is a local repair of the same third-arc spectral-distance calculation, not an added assumption or a different power-sum mechanism.

## 7. Upper-bound conclusion

Combining the three arcs in (B),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal S_k
 &\leq\frac{de}{t^2}(1+3t)
 +\frac{4de(1+t)}{kt^3}
 +\frac{2de}{kt^2}\left(3+\frac1\Delta\right)\
 &=\frac{de}{t^2}
 \left(1+3t+\frac4{kt}+\frac{10}{k}+\frac2{k\Delta}\right)
 =\frac{de}{t^2}(1+\varepsilon_{k,\gamma}).
 \end{aligned}
\]
For \(k=1\), the same inequality follows from \(\mathcal S_1=0\), without introducing \(c\). Substitution into (A) gives exactly (1). All denominators are positive under the stated strict step-size restriction. The argument allows \(\sigma=0\), including the zero-error case, and covers \(\gamma=0\). No \(\omega\)-dependent factor remains in the bound.

## 8. Sharpness construction and exact scalar moments

Fix \(d\), \(0<\omega\leq1/d\), \(\sigma>0\), and deterministic \(\theta_0\). Take the uniform distribution on \(\{1,\ldots,d\}\), the constant transition \(X'=X\), features \(\phi(i)=\sqrt{d\omega}\,e_i\), and independent rewards \(R\sim N(0,\sigma^2)\). Draw independent triples at each iteration. This same law and feature map work for every \(\gamma\) and every step-size fraction. Their conditional reward second moment is \(\sigma^2\), their features have norm at most one, and
\[
 \Sigma=C=\omega I,\quad H=t\omega I,\quad\theta^*=0.
\]
The residual second moment equals, rather than merely being bounded by, \(\sigma^2\). The loss is \(\omega\mathbb E\|\bar\theta_k\|^2\).

For each coordinate, put \(B_j=\mathbf1_{\{X_j=i\}}\), \(p=1/d\), and
\[
 \ell=d\alpha t\omega,\quad \tau^2=\alpha^2d\omega\sigma^2,\quad
 r_0=1-p\ell,\quad s_0=1-p(2\ell-\ell^2).
\]
The coordinate recursion is
\[
 Y_j=(1-\ell B_j)Y_{j-1}+B_jZ_j,
 \qquad Z_j=\alpha\sqrt{d\omega}R_j,
\]
where \(B_j\) is independent of \(Z_j\) and \(\mathbb EZ_j^2=\tau^2\). Split \(Y\) into its initial-condition component \(Y^b\) and zero-initialized reward component \(Y^v\). Conditional on the entire sequence of state indicators, the averaged bias is deterministic and the averaged reward component is centered. Their cross term therefore has expectation zero.

For the bias,
\[
 \mathbb EY_j^b=r_0^jY_0,\qquad
 \mathbb E[(\bar Y_k^b)^2]\geq
 Y_0^2\left(\frac{1-r_0^k}{kp\ell}\right)^2.
\]
This Jensen lower bound is sufficient; equality or asymptotic equality of the bias second moment is not needed.

For the noise, write \(\ell'=2\ell-\ell^2\). Along the required limit, eventually \(0<\ell<1\). Direct recursion and conditioning give
\[
 \mathbb E[(Y_i^v)^2]=\frac{\tau^2(1-s_0^i)}{\ell'},\qquad
 \mathbb E[Y_i^vY_j^v]=r_0^{j-i}\frac{\tau^2(1-s_0^i)}{\ell'}\quad(i\leq j).
\]
Consequently the exact averaged second moment is
\[
 \mathbb E[(\bar Y_k^v)^2]
 =\frac{\tau^2}{\ell'k^2}
 \sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(1-s_0^i)
 \left\{\frac{2(1-r_0^{k-i})}{p\ell}-1\right\}. \tag{E}
\]
Keeping this finite sum avoids the missing geometric-series endpoint factor in the source's subsequent closed-form display.

Fix \(\lambda\in(0,1/2]\) and take \(\alpha=\lambda a_0\). Then \(\ell\to0\) and \(C_k=kp\ell=k\alpha t\omega\to C_*=5.5\). Uniformly for \(0\leq i\leq k\), the powers in (E) converge to \(e^{-C_*i/k}\) and \(e^{-2C_*i/k}\), respectively. Dividing (E) by \(\tau^2/(p\ell^2k)\) gives
\[
 \frac{2\ell}{\ell'}\frac1k\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(1-s_0^i)(1-r_0^{k-i})
 -\frac{p\ell^2}{\ell'}\frac1k\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(1-s_0^i).
\]
The second term tends to zero. The first tends by a Riemann sum to
\[
 f(C_*)=\int_0^1(1-e^{-2C_*u})(1-e^{-C_*(1-u)})\,du
 =1-\frac{3-4e^{-C_*}+e^{-2C_*}}{2C_*}>0.
\]
The varying value of \(\tau^2\) causes no difficulty because it cancels in this normalized identity.

Let
\[
 B_k=\frac{\|\theta_0\|^2}{\alpha t k},\qquad
 N_k=\frac{d\sigma^2}{t^2k},\qquad b(C)=\frac{(1-e^{-C})^2}{C}.
\]
Summing the coordinate bias lower bounds and noise second moments, and multiplying by \(\omega\), yields
\[
 \mathbb E\|V_{\bar\theta_k}\|_\pi^2
 \geq (b(C_*)-o(1))B_k+(f(C_*)-o(1))N_k.
\]
The factor \(d\) in \(N_k\) is essential: each coordinate contributes \(\sigma^2/(t^2k)\) after multiplication by \(\omega\).

Finally, Young's inequality with \(A_*=27/10\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_{k,\gamma}
 &=\left(\sqrt{B_k/2}+\sqrt{eN_k/(1-\lambda)}\right)^2\
 &\leq\frac{37}{20}B_k+\frac{74e}{27}N_k.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The exact coefficient \(74e/27\), not a downward-rounded 7.45, is used. Put \(u=e^{-5.5}\). Since \(u<1/32\) and \(e<11/4\),
\[
 11b(5.5)=2(1-u)^2>37/20,\qquad
 11f(5.5)=8+4u-u^2>8>74e/27.
\]
The strict margins absorb the vanishing errors, proving the required liminf inequality. The denominator is positive because \(\sigma>0\). Also \(\alpha/a_1\to0\) and \(\varepsilon_{k,\gamma}\to0\): the constraint \(k\alpha t\omega\to5.5\), with fixed positive \(\lambda,\omega\), implies \(kt\to\infty\). Thus \(E_{k,\gamma}\) is the stated asymptotic equivalent of the full upper expression in this experiment.

## Source-repair accounting

The proof retains the source's covariance reduction, finite-power-sum representation, three-region resolvent analysis, and scalar sharpness experiment. Local source repairs are recorded for theory_003, theory_008, theory_009, theory_010, theory_011, and theory_021. In particular, the power-series integral keeps only equal indices; the near-one auxiliary argument does not invert a potentially singular positive-semidefinite matrix; the last arc is integrated correctly; the sharpness variance retains its dimension factor; and its final comparison is E/11.

For contextual completeness, the external Kreiss statement uses \(\sup_{j\geq0}\|Q^j\|\), not \(\sup_{j\geq1}\|Q^j\|\), on both sides of its comparison with the Kreiss constant. The matrix used above is real, finite dimensional, and power bounded, as already proved. The specialized estimate is nevertheless derived directly rather than inferred from that external theorem.
