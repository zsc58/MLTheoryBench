# Reference Proof

## Proof Structure

First characterize the projection by polyhedral KKT conditions. For each possible support of a multiplier, these conditions define a polyhedron in the input variable and yield an affine formula for the projection. A finite hyperplane refinement of these polyhedra gives the asserted cells and exceptional set. Finally, differentiability at a cell boundary forces the derivative to equal the linear part of any adjacent cell; elementary pseudoinverse identities then give the spectral and suppression conclusions.

## Complete Proof

Write the rows of $A$ as $a_1^T,\ldots,a_m^T$. Since $C$ is a nonempty closed convex set and the squared-distance objective is coercive and strictly convex, $g(z)$ exists and is unique for every $z$.

The projection optimality condition is

$$
z-g(z)\in N_C(g(z)).
$$

For a polyhedron, its normal cone at $y\in C$ is the cone generated by the normals of the inequalities active at $y$. Consequently, for every $z$ there are an index set $I\subseteq[m]$ and multipliers $\lambda_I\geq 0$ such that, with $y=g(z)$,

$$
y-z+A_I^T\lambda_I=0,\qquad A_Iy=b_I,\qquad Ay\leq b. \tag{1}
$$

Zero multipliers may be discarded, but neither the active set nor the multiplier vector is asserted to be unique.

For each $I\subseteq[m]$, let $S_I$ be the set of inputs $z$ for which some pair $(y,\lambda_I)$ satisfies (1). This is the projection onto the $z$-coordinates of a set described by finitely many linear equalities and inequalities, so $S_I$ is a polyhedron. The KKT characterization shows that the finitely many sets $S_I$ cover $\mathbb R^n$.

Fix $I$ and $z\in S_I$. Substituting $y=z-A_I^T\lambda_I$ into $A_Iy=b_I$ gives

$$
A_IA_I^T\lambda_I=A_Iz-b_I. \tag{2}
$$

The right side belongs to the range of $A_IA_I^T$. If $\lambda_I$ and $\lambda_I'$ both solve (2), their difference lies in $\ker(A_IA_I^T)=\ker(A_I^T)$, so they give the same value of $A_I^T\lambda_I$. Hence

$$
A_I^T\lambda_I=A_I^T(A_IA_I^T)^+(A_Iz-b_I)
$$

and therefore, throughout $S_I$,

$$
g(z)=P_Iz+c_I,
\quad
P_I=I_n-A_I^T(A_IA_I^T)^+A_I,
\quad
c_I=A_I^T(A_IA_I^T)^+b_I. \tag{3}
$$

This argument remains valid for redundant or linearly dependent active inequalities.

Choose a finite half-space representation of every $S_I$. Collect the supporting hyperplanes appearing in these representations, together with affine hyperplanes containing any lower-dimensional $S_I$, and discard trivial or repeated hyperplanes. Their union $E$ is a finite union of proper affine hyperplanes and thus has Lebesgue measure zero. The connected chambers of the resulting finite hyperplane arrangement are finitely many pairwise disjoint open polyhedral cells whose union is $\mathbb R^n\setminus E$.

Membership in each $S_I$ is constant on any such chamber. Because the sets $S_I$ cover the whole space, each chamber $R_k$ is contained in at least one $S_{I_k}$. Formula (3) then holds throughout $R_k$, and $A_{I_k}g(z)=b_{I_k}$ there. This proves the finite piecewise-affine assertion. It also shows directly that $g$ is differentiable on every chamber.

For use at exceptional points, note that the projection is continuous. Indeed, if $y_j=g(z_j)$, the two projection variational inequalities give

$$
\langle z_1-y_1,y_2-y_1\rangle\leq0,
\qquad
\langle z_2-y_2,y_1-y_2\rangle\leq0.
$$

Adding them yields $\|y_1-y_2\|^2\leq\langle y_1-y_2,z_1-z_2\rangle$, and hence $\|g(z_1)-g(z_2)\|\leq\|z_1-z_2\|$.

Now suppose that $g$ is differentiable at an arbitrary $z$, possibly with $z\in E$. Some full-dimensional chamber $R_k$ has $z$ in its closure. Continuity and (3) imply

$$
g(z)=P_{I_k}z+c_{I_k}.
$$

The tangent cone of $R_k$ at $z$ has nonempty interior. For every direction $h$ in the interior of this cone and all sufficiently small $t>0$, $z+th\in R_k$, so differentiability gives

$$
Dg(z)h=\lim_{t\downarrow0}\frac{g(z+th)-g(z)}{t}=P_{I_k}h.
$$

The two linear maps agree on a set with nonempty interior and therefore agree everywhere:

$$
Dg(z)=P_{I_k}=I_n-A_{I_k}^T(A_{I_k}A_{I_k}^T)^+A_{I_k}. \tag{4}
$$

The equality $A_{I_k}g(z)=b_{I_k}$ follows by taking limits from the chamber, so $I_k$ is active at $g(z)$.

Finally,

$$
Q_I=A_I^T(A_IA_I^T)^+A_I
$$

is the orthogonal projector onto $\operatorname{range}(A_I^T)$. Thus $P_I=I_n-Q_I$ is the orthogonal projector onto $\ker(A_I)$: it is symmetric and idempotent. Its eigenvalues consequently belong to $\{0,1\}$. Moreover,

$$
A_IP_I=0,
\qquad
P_IA_I^T=0.
$$

Applying these identities to (4) gives $A_{I_k}Dg(z)v=0$ for every $v$ and $Dg(z)A_{I_k}^Tu=0$ for every $u$. Hence infinitesimal output changes are tangent to the active constraints, while their normal directions are annihilated in differentiation. This proves every clause of the theorem.
