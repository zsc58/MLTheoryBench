# Reference Proof

## Proof Structure

Expand the normalized update through second order and compare it with a globally regular truncated recursion using a martingale maximal estimate. Identify the truncated recursion's actual ambient drift and joint covariance, apply diffusion approximation, and show that the limiting diffusion stays on the product of spheres. On that invariant set its drift is the one stated in the problem.

For two particles, compute the overlap drift and quadratic variation directly from the common matrix noise. Both interaction rules have the same scale density. Its endpoint asymptotics, finite-time endpoint inaccessibility, and bounded scale-martingale arguments give the exhaustive clustering alternatives and threshold.

## Complete Proof

### 1. Regularization and the normalized expansion

Write $h=L^{-1}$ and let $\mathcal M=(\mathbb S^{d-1})^N$. All constants below may depend on the fixed parameters, the common entry bound, and a fixed time horizon $T$, but not on $L$ or the layer index.

Both attention maps are smooth on the ambient Euclidean space; the denominator of the normalized map is everywhere positive. On $\mathcal M$ the interaction vectors are uniformly bounded. Since the matrix entries have a common bound, so are $G\Phi_{\theta,i}(x)$ for $x\in\mathcal M$. For sufficiently small $h$, the vectors being normalized have norm bounded below by $1/2$.

Choose a smooth compactly supported cutoff equal to one on a neighborhood of $\mathcal M$, and multiply the attention maps by it. Denote the resulting ambient maps by $\widehat\Phi_i$. Set

$$
F_i(x,G)=P_{x_i}G\widehat\Phi_i(x),\qquad
H_i(x,G)=g(x_i,G\widehat\Phi_i(x)),
$$

where

$$
g(x,u)=\frac{3\langle x,u\rangle^2-\|u\|^2}{2}x-\langle x,u\rangle u.
$$

The full vectors $F,H$ are globally bounded and Lipschitz in $x$, uniformly over the bounded matrix class. Their derivatives are bounded as well. For unit $x_i$ and bounded $u$, Taylor expansion gives

$$
\frac{x_i+\sqrt h\,u}{\|x_i+\sqrt h\,u\|}
=x_i+\sqrt h\,P_{x_i}u+h g(x_i,u)+h^{3/2}\mathcal R_i,
\qquad \|\mathcal R_i\|\le C.
$$

Consequently, the exact recursion satisfies

$$
X_{n+1}=X_n+\sqrt h\,F(X_n,G_{n+1})+hH(X_n,G_{n+1})+h^{3/2}\mathcal R_n,
$$

with uniformly bounded vector remainders. Define its coupled truncation by

$$
\widetilde X_{n+1}=\widetilde X_n+\sqrt h\,F(\widetilde X_n,G_{n+1})+hH(\widetilde X_n,G_{n+1}),
\qquad \widetilde X_0=X_0.
$$

### 2. Uniform-path control of the truncation error

Let $\Delta_n=X_n-\widetilde X_n$, and use the filtration generated by the initial vector and the first $n$ matrices. Since $F$ is linear in the centered fresh matrix,

$$
M_m=\sqrt h\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\bigl(F(X_k,G_{k+1})-F(\widetilde X_k,G_{k+1})\bigr)
$$

is a vector martingale. The difference recursion is

$$
\Delta_m=M_m+h\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\bigl(H(X_k,G_{k+1})-H(\widetilde X_k,G_{k+1})\bigr)
+h^{3/2}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathcal R_k.
$$

The discrete $L^2$ martingale maximal inequality and the Lipschitz bound give

$$
\mathbb E\max_{j\le m}\|M_j\|^2\le C h\sum_{k<m}\mathbb E\|\Delta_k\|^2.
$$

For $m\le \lceil T/h\rceil$, Cauchy-Schwarz bounds the squared maximum of the second sum by

$$
C h^2 m\sum_{k<m}\mathbb E\|\Delta_k\|^2
\le C_T h\sum_{k<m}\mathbb E\|\Delta_k\|^2,
$$

whereas the squared accumulated remainder is at most $C h^3m^2\le C_T h$. Thus, with $D_m=\mathbb E\max_{j\le m}\|\Delta_j\|^2$,

$$
D_m\le C_T h\sum_{k<m}D_k+C_T h.
$$

Discrete Gronwall yields

$$
\mathbb E\max_{m\le\lceil T/h\rceil}\|X_m-\widetilde X_m\|^2\le C_T h.
$$

Affine interpolation cannot increase the maximum difference between grid values. Therefore the exact and truncated interpolations differ by a quantity tending to zero in probability in the uniform norm on every finite horizon.

### 3. Conditional characteristics and diffusion approximation

For deterministic vectors $a,b,x$, independence, centering, and variance $\rho^2$ of the matrix entries imply

$$
\mathbb E[(Ga)(Gb)^\top]=\rho^2\langle a,b\rangle I_d,
$$

and consequently

$$
\mathbb E\|Ga\|^2=d\rho^2\|a\|^2,\qquad
\mathbb E\langle x,Ga\rangle^2=\rho^2\|a\|^2\|x\|^2,\qquad
\mathbb E[\langle x,Ga\rangle Ga]=\rho^2\|a\|^2x.
$$

Put $a_i=\widehat\Phi_i(x)$. The actual ambient mean of the second-order coefficient is

$$
\overline b_i(x)=\mathbb E H_i(x,G)
=\frac{\rho^2}{2}\|a_i\|^2(3\|x_i\|^2-d-2)x_i.
$$

Only when $\|x_i\|=1$ does this simplify to $(1-d)\rho^2\|a_i\|^2x_i/2$. The joint first-order covariance has blocks

$$
A_{ij}(x)=\mathbb E[F_i(x,G)F_j(x,G)^\top]
=\rho^2\langle a_i,a_j\rangle P_{x_i}P_{x_j}^\top.
$$

For a truncated increment $\xi_h=\sqrt h F+hH$, uniformly in the current state,

$$
h^{-1}\mathbb E[\xi_h\mid x]=\overline b(x),\qquad
h^{-1}\mathbb E[\xi_h\xi_h^\top\mid x]=A(x)+O(\sqrt h),
\qquad \|\xi_h\|\le C\sqrt h.
$$

In particular, the scaled conditional Lindeberg terms vanish.

The standard diffusion-approximation principle used here is the following: for time-step-$h$ Markov schemes, locally uniform convergence of the scaled conditional first and second moments to continuous coefficients, a conditional Lindeberg condition, compact containment on finite time horizons, and well-posedness of the limiting diffusion martingale problem imply weak convergence of their interpolations to that diffusion; vanishing maximum jumps make the limit and the affine-interpolation convergence continuous-path convergence. In the present application the stronger uniform bounds above hold. Compact containment follows directly because $F,H$ vanish outside a fixed compact set and each jump is bounded by $C\sqrt h$.

The limiting covariance is represented by one common matrix Brownian motion:

$$
dV_t^i=\overline b_i(V_t)dt+\rho P_{V_t^i}[dW_t\,\widehat\Phi_i(V_t)].
$$

Its drift and individual Brownian vector fields are smooth and compactly supported, hence bounded and globally Lipschitz. The standard Lipschitz SDE existence and uniqueness theorem gives a unique global strong solution for the initial law, with Brownian motion independent of the initial vector. Equivalently, its diffusion martingale problem is well posed: the usual SDE/martingale-problem correspondence identifies the generator $\overline b\cdot\nabla+\tfrac12 A:\nabla^2$, and uniqueness follows from the Lipschitz coefficients. Thus every hypothesis of the stated diffusion-approximation principle is satisfied, and the truncated interpolations converge to $V$.

### 4. Sphere invariance and identification with the stated SDE

For the ambient truncated-limit diffusion, set $r_i=\|V^i\|^2$. Since

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(P_xP_x^\top)=d-2\|x\|^2+\|x\|^4,
\qquad \langle x,P_xv\rangle=(1-\|x\|^2)\langle x,v\rangle,
$$

Itô's formula gives

$$
dr_i=\rho^2\|\widehat\Phi_i(V)\|^2(r_i-1)(4r_i-d)dt
+2\rho(1-r_i)\langle V^i,dW\,\widehat\Phi_i(V)\rangle.
$$

Every coefficient in the equation for $q_i=r_i-1$ contains $q_i$. Stop when the ambient process leaves a fixed large ball. The drift and quadratic-variation terms in Itô's formula for $q_i^2$ are bounded in absolute value by a constant times $q_i^2$. Taking expectations and applying Gronwall from $q_i(0)=0$ gives $q_i=0$ up to that stopping time. Localization proves that all particles stay on the sphere, simultaneously for all times almost surely.

On this invariant set $\widehat\Phi_i=\Phi_{\theta,i}$ and

$$
\overline b_i=\frac{1-d}{2}\rho^2\|\Phi_{\theta,i}\|^2 V^i.
$$

Hence $V$ solves exactly the SDE in the task.

For completeness, sphere invariance and uniqueness can also be checked directly for that stated SDE, without using the truncated-limit drift. Smoothly localize its ambient coefficients near $\mathcal M$. Before exiting that neighborhood, the full radial equation is

$$
dr_i=\rho^2\|\Phi_{\theta,i}(U)\|^2(r_i-1)(r_i-d)dt
+2\rho(1-r_i)\langle U^i,dW\,\Phi_{\theta,i}(U)\rangle.
$$

The same stopped square-moment argument gives $r_i=1$. The process therefore cannot leave the compact product of spheres, so the local solution is global and unique. This identifies $V$ with $U$ in law.

The uniform-path approximation from Section 2 and Slutsky's theorem on continuous path space now imply $X_L\Rightarrow U$ on every finite horizon. These convergences and finite-horizon tightness imply convergence on $C([0,\infty);(\mathbb R^d)^N)$ with its locally uniform topology. Every surviving conditional characteristic uses only the entry means and variance, so, with the initial law and other parameters fixed, the limiting law is independent of higher features of the entry distribution.

### 5. The closed overlap diffusion

Now let $N=2$, $Q^\top K=I_d$, and put $R_t=\langle U_t^1,U_t^2\rangle$. Sphere invariance gives

$$
\|U_t^1-U_t^2\|^2=2(1-R_t),\qquad
\|U_t^1+U_t^2\|^2=2(1+R_t).
$$

Write $x=U^1$, $y=U^2$, $r=\langle x,y\rangle$, and $a_i=\Phi_{\theta,i}(x,y)$. The common-noise covariance implies

$$
\langle dU^1,dU^2\rangle
=\rho^2\langle a_1,a_2\rangle\operatorname{Tr}(P_xP_y)dt
=\rho^2\langle a_1,a_2\rangle(d-2+r^2)dt.
$$

The overlap martingale coefficient, as a matrix paired with $dW$, is

$$
\rho\bigl[(y-rx)a_1^\top+(x-ry)a_2^\top\bigr].
$$

Using $\|y-rx\|^2=\|x-ry\|^2=1-r^2$ and $\langle y-rx,x-ry\rangle=-r(1-r^2)$, its squared Frobenius norm is

$$
\rho^2(1-r^2)\bigl(\|a_1\|^2+\|a_2\|^2-2r\langle a_1,a_2\rangle\bigr).
$$

For both interaction rules write

$$
a_1=p x+q y,\qquad a_2=p y+q x.
$$

For the averaged rule, $p=e^\theta/2$ and $q=e^{\theta r}/2$. For the normalized rule, $p=e^\theta/(e^\theta+e^{\theta r})$ and $q=e^{\theta r}/(e^\theta+e^{\theta r})$. In either case,

$$
\|a_1\|^2=\|a_2\|^2=p^2+q^2+2pq r,\qquad
\langle a_1,a_2\rangle=(p^2+q^2)r+2pq.
$$

Substituting into the drift and quadratic variation yields

$$
\mu(r)=\rho^2(1-r^2)\bigl[2(d-2)pq-r(p^2+q^2)\bigr],\qquad
\alpha(r)^2=2\rho^2(p^2+q^2)(1-r^2)^2.
$$

Normalizing the scalar overlap martingale by its strictly positive interior quadratic-variation coefficient gives a standard scalar Brownian motion until any interior exit time. Thus $dR=\mu(R)dt+\alpha(R)dB$. Explicitly, for the averaged rule,

$$
\begin{aligned}
dR_t={}&\frac{\rho^2}{4}(1-R_t^2)
\left[2(d-2)e^{\theta(1+R_t)}-R_t(e^{2\theta}+e^{2\theta R_t})\right]dt\\
&+\frac{\rho}{\sqrt2}(1-R_t^2)\sqrt{e^{2\theta}+e^{2\theta R_t}}\,dB_t.
\end{aligned}
$$

For the normalized rule,

$$
\begin{aligned}
dR_t={}&\rho^2(1-R_t^2)
\frac{2(d-2)e^{\theta(1+R_t)}-R_t(e^{2\theta}+e^{2\theta R_t})}
{(e^\theta+e^{\theta R_t})^2}dt\\
&+\sqrt2\rho(1-R_t^2)
\frac{\sqrt{e^{2\theta}+e^{2\theta R_t}}}{e^\theta+e^{\theta R_t}}\,dB_t.
\end{aligned}
$$

In particular, these equations agree at $\theta=0$, when the two attention rules themselves agree. The factor $\sqrt2$ in the normalized coefficient is essential.

### 6. Endpoint inaccessibility and scale asymptotics

Both equations have the form

$$
dR_t=(1-R_t^2)\bigl(c(R_t)dt+\gamma(R_t)dB_t\bigr),
$$

where $c,\gamma$ are smooth and bounded on $[-1,1]$ and $\gamma$ is strictly positive there. Before approaching an endpoint, Itô's formula for $Y=\operatorname{arctanh}R$ gives

$$
dY_t=\bigl[c(\tanh Y_t)+\tanh(Y_t)\gamma(\tanh Y_t)^2\bigr]dt
+\gamma(\tanh Y_t)dB_t.
$$

These coefficients are bounded and globally Lipschitz on $\mathbb R$. This SDE has no finite-time explosion, and transforming back proves $R_t\in(-1,1)$ for every finite $t$ almost surely when $R_0\in(-1,1)$.

For either interaction rule,

$$
\frac{2\mu(r)}{\alpha(r)^2}
=\frac{\psi(r)-r}{1-r^2},\qquad
\psi(r)=\frac{2(d-2)e^{\theta(1+r)}}{e^{2\theta}+e^{2\theta r}}
=\frac{d-2}{\cosh(\theta(1-r))}.
$$

Fix $r_*\in(-1,1)$ and define

$$
s(r)=\exp\left(-\int_{r_*}^r\frac{2\mu(y)}{\alpha(y)^2}dy\right),\qquad
S(r)=\int_{r_*}^r s(y)dy.
$$

The function $\psi$ is smooth on the closed interval, with $\psi(1)=d-2$ and $\psi(-1)=(d-2)/\cosh(2\theta)$. Thus

$$
\frac{2\mu(r)}{\alpha(r)^2}
=\frac{d-3}{2(1-r)}+O(1)\quad(r\uparrow1),
$$

and

$$
\frac{2\mu(r)}{\alpha(r)^2}
=\frac{1+(d-2)/\cosh(2\theta)}{2(1+r)}+O(1)\quad(r\downarrow-1).
$$

Integrating the bounded remainders shows that the scale density is asymptotic to a positive constant times the corresponding power:

$$
s(1-\delta)\asymp\delta^{(d-3)/2},\qquad
s(-1+\delta)\asymp\delta^{-1/2-(d-2)/(2\cosh(2\theta))}.
$$

Consequently, $S(1)<\infty$ for all $d\ge2$, whereas

$$
S(-1)>-\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d-2<\cosh(2\theta).
$$

At equality the left density is comparable to $\delta^{-1}$ and its integral diverges. This includes the boundary case $d=3,\theta=0$. For $d=2$, both powers are $-1/2$ and both integrals are finite.

### 7. Exhaustion of the clustering outcomes

The defining scale equation and Itô's formula give

$$
dS(R_t)=s(R_t)\alpha(R_t)dB_t.
$$

First justify interior hitting probabilities. For $-1<a<r<b<1$, stop at $\tau=\tau_a\wedge\tau_b$, where $\tau_z=\inf\{t\ge0:R_t=z\}$. The stopped scale martingale is bounded. Its quadratic-variation rate before $\tau$ has a strictly positive lower bound on $[a,b]$. The martingale square identity therefore bounds $\mathbb E(t\wedge\tau)$ uniformly in $t$, so $\tau<\infty$ almost surely. Optional stopping yields

$$
\mathbb P_r(\tau_b<\tau_a)=\frac{S(r)-S(a)}{S(b)-S(a)}.
$$

Suppose first that both endpoint scale limits are finite. Then $S(R_t)$ is a bounded martingale and converges almost surely and in $L^1$. Strict monotonicity of $S$ implies that $R_t$ has a limit in $[-1,1]$. This limit cannot be interior: a bounded martingale has finite total quadratic variation almost surely, whereas convergence of $R_t$ to any interior point would eventually keep $s(R_t)\alpha(R_t)$ bounded below by a positive constant and force infinite quadratic variation. Hence the limit is an endpoint. Taking expectations of the limiting scale variable gives

$$
\mathbb P_r(R_t\to1)=\frac{S(r)-S(-1)}{S(1)-S(-1)}>0,
\qquad
\mathbb P_r(R_t\to-1)=\frac{S(1)-S(r)}{S(1)-S(-1)}>0.
$$

Now suppose $S(-1)=-\infty$. Fix $a<r$. The process $S(R_{t\wedge\tau_a})$ is a bounded martingale with values in $[S(a),S(1)]$. It converges; on $\{\tau_a=\infty\}$ its corresponding overlap limit cannot be any point of $[a,1)$, by the same quadratic-variation argument. It must therefore be $1$. Taking expectations at the limit gives

$$
\mathbb P_r(\tau_a=\infty)=\frac{S(r)-S(a)}{S(1)-S(a)}.
$$

As $a\downarrow-1$ through a sequence, these avoidance events increase and their probabilities tend to one. Every such event implies $R_t\to1$, so $R_t\to1$ almost surely.

The distance identities from Section 5 translate these conclusions into $C_+$ and $C_-$. They prove exhaustiveness, positivity of $\mathbb P(C_+)$, and positivity of $\mathbb P(C_-)$ exactly when $d-2<\cosh(2\theta)$.

Finally, the scalar drift and Brownian coefficient extend smoothly to $[-1,1]$ and both vanish at its endpoints. Local uniqueness makes $R\equiv1$ and $R\equiv-1$ the solutions from those respective initial values. This proves both endpoint assertions.
