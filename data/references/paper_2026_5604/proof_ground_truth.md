# Reference Proof

## Proof Structure and Obligations

1. Establish the linear diffusion representation, covariance normalization, fundamental-matrix symmetry, and invertibility of every covariance that is inverted.
2. Construct the continuous endpoint-conditioned Gaussian process, including its marginal formulas and its values at both endpoints.
3. Establish the bridge drift using the positive Gaussian transition density and the diffusion-bridge generator identity, with its hypotheses checked.
4. Prove weak convergence on the full continuous-path space and explicitly simplify the control on the limiting path.
5. Retain the arbitrary affine forcing while deriving all heat spectral formulas, including zero modes and \(\kappa=0\).

The proof uses only the linear-Gaussian calculations and standard diffusion-bridge facts recovered from the source. Neither the marginal-control proposition nor the specialized generator-mixture result is a prerequisite.

## Complete Proof

### 1. Linear diffusion and covariance matrices

Write \(A_t=H_t(L)\). The matrices \(A_t\) are real symmetric and commute at all times because they are polynomials in the same real symmetric matrix. Consequently,
\[
 \Phi_{s,t}=\exp\left(\int_s^t A_q\,dq\right)
\]
solves the fundamental-matrix equation. It is symmetric, invertible, and satisfies
\[
 \Phi_{r,t}=\Phi_{s,t}\Phi_{r,s}\qquad(r\le s\le t).
\]
All these matrices and their inverses are bounded on their compact time domains. The continuous coefficients give a globally Lipschitz affine drift, so the reference linear SDE is well posed. Variation of constants gives
\[
 X_t=\Phi_{r,t}X_r+\int_r^t\Phi_{q,t}\alpha_q\,dq
       +\sigma\int_r^t\Phi_{q,t}\,dW_q.
\]
The deterministic integrands are square integrable. Brownian increments after \(r\) are independent of the state at \(r\), and their stochastic integrals are jointly Gaussian. Thus, conditional on \(X_r=x\), the mean is \(m_{t\mid r}(x)\) and the cross covariance at times \(s,t\ge r\) is
\[
 \sigma^2\int_r^{s\wedge t}\Phi_{q,s}\Phi_{q,t}^{\mathsf T}\,dq
 =\sigma^2\widetilde\Sigma_{s,t\mid r}.
\]
In particular, the covariance normalization contains exactly one factor \(\sigma^2\).

For \(r<t\) and a nonzero vector \(v\),
\[
 v^{\mathsf T}\widetilde\Sigma_{t,t\mid r}v
 =\int_r^t\|\Phi_{q,t}^{\mathsf T}v\|^2\,dq>0.
\]
Indeed, the integrand is continuous and equals \(\|v\|^2>0\) at \(q=t\). This proves positive definiteness of \(\widetilde\Sigma_{1,1}\) and of \(\widetilde\Sigma_{1,1\mid t}\) for every \(t<1\). No invertibility of \(L\), and no inverse at \(t=1\) of the latter covariance, is needed. If zero-dimensional signal spaces are admitted, every assertion is trivial on their singleton path space; the density calculations below concern \(d\ge1\).

### 2. Conditioning on the endpoint as a continuous-path law

Set
\[
 Z_t=\int_0^t\Phi_{q,t}\,dW_q,
 \qquad S=\widetilde\Sigma_{1,1},
 \qquad C_t=\widetilde\Sigma_{t,1},
 \qquad \delta=x_1-m_1(x_0).
\]
The process \(Z\) is continuous. For example, with \(F_t=\Phi_{0,t}\), it has the representation
\[
 Z_t=F_t\int_0^t F_q^{-1}\,dW_q,
\]
whose stochastic integral has continuous paths. The reference process started at \(x_0\) is \(X_t=m_t(x_0)+\sigma Z_t\).

Define the continuous centered Gaussian process
\[
 G_t=Z_t-C_tS^{-1}Z_1.
\]
For every time \(t\),
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(G_t,Z_1)=C_t-C_tS^{-1}S=0.
\]
Every finite collection of values of \(G\) is therefore independent of \(Z_1\), by joint Gaussianity. Since the Borel sigma-field of continuous-path space is generated by evaluations at rational times, \(G\) as a path-valued random variable is independent of \(Z_1\). We have the identity
\[
 X_t=m_t(x_0)+C_tS^{-1}\bigl(X_1-m_1(x_0)\bigr)+\sigma G_t.
\]
It follows that the conditional path law at terminal value \(x_1\) is the law of
\[
 b_t+\sigma G_t,
 \qquad b_t=m_t(x_0)+C_tS^{-1}\delta.
\]
This gives a version defined at every \(x_1\). It is weakly continuous in \(x_1\), because its deterministic translation is continuous in \(x_1\) in the uniform norm. The terminal Gaussian density is strictly positive everywhere, so this continuous version is unique among weakly continuous versions.

Computing the covariance of \(G\) gives, for all \(s,t\in[0,1]\),
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(G_s,G_t)
 =\widetilde\Sigma_{s,t}-\widetilde\Sigma_{s,1}
 S^{-1}\widetilde\Sigma_{1,t}.
\]
The asserted one-time Gaussian mean and covariance follow by setting \(s=t\). Degenerate Gaussian marginals at the endpoints cause no problem. In fact, \(C_0=0\), \(C_1=S\), and \(Z_0=0\), so
\[
 b_0=x_0,\qquad b_1=x_1,\qquad G_0=G_1=0.
\]
The formula for \(b\) is continuous up to both endpoints and agrees with the specified value \(b_1=x_1\).

### 3. Bridge drift and the transition-density score

Fix \(t<1\), and set \(R_t=\widetilde\Sigma_{1,1\mid t}\). The reference transition density is
\[
 p_{t,1}(x,x_1)
 =(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-d/2}(\det R_t)^{-1/2}
 \exp\left[-\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}
 (x_1-m_{1\mid t}(x))^{\mathsf T}R_t^{-1}
 (x_1-m_{1\mid t}(x))\right].
\]
It is strictly positive. As a function of \((t,x)\) on \(t<1\), it is continuously differentiable in time and twice continuously differentiable in space. These properties follow from the continuous deterministic coefficients, the fundamental-matrix formulas, and the strict positive definiteness of \(R_t\).

The standard diffusion-bridge identity used here has the following relevant form: for a Markov diffusion with covariance matrix \(\sigma^2 I\) and a positive sufficiently smooth transition density, conditioning on terminal value \(x_1\) adds the drift \(\sigma^2\nabla_x\log p_{t,1}(x,x_1)\). Its generator form can also be seen directly. Put \(\ell(t,x)=p_{t,1}(x,x_1)\). Bayes' formula and the Markov property give the bridge transition density, for \(s<t<1\), as
\[
 q_{s,t}(x,y)=p_{s,t}(x,y)\frac{\ell(t,y)}{\ell(s,x)}.
\]
The Gaussian transition density satisfies the backward equation
\(\partial_t\ell+\mathcal L_t\ell=0\), where
\[
 \mathcal L_t f=(A_tx+\alpha_t)\cdot\nabla f
                  +\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\Delta f.
\]
Differentiating the bridge transition operator and applying the product rule therefore gives
\[
 \mathcal L_t^{\mathrm{br}} f
 =\ell^{-1}\bigl(\mathcal L_t(\ell f)-f\mathcal L_t\ell\bigr)
 =\mathcal L_t f+\sigma^2\nabla\log\ell\cdot\nabla f.
\]
This is the generator of the asserted bridge diffusion. On every interval \([0,T]\) with \(T<1\), its drift coefficients are continuous and affine in the state, with bounded coefficients; thus the usual diffusion/SDE characterization applies there. The continuous endpoint extension is already supplied by the conditioned Gaussian path law above. This argument does not invoke generator matching or averaging over endpoint couplings.

Since \(D_xm_{1\mid t}(x)=\Phi_{t,1}\), differentiation of the displayed Gaussian density gives
\[
 \nabla_x\log p_{t,1}(x,x_1)
 =\sigma^{-2}\Phi_{t,1}^{\mathsf T}R_t^{-1}
       \bigl(x_1-m_{1\mid t}(x)\bigr).
\]
The symmetry of \(\Phi_{t,1}\), proved in Step 1, permits removal of its transpose. Multiplication by \(\sigma^2\) yields precisely
\[
 u_t(x)=\Phi_{t,1}R_t^{-1}\bigl(x_1-m_{1\mid t}(x)\bigr).
\]

### 4. Full-path zero-noise limit and control on the limiting path

The process \(G\) constructed in Step 2 does not depend on \(\sigma\), and \(\|G\|_\infty<\infty\) almost surely. On this common probability space,
\[
 \|b+\sigma G-b\|_\infty=\sigma\|G\|_\infty\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Consequently, the bridge laws converge weakly to \(\delta_b\) on the full space \(C([0,1];\mathbb R^d)\) with the uniform topology. This establishes more than convergence of individual marginal variances.

To simplify the control, put \(P_t=\Phi_{t,1}\) and \(Q_t=\widetilde\Sigma_{t,t}\). The fundamental-matrix composition rule and splitting the covariance integral at \(t\) give
\[
 C_t=Q_tP_t^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 S=P_tQ_tP_t^{\mathsf T}+R_t.
\]
Likewise, splitting the deterministic forcing integral gives
\[
 m_{1\mid t}\bigl(m_t(x_0)\bigr)=m_1(x_0).
\]
Because \(m_{1\mid t}\) is affine with linear part \(P_t\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 x_1-m_{1\mid t}(b_t)
 &=\delta-P_tC_tS^{-1}\delta\\
 &=(S-P_tC_t)S^{-1}\delta\\
 &=R_tS^{-1}\delta.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Substituting this identity into the bridge control gives, for every \(t<1\),
\[
 u_t(b_t)=P_tS^{-1}\delta
 =\Phi_{t,1}\widetilde\Sigma_{1,1}^{-1}
       \bigl(x_1-m_1(x_0)\bigr).
\]
This formula concerns the value on the deterministic limiting path; it does not assert that the finite-noise bridge drift is independent of an arbitrary state \(x\).

For consistency with the limiting dynamics, differentiating \(C_t\) gives
\(\dot C_t=A_tC_t+\Phi_{t,1}^{\mathsf T}\). Together with the mean equation and symmetry, this implies
\[
 \dot b_t=A_tb_t+\alpha_t+u_t(b_t),\qquad t<1.
\]
No uniform convergence of the random bridge controls near the terminal singularity is claimed or required.

### 5. Heat drift with arbitrary deterministic forcing

Now set \(A_t=-\kappa L\). Then
\[
 \Phi_{s,t}=e^{-\kappa L(t-s)},\qquad
 h_t=\int_0^t e^{-\kappa L(t-q)}\alpha_q\,dq,
\]
and
\[
 \dot h_t=-\kappa Lh_t+\alpha_t,\qquad h_0=0.
\]
Subtracting \(h_t\) from the reference process removes the affine forcing without changing the diffusion coefficient or the added bridge control. In an orthonormal eigenbasis, the centered coordinates therefore have scalar drift \(-a\), where \(a=\kappa\lambda_i\ge0\), and endpoints
\[
 y_{0,i}=\langle x_0,v_i\rangle,\qquad
 y_{1,i}=\langle x_1-h_1,v_i\rangle.
\]
In particular,
\[
 \langle m_1(x_0)-h_1,v_i\rangle=e^{-a}y_{0,i},
 \qquad
 \langle\delta,v_i\rangle=y_{1,i}-e^{-a}y_{0,i}.
\]
The deterministic translation is essential when \(\alpha\) is nonzero; no assumption \(\alpha=0\) is made.

For \(a>0\), integration in this eigendirection gives
\[
 S_i=\frac{1-e^{-2a}}{2a},\qquad
 (C_t)_i=e^{-a(1-t)}\frac{1-e^{-2at}}{2a}.
\]
Thus
\[
 \langle b_t-h_t,v_i\rangle
 =e^{-at}y_{0,i}
 +\beta_t\bigl(y_{1,i}-e^{-a}y_{0,i}\bigr),
 \qquad
 \beta_t=\frac{e^{-a(1-t)}(1-e^{-2at})}{1-e^{-2a}}.
\]
Elementary exponential identities give
\[
 \beta_t=\frac{\sinh(at)}{\sinh a},\qquad
 e^{-at}-e^{-a}\beta_t=\frac{\sinh(a(1-t))}{\sinh a}.
\]
These are the asserted hyperbolic-sine path coefficients. The limiting control is
\[
 \langle u_t(b_t),v_i\rangle
 =\frac{e^{-a(1-t)}}{S_i}
      \bigl(y_{1,i}-e^{-a}y_{0,i}\bigr)
 =\frac{2a e^{-a(1-t)}}{1-e^{-2a}}
      \bigl(y_{1,i}-e^{-a}y_{0,i}\bigr).
\]
For \(a=0\), the corresponding integrals are \(S_i=1\) and \((C_t)_i=t\), and the propagator is 1. Hence
\[
 \langle b_t-h_t,v_i\rangle=(1-t)y_{0,i}+ty_{1,i},
 \qquad
 \langle u_t(b_t),v_i\rangle=y_{1,i}-y_{0,i}.
\]
This covers zero eigenvalues, repeated eigenvalues, \(L=0\), and \(\kappa=0\). The path expressions agree with both endpoints. The controls are asserted only for \(t<1\), where their original bridge definition is valid.
