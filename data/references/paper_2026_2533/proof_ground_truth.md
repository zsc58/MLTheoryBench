# Reference Proof

## Proof Structure

Condition on training and fix the test covariate. Compare the fitted PIT with the true uniform PIT. Use generalized-inverse endpoint probabilities, with left limits, to control response coverage by the expected calibrated PIT gap. Derive the gap bound by coupling the marginal calibration PITs to uniform variables. Finally integrate the conditional discrepancy over covariates and take the joint training/calibration limit.

## Complete Proof

Write m for the training size and let T_m be the sigma-field generated by the training sample. All fitted quantities below are fixed conditional on T_m. Let P_X denote the common calibration/test covariate law. Fix x in the full-P_X-measure set where the true conditional response law is continuous, its CDF is strictly increasing on its support, and the stated consistency assumption holds.

Define

    delta_m(x) = sup_y |F-hat(y|x)-F(y|x)|,
    G_{m,x}(u) = P(F-hat(Y|x)<=u | T_m, X=x),
    Delta_m(x) = sup_{0<=u<=1} |G_{m,x}(u)-u|,
    bar-Delta_m = integral Delta_m(t) dP_X(t).

The conditional probability integral transform V=F(Y|x) is uniform on [0,1], since the true conditional CDF is continuous. One elementary verification is to use its generalized quantiles: for 0<t<1, continuity gives P(F(Y|x)<=t)=t; possible flat portions of the true CDF carry no response probability. Also |F-hat(Y|x)-V|<=delta_m(x). Thus, for every 0<=u<=1,

    max(0,u-delta_m(x)) <= G_{m,x}(u)
                            <= min(1,u+delta_m(x)).

Consequently Delta_m(x)<=delta_m(x)=o_P(1).

### Inverse Endpoints And Conditional Coverage

Let h be any proper CDF on R and q(u)=inf{y:h(y)>=u}, with inf(empty)=+infinity and q(0)=-infinity. For 0<u<=1, monotonicity and right-continuity imply

    {Y<q(u)} = {h(Y)<u}.

Indeed, if q(u) is finite, right-continuity ensures h(q(u))>=u, and all values below q(u) have CDF below u. If q(u)=+infinity, h(y)<u for every finite y. At u=0, both displayed events are empty. Under a continuous real-valued response law, P(Y=q(u))=0, including the impossible infinite-endpoint equalities. Therefore, if G is the CDF of h(Y),

    P(Y<=q(u)) = G(u-),

where G(0-)=0. For 0<=a<=b<=1 this gives

    P(q(a)<=Y<=q(b)) = G(b-)-G(a-).

This formula also covers a=b and endpoints zero and one. For u>0, taking left limits in |G(u)-u|<=Delta gives |G(u-)-u|<=Delta; at u=0 the corresponding discrepancy is zero.

Now fix T_m and X_{n+1}=x. The starting point and indices are deterministic, while calibration is independent of the test response. Put

    a=U_(L(z)),   b=U_(H(z)).

Conditional on the calibration sample as well, the preceding inverse identity applies with h=F-hat(·|x) and G=G_{m,x}. Averaging over calibration yields

    |P_cal,test(Y_{n+1} in C-hat(x) | T_m,X_{n+1}=x)
       - E_cal[b-a | T_m,X_{n+1}=x]| <= 2 Delta_m(x).       (1)

No inclusive-PIT event equivalence is used. In particular, the proof does not assume that the estimated CDF is continuous or strictly increasing.

### Marginal PIT Law And Order Statistics

Conditional on T_m, the calibration PITs are iid on [0,1], and their common CDF is

    G_m(u)=integral G_{m,t}(u) dP_X(t).

Hence

    sup_{0<=u<=1}|G_m(u)-u| <= bar-Delta_m.                (2)

For completeness, let G be any CDF of a variable in [0,1] with sup_u|G(u)-u|<=epsilon. Define, for 0<t<1,

    R(t)=inf{v in [0,1]:G(v)>=t}.

If v<t-epsilon, then G(v)<=v+epsilon<t. Also min(1,t+epsilon) has CDF at least t: use G(t+epsilon)>=t when t+epsilon<1, and G(1)=1 otherwise. Thus

    |R(t)-t|<=epsilon.                                   (3)

For iid Uniform(0,1) variables V_1,...,V_n, the variables R(V_j) have CDF G. Because R is nondecreasing, their kth order statistic is R(V_(k)), even when G has atoms. It follows from (3) that each order statistic is within epsilon of the coupled uniform order statistic.

The kth uniform order statistic has density

    n! / ((k-1)!(n-k)!) * v^(k-1) * (1-v)^(n-k),  0<v<1.

This density follows by choosing the k-1 observations below v and n-k above v. Its mean is k/(n+1), as integration gives

    integral_0^1 v^k(1-v)^(n-k) dv = k!(n-k)!/(n+1)!,

which follows by repeated integration by parts. Thus, for any fixed 1<=l<=h<=n,

    |E[U_(h)-U_(l)]-(h-l)/(n+1)|<=2 epsilon.              (4)

Apply (4) conditional on T_m and X_{n+1}=x with G=G_m, epsilon=bar-Delta_m, l=L(z), and h=H(z). Independence of calibration from the test covariate leaves its iid law unchanged. The bound holds uniformly over all admissible indices, so it also holds for their training- and covariate-dependent choice.

Let N=n+1 and w=1-alpha. The index definitions give

    H(z)-L(z)-wN
      = [ceil((z+w)N)-(z+w)N] + [zN-floor(zN)] - 1.

The right-hand side belongs to [-1,1), so

    |(H(z)-L(z))/(n+1)-(1-alpha)|<=1/(n+1).

Combining this with (4),

    |E_cal[b-a | T_m,X_{n+1}=x]-(1-alpha)|
       <=2 bar-Delta_m+1/(n+1).                          (5)

### Integrated Convergence And The Joint Limit

For almost every t, Delta_m(t)->P0 and 0<=Delta_m(t)<=1. For every eta>0,

    E_training Delta_m(t) <= eta + P_training(Delta_m(t)>eta),

so E_training Delta_m(t)->0. Dominated convergence over the fixed law P_X, followed by Tonelli's theorem, gives

    E_training bar-Delta_m
      = integral E_training Delta_m(t) dP_X(t) -> 0.

Markov's inequality then implies bar-Delta_m->P0. The relevant discrepancies are measurable: the CDF suprema can be taken over a countable dense set with endpoints, using right-continuity.

Combining (1) and (5), for almost every x,

    |P_cal,test(Y_{n+1} in C-hat(x) | T_m,X_{n+1}=x)
       -(1-alpha)|
       <=2 Delta_m(x)+2 bar-Delta_m+1/(n+1).

For every joint sequence m->infinity and n->infinity satisfying the stated index-domain conditions, this bound tends to zero in training-sample probability. Therefore

    P_cal,test(Y_{n+1} in C-hat(X_{n+1}) | D_tr,X_{n+1}=x)
       =1-alpha+o_P(1)

for P_X-almost every x, as required.
