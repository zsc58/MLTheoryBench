# Reference Proof

## Proof Structure

1. Establish centering and isotropy of a Gaussian TT probe.
2. Define the contracted interfaces and derive their conditional Gaussian moment recursion.
3. Use the fourth-moment case to obtain the quadratic-form variance and single-block Chebyshev bound.
4. Amplify independent blocks and distinguish the guarantee for arbitrary admissible block sizes from the sample budget obtained by choosing the smallest admissible integers.

## Complete Proof

All parameters and A are fixed. Every entry of every core is mutually independent, and different probes use independent cores. All contractions below are finite polynomials in finitely many Gaussian variables, so they are measurable and have all moments used below.

### 1. Centering and isotropy

For a coordinate I, expand the defining contraction of omega_I. Each summand has one centered entry from each of d independent cores, so E omega_I=0. In E[omega_I omega_J], independence across cores factors each summand into Gaussian second moments. A summand vanishes unless I=J and the internal contraction indices in the two copies agree. For I=J, exactly r^(d-1) summands contribute one. The normalization r^{-(d-1)} therefore gives E[omega_I omega_J]=1 when I=J and zero otherwise. Hence E[omega omega^T]=I_N.

### 2. A Gaussian matrix moment inequality

Fix an integer p>=1 and put C_p(r)=prod_{i=0}^{p-1}(r+2i). Let X be a deterministic s-by-t matrix and Z a t-by-r matrix of independent standard normal entries. An SVD of X and orthogonal invariance of the standard Gaussian distribution give

||XZ||_F^2 = sum_j sigma_j^2 V_j

in distribution, where sigma_j are the singular values of X and each V_j is chi-square with r degrees of freedom. Its moment is E V_j^p=C_p(r). For completeness, this follows by differentiating p times at zero the moment generating function E exp(t V_j)=(1-2t)^(-r/2), valid for t<1/2; the latter follows from the one-dimensional Gaussian integral and independence of the r squared normal entries.

Minkowski's inequality in L_p gives

(E||XZ||_F^{2p})^(1/p) <= sum_j sigma_j^2 (E V_j^p)^(1/p) = C_p(r)^(1/p)||X||_F^2.

Consequently,

E||XZ||_F^{2p} <= C_p(r)||X||_F^{2p}.

The same statement holds conditionally when X is measurable with respect to the conditioning sigma-field and Z is independent of that sigma-field. This includes X=0 and singular X.

### 3. Explicit TT interface recursion

Fix a deterministic unit vector u in R^{n^d}. For 1<=m<=d-1, define the unnormalized left interface L_m in R^{n^m times r} by

L_1(i,a)=G^(1)_{1,i,a},

L_m((J,i),a)=sum_{b=1}^r L_{m-1}(J,b)G^(m)_{b,i,a} for m>=2,

where J is a prefix of length m-1. Define U_m in R^{n^m times n^{d-m}} by U_m(J,K)=u_{(J,K)}, and put P_m=U_m^T L_m. Let F_m be the sigma-field generated by the first m cores.

At m=1, P_1=U_1^T L_1. The columns of L_1 are independent standard Gaussian vectors, and ||U_1||_F=||u||_2=1. The Gaussian matrix inequality gives

E||P_1||_F^{2p} <= C_p(r).

For 2<=m<=d-1, contraction of the indices gives, with K a suffix of length d-m,

P_m(K,a)=sum_{i=1}^n sum_{b=1}^r P_{m-1}((i,K),b)G^(m)_{b,i,a}.

Define the n^{d-m}-by-nr matrix X_m by

X_m(K,(i,b))=P_{m-1}((i,K),b).

This is a reshaping, so ||X_m||_F=||P_{m-1}||_F. Conditional on F_{m-1}, the matrix X_m is fixed, and the r columns assembled from G^(m) are independent standard Gaussian vectors in R^{nr}, independent of F_{m-1}. Thus the columns of P_m are conditionally independent with covariance X_m X_m^T. The preceding inequality yields

E[||P_m||_F^{2p}|F_{m-1}] <= C_p(r)||P_{m-1}||_F^{2p}.

Taking expectations and iterating gives

E||P_{d-1}||_F^{2p} <= C_p(r)^(d-1).

When d=2, there are no recursive steps between the base case and P_{d-1}, so the same formula holds directly.

The final contraction is

u^T omega = r^{-(d-1)/2} sum_{i=1}^n sum_{a=1}^r P_{d-1}(i,a)G^(d)_{a,i,1}.

Conditional on F_{d-1}, this is centered Gaussian with variance r^{-(d-1)}||P_{d-1}||_F^2. The scalar Gaussian moment formula, including zero variance, therefore gives

E[(u^T omega)^{2p}|F_{d-1}] = (2p-1)!! r^{-p(d-1)}||P_{d-1}||_F^{2p}.

Set Q_p(r)=prod_{i=1}^{p-1}(1+2i/r), with empty product one. Since C_p(r)=r^p Q_p(r), taking expectations proves

E[(u^T omega)^{2p}] <= (2p-1)!! Q_p(r)^(d-1).

Only p=2 is needed below. Scaling the unit-vector bound, and treating the zero vector directly, gives for every deterministic v in R^N

E[(v^T omega)^4] <= 3(1+2/r)^(d-1)||v||_2^4.

### 4. Quadratic-form variance and a single block

Take a PSD factorization A=BB^T and write b_1,...,b_N for the columns of B. Isotropy implies

E[omega^T A omega] = E||B^T omega||_2^2 = sum_j ||b_j||_2^2 = tr(A).

Cauchy-Schwarz and the directional fourth-moment estimate give

E[(omega^T A omega)^2]
= sum_{i,j} E[(b_i^T omega)^2(b_j^T omega)^2]
<= sum_{i,j} (E[(b_i^T omega)^4] E[(b_j^T omega)^4])^(1/2)
<= 3(1+2/r)^(d-1)(sum_j ||b_j||_2^2)^2
= 3(1+2/r)^(d-1)tr(A)^2.

This argument includes zero columns of B without normalization. Consequently,

Var(omega^T A omega) <= [3(1+2/r)^(d-1)-1]tr(A)^2.

Because r>=d-1>=1, log(1+2/r)<=2/r gives (1+2/r)^(d-1)<=e^2. Since 3e^2-1<22,

Var(omega^T A omega) <= 22 tr(A)^2.

Independence of the ell probes in a block gives Var(H_ell(A))<=22 tr(A)^2/ell. Since epsilon tr(A)>0, Chebyshev's inequality yields

P(|H_ell(A)-tr(A)|>epsilon tr(A)) <= 22/(ell epsilon^2) <= 1/4.

### 5. Median amplification

Let F_b be the indicator that block b lies outside the closed interval [tr(A)-epsilon tr(A),tr(A)+epsilon tr(A)]. These indicators are independent, with probabilities q_b<=1/4.

If fewer than k/2 blocks fail, a strict majority lie in that interval. For odd k their central order statistic lies in it; for even k both central order statistics lie in it. Therefore every allowed median also lies in the interval. Thus failure of the median implies S=sum_b F_b>=k/2.

Exponential Markov at t=log(3) and independence give

P(S>=k/2) <= 3^(-k/2) E[3^S]
= 3^(-k/2) prod_b (1+2q_b)
<= 3^(-k/2)(3/2)^k
= (3/4)^(k/2).

This proof applies to every positive integer k, including even k, and to q_b=0. Since log(4/3)/2>1/7, k>=7 log(1/delta) implies (3/4)^(k/2)<=delta. This proves the stated probability guarantee for every admissible k and ell.

### 6. Sample count

The guarantee does not bound how many probes an arbitrarily oversized choice of k and ell uses. For the complexity consequence, choose

k=ceil(7 log(1/delta)),  ell=ceil(88 epsilon^(-2)).

Both are positive integers. Because 0<epsilon<1,

ell <= 88 epsilon^(-2)+1 <= 89 epsilon^(-2),

and k<=7 log(1/delta)+1. Hence

k ell <= 89 epsilon^(-2)(1+7 log(1/delta)).

This is uniformly O(epsilon^(-2)(1+log(1/delta))) for 0<delta<1 and is O(epsilon^(-2)log(1/delta)) as delta tends to zero, uniformly in the other admissible parameters. The constants are independent of n,d,r,A. The additive one accounts only for integer rounding and does not alter the logarithmic small-failure-probability rate.

All theorem-critical obligations are explicitly closed; no unresolved specialized dependency remains.
