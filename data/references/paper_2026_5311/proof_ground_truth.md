# Reference Proof

## Proof Structure

The latent-task regret is not generally observable. First replace it, for the purpose of comparing expectations, by its posterior expected instantaneous regret given the history and chosen action. This produces an adapted process without changing either policy's expected regret. Apply an exponential change of measure to that process, factor interaction KL through the action kernels, and compare action mismatch to forward completion mismatch. Finally, use current-first conditional KL decomposition, self-contraction, and maximal-action coverage to obtain the observed mismatch. The log-loss interpretation is verified with the specified dominating kernels.

## Complete Proof

Put \(\mathcal F_t=\sigma(H_t)\) and \(m_t=\mathbb E_{\mathbb Q}r_t\). All regrets are bounded, since \(0\le r_t\le B\max\{b,h\}\).

### 1. Common posteriors and observable expected regret

The two interactions start from the same joint law of \((P,D_{1:T})\). Given these latent variables, the remaining interaction is generated by history-based action kernels and the deterministic observation map. The likelihood of a history contains policy action factors that depend only on that history; these factors cancel when conditioning the latent variables on a fixed history. Consequently the latent posterior at a fixed history is the same under both policies wherever both conditionals are relevant. Action sampling, conditional on history, is independent of the latent variables.

More explicitly, completion domination implies \(K_t(\cdot\mid h)\le V K_t^0(\cdot\mid h)\). Sequentially, the deployed law of every prefix, including its latent variables, is absolutely continuous with respect to the ideal law. Its likelihood ratio is the product of the action-kernel ratios and is a function of the observed prefix. The same statement holds for a prefix ending immediately after an action. Thus conditioning on such a prefix leaves the same latent posterior under both laws.

Choose measurable versions of
\[
c_t(h,x)=\mathbb E_{\mathbb Q}[f_P(x)-f_P(x_P^*)\mid H_{t-1}=h],
\qquad Y_t=c_t(H_{t-1},X_t).
\]
The preceding posterior and action-independence statements imply, under either \(\nu\in\{\pi_0,\pi\}\),
\[
\mathbb E_\nu[r_t\mid H_{t-1},X_t]=Y_t,
\qquad
\mathbb E_\nu\sum_tY_t=\operatorname{Reg}_T(\nu).
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, \(Y_t\) is \(\mathcal F_t\)-measurable. Under \(\mathbb Q\), conditional expectation preserves the range bound, so \(Y_t\in[u,u+L]\) almost surely, and the tower property gives
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathbb Q}[Y_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
=\mathbb E_{\mathbb Q}[r_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]=m_t.
\tag{2}
\]
This argument does not assert that the hidden-task variables \(r_t\) themselves are adapted.

### 2. Regret transport

Conditional Hoeffding's lemma states that a random variable in an interval of length \(L\), centered by its conditional mean, has conditional MGF at most \(\exp(\lambda^2L^2/8)\). Applying it to \(Y_t\) and using (2),
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathbb Q}[e^{\lambda(Y_t-m_t)}\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]
\le e^{\lambda^2L^2/8}.
\]
The preceding centered partial sum is \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\)-measurable. Iterated conditioning therefore yields
\[
\log\mathbb E_{\mathbb Q}\exp\!\left(\lambda\sum_{t=1}^T(Y_t-m_t)\right)
\le \lambda^2TL^2/8.
\tag{3}
\]
For absolutely continuous probability laws \(M\ll N\), the variational change-of-measure inequality is
\(\mathbb E_M F\le\mathrm{KL}(M\|N)+\log\mathbb E_Ne^F\).
Apply it to the full interaction laws and \(F=\lambda\sum_tY_t\). The random variable is bounded and absolute continuity was established above. Using (1) and (3), for every \(\lambda>0\),
\[
\operatorname{Reg}_T(\pi)-\operatorname{Reg}_T(\pi_0)
\le \frac{\mathrm{KL}(\mathbb P\|\mathbb Q)}{\lambda}+\frac{\lambda TL^2}{8}.
\]
Optimization, with \(\lambda\downarrow0\) when KL is zero, gives
\[
\operatorname{Reg}_T(\pi)-\operatorname{Reg}_T(\pi_0)
\le L\sqrt{\frac T2\mathrm{KL}(\mathbb P\|\mathbb Q)}.
\tag{4}
\]

### 3. Interaction-to-completion comparison

Factor the full joint laws by first drawing the common latent variables, then successively drawing each action and producing its observation. The initial law and observation kernels agree, and the action kernels depend only on observed histories. The conditional KL chain rule gives
\[
\mathrm{KL}(\mathbb P\|\mathbb Q)
=\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb E_{H_{t-1}\sim\mathbb P}
\mathrm{KL}(K_t(\cdot\mid H_{t-1})\|K_t^0(\cdot\mid H_{t-1})).
\tag{5}
\]
This also equals KL between the observed-history laws. Each action likelihood ratio is bounded above by \(V\), so the divergence in (5) is finite and at most \(T\log V\).

Data processing through the common measurable oracle gives, for each relevant history,
\[
\mathrm{KL}(K_t\|K_t^0)\le\mathrm{KL}(\widehat Q_t\|Q_t^0).
\]
Use the unnormalized squared Hellinger distance
\[
H^2(M,N)=\int(\sqrt{dM/d\mu}-\sqrt{dN/d\mu})^2\,d\mu.
\]
The standard comparisons in this normalization are \(H^2(M,N)\le\mathrm{KL}(M\|N)\), and, if \(M\le VN\),
\(\mathrm{KL}(M\|N)\le(2+\log V)H^2(M,N)\).
Completion domination verifies the second hypothesis. Symmetry of \(H^2\) therefore gives
\[
\mathrm{KL}(\widehat Q_t\|Q_t^0)
\le(2+\log V)\mathrm{KL}(Q_t^0\|\widehat Q_t).
\]
If the forward KL is infinite this inequality is immediate. Define privately
\[
\Delta_T^{\rm cmp}=\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb E_{H_{t-1}\sim\mathbb P}
\mathrm{KL}(Q_t^0(\cdot\mid H_{t-1})\|\widehat Q_t(\cdot\mid H_{t-1})).
\]
Then
\[
\mathrm{KL}(\mathbb P\|\mathbb Q)\le(2+\log V)\Delta_T^{\rm cmp}.
\tag{6}
\]

### 4. Observable mismatch bridge

If \(\Delta_T^{\rm obs}=+\infty\), the requested regret inequality follows immediately from bounded regret. Hence assume it is finite.

Fix a deployed history outside the null sets in the assumptions, and write
\[
g_{t,h}(x)=\mathrm{KL}(P^{\rm obs}_{t,h,x}\|\widehat P^{\rm obs}_{t,h,x}).
\]
At action \(B\), the observation map is \(d\mapsto(d,1)\), with measurable inverse \((s,1)\mapsto s\). Applying data processing in both directions gives
\[
g_{t,h}(B)=d_t(h).
\]
Nonnegativity and coverage imply
\[
\mathbb E_{X\sim K_t(\cdot\mid h)}g_{t,h}(X)\ge\rho d_t(h).
\tag{7}
\]
The left side is finite for almost every deployed history, so \(d_t(h)<\infty\) there. Self-contraction makes each \(\delta_{t,s}(h)\) finite as well. Finite first-coordinate KL gives first-coordinate absolute continuity. Inductively, finite conditional KL almost surely under each true prefix gives absolute continuity of the next joint prefix. Thus the hypotheses of the conditional KL chain rule hold at every step, and in the specified current-first order,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{KL}(Q_t^0(\cdot\mid h)\|\widehat Q_t(\cdot\mid h))
&=d_t(h)+\sum_{s=2}^T\delta_{t,s}(h)\\
&\le\left(1+a\sum_{s=2}^T\frac1s\right)d_t(h)\\
&\le(1+a\log T)d_t(h).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
For \(T=1\) the sum is empty and \(\log T=0\); for \(a=0\) all later conditional divergences vanish. No undefined product with infinity is used in this finite-mismatch argument.

Combining (7) and (8), then averaging and summing, gives
\[
\Delta_T^{\rm cmp}\le\frac{1+a\log T}{\rho}\Delta_T^{\rm obs}.
\tag{9}
\]
Substitute (9) into (6), and then into (4), to obtain
\[
\operatorname{Reg}_T(\pi)
\le\operatorname{Reg}_T(\pi_0)
+L\sqrt{\frac{T(2+\log V)(1+a\log T)}{2\rho}\Delta_T^{\rm obs}}.
\]
In particular, zero observed mismatch forces zero interaction KL and equal expected regrets. When \(V=1\), completion domination already forces equality of the completion kernels.

### 5. Predictive-loss identity

For a fixed \((t,h,x)\), abbreviate the observation laws by \(M,N\), set \(\mu=M+N\), and let \(p=dM/d\mu\), \(q=dN/d\mu\). Both densities belong to \([0,1]\), so the two negative log-losses are nonnegative. Moreover,
\[
\mathrm{KL}(M\|\mu/2)=\log2+\int\log p\,dM\ge0,
\]
which shows
\[
0\le\int-\log p\,dM\le\log2.
\]
The true population loss is therefore at most \(T\log2\). The model population loss lies in \([0,+\infty]\). The restored absolute-continuity condition permits the usual log-density ratio, and the finite true loss justifies
\[
\mathrm{KL}(M\|N)=\int-\log q\,dM-\int-\log p\,dM.
\]
Under the deployed interaction the conditional observation law is \(M\), by exogeneity and history-only action sampling. Integrating the preceding equality over deployed histories and actions and summing gives
\[
\Delta_T^{\rm obs}=\mathcal L_T(\widehat p;\pi)-\mathcal L_T(p^0;\pi).
\]
This is a defined extended-real difference, never infinity minus infinity, and proves the stated predictive-loss interpretation.
