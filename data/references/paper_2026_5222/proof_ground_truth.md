# Reference Proof

## Proof Structure and Obligations

The proof must establish: (1) bounded, conditionally mean-zero Bellman residuals, including the dummy transition; (2) conditional independence across trajectories and the weighted concentration bounds with the displayed constants; (3) the exact telescoping identity with both endpoint terms accounted for; and (4) the matching-loss bounds supplied by realizability. The only external inequality needed is the standard conditional Hoeffding inequality stated below. Exact minimization, optimizer existence, and least-second-moment selection are not proof obligations or hypotheses.

The source basis is the finite MDP and i.i.d. experiment at L000024–L000030, the loss and estimator at L000044–L000052, the unrestricted prefix-measurable weight guarantee at L000099–L000102 and L000115, and the proof at L000273–L000298. The additional assumptions of the later random-design analysis are not used.

## Well-Definedness

Every discriminator is real-valued on a finite state-action domain, so each policy expectation and each summand in the matching loss is finite. Realizability makes every discriminator class nonempty. The supremum may nevertheless be infinite because the class need not be uniformly bounded.

For each fixed array of sampled states and actions, the matching loss is a supremum of continuous functions of its two weight vectors. It is therefore lower semicontinuous and extended-real Borel measurable. There are only finitely many possible state-action arrays, so composing these functions with the measurable selected weights gives measurable losses. The estimator and empirical norms are finite measurable random variables because the weights are finite real vectors and the rewards are bounded. Thus the event in the conclusion is well-defined, including when a loss is infinite.

## 1. Bellman Residuals

For \(0\le h\le H\), let \(\mathcal G_h\) be the sigma-field generated by all trajectory prefixes through \((S_h^{(i)},A_h^{(i)})\), excluding \(R_h^{(i)}\) and the following transition. At \(h=0\), this sigma-field contains only the deterministic dummy pairs. Define
\[
Z_h^{(i)}=q_h(S_h^{(i)},A_h^{(i)})-R_h^{(i)}-q_{h+1}(S_{h+1}^{(i)},\pi).
\]
The evaluation policy and MDP are fixed, so the functions \(q_h\) are fixed. The Markov reward and transition laws, shared by behavior and evaluation trajectories, together with the Bellman equations imply
\[
\mathbb E[Z_h^{(i)}\mid\mathcal G_h]=0.
\]
Conditioning on other trajectories' prefixes does not change this conditional mean because the complete trajectories are independent.

For \(1\le h\le H\), both \(q_h(S_h^{(i)},A_h^{(i)})\) and \(R_h^{(i)}+q_{h+1}(S_{h+1}^{(i)},\pi)\) lie in \([0,(H-h+1)R_{\max}]\subseteq[0,B]\). At \(h=0\), the corresponding quantities are \(J(\pi)\) and \(q_1(S_1^{(i)},\pi)\), also in \([0,B]\). Consequently,
\[
|Z_h^{(i)}|\le B\quad\text{almost surely for all }h,i.
\]

## 2. Conditional Concentration

The relevant standard Hoeffding inequality is the following: if independent mean-zero variables \(X_i\) satisfy \(|X_i|\le c_i\), with finite nonnegative constants \(c_i\) and \(C=\sum_i c_i^2>0\), then for \(t>0\),
\[
\Pr\!\left(\left|\frac1n\sum_iX_i\right|>t\right)
\le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{n^2t^2}{2C}\right).
\]
If \(C=0\), the sum is identically zero. The same statement applies under a conditional law when the variables are conditionally independent and the bounds are fixed under that conditioning. This is the conditional Hoeffding result invoked in source L000279–L000281 and recorded as standard independent background in theory_014.

Fix \(h\) and condition on \(\mathcal G_h\). Independence of the complete trajectories implies that the conditional joint law of their next reward-transition pairs factors into the individual conditional laws given their respective prefixes. Thus \(Z_h^{(1)},\ldots,Z_h^{(n)}\) are conditionally independent. Their conditional means vanish and their absolute values are at most \(B\), as established above. Prefix measurability fixes every coefficient \(u_h^{(i)}\) under this conditioning, even though the coefficients may depend jointly on all prefixes and may have either sign.

Set
\[
C_h:=B^2\sum_{i=1}^n(u_h^{(i)})^2=nB^2\|u_h\|_{[n]}^2.
\]
This quantity is finite and \(\mathcal G_h\)-measurable. On conditional realizations where \(C_h>0\), apply Hoeffding with \(X_i=u_h^{(i)}Z_h^{(i)}\) and \(c_i=B|u_h^{(i)}|\). It gives
\[
\Pr\left(\left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n u_h^{(i)}Z_h^{(i)}\right|>t\ \middle|\ \mathcal G_h\right)
\le 2\exp\left(-\frac{nt^2}{2B^2\|u_h\|_{[n]}^2}\right).
\]
The threshold \(\eta_h\) is fixed under this conditional law. Substituting its definition yields
\[
2\exp\left(-\frac{n\eta_h^2}{2B^2\|u_h\|_{[n]}^2}\right)
=2\exp\left(-\log\frac{2(H+1)}{\delta}\right)
=\frac{\delta}{H+1}.
\]
If \(C_h=0\), every weighted residual is zero and \(\eta_h=0\), so the same failure-probability bound holds without dividing by zero. This handles both \(B=0\) and zero weight vectors. At \(h=0\), the argument applies with \(u_0^{(i)}=1\).

These are conditional-law arguments with finite coefficients; no unconditional moment bound on the learned weights is required. Averaging the conditional failure probabilities and taking a union bound over \(h=0,\ldots,H\) gives an event \(\mathcal E\) with \(\Pr(\mathcal E)\ge1-\delta\) on which
\[
\left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n u_h^{(i)}Z_h^{(i)}\right|\le\eta_h,
\qquad 0\le h\le H. \tag{1}
\]
No independence across stages, and no simultaneous event over all possible selection rules, is asserted or needed.

## 3. Telescoping Identity

For each observed transition,
\[
R_h^{(i)}=q_h(S_h^{(i)},A_h^{(i)})-q_{h+1}(S_{h+1}^{(i)},\pi)-Z_h^{(i)}.
\]
At the initial transition, this also gives
\[
J(\pi)=q_1(S_1^{(i)},\pi)+Z_0^{(i)},
\]
since the initial pair is fixed and \(R_0^{(i)}=0\). Sum the reward identities for \(h=1,\ldots,H\), average the initial identity using \(u_0^{(i)}=1\), and subtract. Reindexing the next-value terms gives the exact equality
\[
\widehat J(\pi)-J(\pi)
=
\sum_{h=1}^H\left[
 \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n u_h^{(i)}q_h(S_h^{(i)},A_h^{(i)})
 -\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n u_{h-1}^{(i)}q_h(S_h^{(i)},\pi)
\right]
-\sum_{h=0}^H\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n u_h^{(i)}Z_h^{(i)}. \tag{2}
\]
There is no terminal remainder because \(q_{H+1}\equiv0\). No weight vector at stage \(H+1\) is needed.

## 4. Realizability and Conclusion

For each \(1\le h\le H\), realizability gives \(q_h\in\mathcal F_h\). Therefore the absolute value of the corresponding bracketed term in (2) is at most \(\ell_h(u_h;u_{h-1})\). On \(\mathcal E\), the triangle inequality and (1) now imply
\[
|\widehat J(\pi)-J(\pi)|
\le\sum_{h=1}^H\ell_h(u_h;u_{h-1})+\sum_{h=0}^H\eta_h.
\]
If any matching loss is infinite, this inequality holds in the stipulated extended-real sense as well. Since \(\Pr(\mathcal E)\ge1-\delta\), the result follows for every fixed admissible family of selection rules. All four theorem-critical obligations are closed.
