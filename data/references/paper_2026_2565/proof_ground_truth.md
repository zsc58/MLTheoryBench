# Reference Proof

Write b=q_-\mu and h=\log(2T/\delta). All bounds below hold pathwise unless a probability is stated. The source's index repair recorded for theory_012 is used: quantities inside a sum over \ell carry index \ell or \ell+1, not the outer time index.

The bias-corrected moments are weighted averages with weights w_{i,t,k}. Consequently \|\widehat m_t\|\leq G, every coordinate of \widehat v_t lies in [0,G^2], and \|\theta_{t+1}-\theta_t\|\leq\alpha q_+G. Conditional expectation gives \widetilde v_{t+1}=\beta_2v_t+(1-\beta_2)s_{t+1}(\theta_t). Both preconditioners have operator norm at most q_+. Put r_{t+1}=\widehat m_{t+1}-\bar g_{t+1}(\theta_t) and \eta_{t+1}=(P_{t+1}-\widetilde P_{t+1})\widehat m_{t+1}. Then P_{t+1}\widehat m_{t+1}=\widetilde P_{t+1}\bar g_{t+1}(\theta_t)+\widetilde P_{t+1}r_{t+1}+\eta_{t+1}; this identity is applied before Euclidean projection.

For nonnegative a,c, |(\sqrt a+\epsilon)^{-1}-(\sqrt c+\epsilon)^{-1}|^2\leq\epsilon^{-4}|a-c|. Also \|v_{t+1}\|_\infty\leq G^2(1-\beta_2^{t+1}) and \|g_{t+1}^{\odot2}-s_{t+1}(\theta_t)\|_\infty\leq G^2. Splitting \widehat v_{t+1}-\widetilde v_{t+1} into its bias-correction part and (1-\beta_2)(g_{t+1}^{\odot2}-s_{t+1}(\theta_t)) therefore gives \|\eta_{t+1}\|^2\leq G^4\epsilon^{-4}[\beta_2^{t+1}+1-\beta_2].

Unroll \widehat m_{t+1}. Its error r_{t+1} is the sum of B_{t+1}=\sum_{k=0}^t w_{1,t+1,k}[\bar g_{k+1}(\theta_k)-\bar g_{t+1}(\theta_t)] and N_{t+1}=\sum_{k=0}^t w_{1,t+1,k}\xi_{k+1}. The difference between mean gradients at successive iterates and times has norm at most L\alpha q_+G+2G=D_1(\alpha). Telescoping gives \|B_{t+1}\|\leq D_1(\alpha)\sum_k w_{1,t+1,k}(t-k)=c_{1,t+1}D_1(\alpha). For fixed t, the summands of N_{t+1} are martingale differences: X_{k+1} and hence \xi_{k+1} are \mathcal F_{k+1}-measurable, and their conditional means vanish. They are bounded by 2G\omega_{1,t+1}; their predictable sum of squared norms is at most d\sigma^2\sum_k w_{1,t+1,k}^2=d\sigma^2\kappa_{1,t+1}. The latter follows from the conditional projection-Orlicz bound by Jensen's inequality, applied to an orthonormal basis. The Hilbert-space Freedman inequality stated in the source says that bounded martingale differences with increment bound R and predictable squared-norm sum at most v satisfy \mathbb P(\|\sum Z_k\|>\sqrt{2vx}+(2R/3)x)\leq2e^{-x}. Its hypotheses hold here. Taking x=h and a union bound over the T values of t gives \|r_{t+1}\|\leq R_{t+1} simultaneously for 0\leq t<T with probability at least 1-\delta.

For the one-step estimate let d_t=\theta_t-\theta_{t+1}^* and u_t=d_t-\alpha\widetilde P_{t+1}\bar g_{t+1}(\theta_t). The zero gradient at the interior minimizer, monotonicity for this run's predictable preconditioner, and Lipschitzness imply \|u_t\|^2\leq(1-2\alpha b+\alpha^2q_+^2L^2)\|d_t\|^2\leq(1-\alpha b)\|d_t\|^2. Apply projection nonexpansiveness, then Young's inequality with parameter \alpha b/4 to the combined error -\alpha(\widetilde P_{t+1}r_{t+1}+\eta_{t+1}). Since \alpha b\leq1, its squared-error coefficients are at most 10\alpha q_+^2/b and 10\alpha/b, and the coefficient on \|d_t\|^2 is at most 1-3\alpha b/4. A second Young inequality, \|d_t\|^2\leq(1+\alpha b/4)\|\theta_t-\theta_t^*\|^2+5\Delta_t^2/(\alpha b), yields
\[
e_{t+1}\leq(1-\alpha b/2)e_t+\frac{5\Delta_t^2}{\alpha b}+\frac{10\alpha q_+^2}{b}\|r_{t+1}\|^2+\frac{10\alpha}{b}\|\eta_{t+1}\|^2.
\]
Here e_t=\|\theta_t-\theta_t^*\|^2. Unroll this pathwise inequality and substitute the two established bounds on their common event. This proves the simultaneous finite-time assertion, including t=0.

For the floor put C=\beta_1/(1-\beta_1), V=(1-\beta_1)/(1+\beta_1), W=1-\beta_1, and A=C D_1(\alpha)+\sigma\sqrt{2dVh}+(4G/3)Wh. These are the limits of c_{1,t}, \kappa_{1,t}, and \omega_{1,t}. The displayed F is 10\Delta^2/(\alpha^2b^2)+20q_+^2A^2/b^2+20G^4\epsilon^{-4}(1-\beta_2)/b^2. The constant-step result e_t\leq\rho^t e_0+F is uniform, even though the individual second-moment transient is not bounded by its limiting value: for t\geq1, monotonicity between \theta_t and \theta_t^*, the zero gradient, and the uniform sample-gradient bound imply e_t\leq(q_+G/b)^2. Moreover A\geq G/2. Indeed, for \beta_1\geq1/5, CD_1(\alpha)\geq G/2; otherwise W>4/5 and (4G/3)Wh>(16G/15)\log2>G/2. Thus 20q_+^2A^2/b^2\geq5(q_+G/b)^2, proving the uniform floor claim without discarding a positive transient. This coarse pathwise check supplements the asymptotic geometric-sum calculation; it does not identify the transient with zero at finite time. For e_0=0, \log_+(0)=0 gives burn-in threshold zero, and the bound follows directly. For e_0>0, \rho^t e_0\leq F once t\geq\lceil\log_+(e_0/F)/(-\log\rho)\rceil. Hence the error is at most 2F in either case.

For the restarted claim, fix the pair (K,\alpha^*) stipulated in the task before assigning \delta/K or computing epoch lengths. The trivial initial sigma-algebra makes e_0 deterministic; F_j and n_j are deterministic too. In epoch j, let t=s_j+k. Its actual uncorrected second moment is v_k^{(j)}, initialized at zero, so its predictable preconditioner is
\[
\widetilde P_{k+1}^{(j)}=\operatorname{Diag}\!\left((\sqrt{\beta_2v_k^{(j)}+(1-\beta_2)s_{t+1}(\theta_t)}+\epsilon)^{-1}\right).
\]
It is generally different from the predictable preconditioner of an uninterrupted run. The restarted-run assumption explicitly supplies adaptive strong monotonicity for this matrix and \bar g_{t+1}, with the same b; its along-iterate conditional noise assumption also applies to the restarted \theta_t. Thus the one-step contraction and martingale estimates above apply with local index k, step size \alpha_j, and D_1(\alpha_j). Bounded gradients and drift remain uniform. Applying these estimates over the deterministic n_j steps with confidence \delta/K, where n_j\leq T, gives an epoch event of probability at least 1-\delta/K on which e_{s_{j+1}}\leq\rho_j^{n_j}e_{s_j}+F_j. No independence between epochs is required.

The first length makes its transient at most F_0/2, including when e_0=0, since n_0\geq1 and \log_+(0)=0. For j\geq1, \rho_j^{n_j}\leq1/8. The affine schedule gives \alpha_{j-1}\leq2\alpha_j and D_1(\alpha_{j-1})\leq2D_1(\alpha_j); comparing the three nonnegative terms defining F gives F_{j-1}\leq4F_j. Induction therefore gives e_{s_{j+1}}\leq2F_j for all epochs on the intersection of their events. A union bound gives probability at least 1-\delta. Finally, \alpha_{K-1}\leq2\alpha^* and F_{K-1}\leq4F(\alpha^*,\delta/K,T). Summing the ceiling bounds, using \sum_{j=1}^{K-1}\alpha_j^{-1}=O((\alpha^*)^{-1}) and K=O((b\alpha^*)^{-1}), proves the stated total-time scaling. The convention at zero and F_j>0 make every epoch length and logarithm in this argument defined.
