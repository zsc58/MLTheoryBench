# Reference Proof

## Proof Structure

Couple the policies until their first action disagreement. Verify that the posterior bound can be applied to the combined observations of the coupled experiment, and then sum the disagreement penalties. Separately, compare the action laws through their sampled vectors and calculate the Gaussian KL divergence.

## Complete Proof

Write C=2B_R. The assumed bound is on E[|f_0(x,a)-f_0(x,a')| | H], not on differences of posterior means.

### 1. Coupling and the combined posterior

Use one draw of the latent arm laws and one common exogenous context sequence. At each round, conditional on the combined past and current context, couple the actions with the correct marginal action kernels for the two policies. Until the first disagreement, use a maximal coupling of Q_t and P_t^Pi at their common history. After disagreement, independent draws from their respective action kernels suffice. All action-coupling randomization is fresh and conditionally independent of the latent laws.

For completeness, maximal coupling is available here because the action space is finite. For probability vectors p and q, put c_a=min(p_a,q_a) and d=1-sum_a c_a=TV(p,q). With probability 1-d, choose a shared action with probabilities c_a/(1-d). With probability d, draw from the residual distributions (p-c)/d and (q-c)/d; their supports are disjoint. Omit any branch of probability zero. The resulting marginals are p and q, and the disagreement probability is d. This construction is measurable in p and q.

If the coupled actions agree, generate one fresh reward from that arm's conditional reward law and give it to both policies. If they differ, generate fresh conditionally independent rewards for the two selected arms. These choices preserve each policy's marginal experiment and ensure identical histories until disagreement. In particular, do not impose a shared noise realization across distinct-arm observations.

Let C_{t-1} be the full combined transcript before round t, with shared reward observations counted once. Let D(C_{t-1}) be its collection of arm-specific context-reward observations. The posterior given C_{t-1} is the ordinary posterior based on D(C_{t-1}). To verify this, factor the conditional likelihood of a finite transcript given the latent laws. Context factors do not depend on those laws. Conditional action-pair probabilities depend only on already observed transcripts and current contexts, so they also contribute no latent-dependent factor. Each distinct reward observation contributes exactly its arm's conditional observation likelihood. Thus the only latent-dependent factors are the usual reward likelihoods, grouped by arm. With the product prior, these are exactly the arm-specific posterior updates applied to D(C_{t-1}). This reasoning also holds through conditional probability kernels when densities are not used.

Conditional on C_{t-1} and X_t, the current action pair is generated independently of the latent laws. The exogenous context itself supplies no additional information about those laws. Consequently, for the sigma-field J_t generated by C_{t-1}, X_t, and both current actions,

E[|f_0(X_t,A_t^{Pi-TS})-f_0(X_t,A_t^{approx})| | J_t] <= C.

Indeed, after fixing the context and action pair, this is precisely the public assumption applied to the finite history collection D(C_{t-1}). The assumption ranges over all finite admissible arm-specific histories, not just one policy's realized histories.

### 2. First disagreement and regret comparison

Let tau be the first round at which the coupled actions differ, with tau=infinity if they never differ. On {tau>=s}, the histories at the beginning of round s coincide, so the maximal coupling gives a conditional disagreement probability at most epsilon_s. Therefore

P(tau=s) <= epsilon_s P(tau>=s) <= epsilon_s,

and hence

P(tau<=t) <= sum_{s=1}^t epsilon_s.

The two policies share the latent function and the context at every round. Their oracle terms therefore cancel, so their instantaneous regret difference equals

f_0(X_t,A_t^{Pi-TS})-f_0(X_t,A_t^{approx}).

This difference is zero on {tau>t}. The event {tau<=t} is J_t-measurable. Applying the conditional bound from the preceding section yields

E[1_{tau<=t}|f_0(X_t,A_t^{Pi-TS})-f_0(X_t,A_t^{approx})|] <= C P(tau<=t).

The expected regrets are finite. For any action and any finite reward vector, its gap from the maximum is bounded by the sum of all unordered pairwise absolute differences. The uniform posterior assumption bounds the conditional expectation of that sum by C times K(K-1)/2. This also covers K=1, when the sum and regret vanish.

Summing the instantaneous comparison and exchanging finite sums now gives

E[Regret_T^{approx}] - E[Regret_T^{Pi-TS}]
<= C sum_{t=1}^T P(tau<=t)
<= C sum_{t=1}^T sum_{s=1}^t epsilon_s
= 2B_R sum_{s=1}^T (T-s+1)epsilon_s.

This proves the first target inequality, including the cases B_R=0 and zero discrepancy.

### 3. Reduction from action laws to vector laws

Fix t, x, and an admissible history collection H. Let h:R^K -> {1,...,K} be the specified measurable argmax map. Then Q_t is the pushforward of G_t^{approx} by h, and P_t^Pi is the pushforward of S_t^Pi by h.

For probability laws M,N and a measurable map h, TV(h_*M,h_*N)<=TV(M,N): each event in the image has a measurable preimage. The triangle inequality follows directly from the event-supremum definition of TV. Thus

TV(Q_t,P_t^Pi)
<= TV(G_t^{approx},S_t^Pi)
<= TV(G_t^{approx},G_t^Pi)+TV(G_t^Pi,S_t^Pi)
<= TV(G_t^{approx},G_t^Pi)+rho_t.

All laws in this display are evaluated at the same t,x,H. In particular, S_t^Pi is the full-history posterior law; no equality between that posterior and its Gaussian approximation is assumed.

### 4. Gaussian calculation

We use Pinsker's inequality in the convention TV(M,N)=sup_E |M(E)-N(E)|:

TV(M,N) <= sqrt(KL(M||N)/2).

For univariate normal laws with real means u,v and positive finite variances alpha,beta,

KL(N(u,alpha)||N(v,beta))
= (1/2)[alpha/beta+(u-v)^2/beta-1+log(beta/alpha)].

This formula follows by taking the expectation of the log density ratio under N(u,alpha): the relevant quadratic expectations are alpha and alpha+(u-v)^2. For independent product laws, log density ratios add and so do their expectations.

Here every coordinate variance is positive and finite, so the Gaussian densities are mutually absolutely continuous and the formulas apply. Substitute

alpha=V_{t,a}^{approx}/s_a,
 beta=V_{t,a}^Pi/s_a,
 u-v=Delta_{t,a}.

It follows that

KL(G_t^{approx}||G_t^Pi)
= (1/2) sum_{a=1}^K [r_{t,a}+s_a Delta_{t,a}^2/V_{t,a}^Pi-1-log r_{t,a}].

Pinsker therefore gives

TV(G_t^{approx},G_t^Pi)
<= (1/2) sqrt(sum_{a=1}^K [r_{t,a}+s_a Delta_{t,a}^2/V_{t,a}^Pi-1-log r_{t,a}]).

Combining this with the previous section and taking the stipulated supremum proves

epsilon_t <= rho_t + (1/2) sup_{x,H} sqrt(sum_{a=1}^K [r_{t,a}+s_a Delta_{t,a}^2/V_{t,a}^Pi-1-log r_{t,a}]).

Each summand is nonnegative because r-1-log r>=0 for r>0. The expression is finite at each fixed admissible t,x,H, but its supremum need not be finite. If that supremum is infinite, the displayed inequality holds in the extended-real sense. The TV quantities themselves always lie in [0,1]. Both requested inequalities are proved.
