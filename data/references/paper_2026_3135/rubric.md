# Scoring Guide

- **0:** No correct idea or relevant valid method.
- **1-2:** Nontrivial relevant ideas, but no complete correct proof step or useful conclusion.
- **3-7:** Some correct core steps or conclusions, but important steps are missing.
- **8-9:** Complete and essentially correct, with only readily repairable errors or omitted cases.
- **10:** Fully correct.

# Core Steps

1. **2 points:** Correctly analyzes the stipulated mean-reverting drift, justifies Ito's formula and the covariance upper bound, and derives E||A_T-g*(x)||^2 <= exp(-2KT)m_2(mu_0)+d Lambda(1-exp(-2KT))/(2K). The O(1/K) assertion is for fixed T>0, not uniformly over arbitrarily small horizons.
2. **3 points:** Derives pointwise convergence of averaged rewards and transition kernels using terminal concentration and the explicit local moduli. Proves finite-horizon value convergence, including the fixed-kernel dominated-convergence step in Bellman induction, and uses a uniform discounted-tail bound and bounded convergence under every rho to obtain the infinite-horizon claim. No additional integrability of the moduli or their state-dependent radii is required. The argument must cover gamma=0.
3. **3 points:** Establishes the uniform finite-set localization lower bound for the full bounded state-dependent volatility class. The short-horizon case must control deterministic-flow concentration using absolute continuity of the fixed initialization and explicitly control the diffusion-to-flow perturbation. The final-window argument must exhibit the time/space rescaling, verify its coefficient bounds, establish a positive probability of avoiding all targets, and justify clipping. It must cover scalar actions as well as higher dimensions and preserve uniformity over target locations, separations, horizons, policies, and states. A precisely stated independent quantitative Krylov lower-estimate theorem may be used as standard external background, but its relevant hypotheses must be verified and the paper-specific local reduction, finite-set argument, and scalar embedding must be supplied. For the external theorem formulation used in the reference proof, explicitly state the small-scale hypothesis bar_N b_(R_*)<1, with bar_N depending only on dimension and ellipticity, and verify that the chosen profile admits such a scale. For b_ell=C_D(ell^(D+1) wedge 1), the choice R_*=min{1,(2 bar_N C_D)^(-1/(D+1))} suffices; any equivalent verification is acceptable. Merely naming Krylov, elliptic smoothing, or anti-concentration does not close this step.
4. **2 points:** Derives the Bellman-gap transfer, either through the one-step Bellman decomposition or the discounted performance-difference identity, and applies the clipped quadratic gap to obtain the uniform c/K value lower bound. If occupancy measures are used, define their normalization. The final constants must depend only on M,R,d,kappa,Lambda,mu_0; do not introduce dependence on gamma by absorbing (1-gamma)^(-1) into the constant. Taking a minimum and maximum over cardinalities 1,...,M, or an equivalent uniform construction, closes the cardinality issue.

# Evaluator Notes

Accept a complete valid alternative proof. Do not require the reference proof's incidental presentation choices or require an additive-volatility density argument when the bounded state-dependent argument already covers the public policy class. A proof restricted to state-independent volatility does not establish the full converse. Constants may be renamed provided their dependence and uniformity are preserved. Standard independent results may be invoked with precise relevant statements and verified hypotheses; paper-specific localization claims and named specialized methods cannot replace the derivation of the task's central bound. An external theorem's finite universal constants need not be numerically evaluated, but every application condition involving them must be stated and checked. The upper target is value convergence, not an O(1/K) value rate under mere continuity. All public objects and quantifiers are those in the current public task.
