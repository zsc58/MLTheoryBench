# Reference Proof

## Proof Structure

Fix G. Represent the admissible kernels by joint probability measures retaining both source and destination coordinates. Prove strong duality by the continuous-function/measure pairing, verifying compactness, the conjugate calculation, and the Fenchel qualification explicitly. Reduce the unrestricted conditional-kernel supremum to the pointwise potential using measurable approximate selectors. Finally apply the Lipschitz estimate and optimize the scalar upper bound, including all zero cases.

## Standard Independent Background

The functional-analytic tools indexed by theory_020 are used in these forms:

1. If T is compact metric, probability measures on T form a compact space under convergence against continuous functions. The continuous dual of C(T), with its supremum norm, consists of finite signed regular Borel measures.
2. A probability measure on a product of standard Borel spaces admits a measurable probability-kernel disintegration with respect to its first marginal.
3. A measure outside a nonempty compact convex set of measures can be strictly separated from it by a continuous function.
4. For proper convex functionals F and H on a Banach space, if one is continuous at a point of the domain of the other, Fenchel duality gives
   inf_z(F(z)+H(z)) = sup_q(-F*(q)-H*(-q)).

The hypotheses of these statements are verified below. No structured-DRO theorem is used as an unproved black box. The measurable-selection step is proved directly.

## Complete Proof

### 1. Joint plans and weak duality

Fix G and put T=SxS. Write s=(x,y), r=(x',v), and define

f(s,r)=ell(r,G)=||x'-G(v)||^2.

Both f and c are continuous and bounded on the compact metric space T. Let

E={(s,r):x'=x},

and let Q be the set of probability measures q on T whose first marginal is mu* and for which q(E)=1.

Every admissible kernel gives q(ds,dr)=mu*(ds)k_s(dr) in Q. Conversely, disintegration of q in Q gives a measurable kernel k_s; since q(E)=1, k_(x,y) is supported on {x}xY for mu*-almost every (x,y), and hence equals delta_x tensor a probability measure on Y. Thus Q is exactly the set of joint measures generated by the admissible kernels. A kernel measurable for the completion of mu* has a Borel version up to a mu*-null set, which suffices for all the integrals here. The continuous identity kernel k_s=delta_s also allows null-set modifications if membership everywhere is desired.

The set Q is nonempty because it contains the identity plan q_0(ds,dr)=mu*(ds)delta_s(dr). It is convex. It is weakly closed: the first-marginal condition is preserved by convergence against continuous functions, and E is closed, so a weak limit of probabilities supported on E is supported on E. Compactness of P(T) therefore makes Q compact.

The loss integral under the induced second marginal equals the integral of f under q. Consequently,

I_epsilon(G)=max{<f,q>:q in Q, <c,q><=epsilon},

where <g,q> denotes integral g dq. This feasible set is nonempty and compact; in particular q_0 is feasible and has zero cost. The maximum is finite.

For lambda>=0 and h in C(T), call (lambda,h) dual-feasible if

<h,q>>=<f-lambda c,q> for every q in Q.

For every budget-feasible q,

<f,q><=lambda epsilon+<h,q><=lambda epsilon+sup_{p in Q}<h,p>.

Taking the primal supremum and dual infimum proves weak duality for the structured continuous-function dual.

### 2. Strong duality with all qualifications checked

Define the support function

sigma_Q(g)=sup_{q in Q}<g,q>,  g in C(T),

and define

Theta(g)=inf_{lambda>=0}{lambda epsilon+sigma_Q(g-lambda c)}.

This is the equality-constraint functional in the source: if D(g) consists of pairs (lambda,h) with lambda>=0 and <g,q>=<h+lambda c,q> for every q in Q, then every such pair has sigma_Q(h)=sigma_Q(g-lambda c), and h=g-lambda c is always an available representative.

The identity plan gives

lambda epsilon+sigma_Q(g-lambda c)>=<g,q_0>.

Choosing lambda=0 gives Theta(g)<=sigma_Q(g). Thus Theta is finite everywhere. Since sigma_Q is convex, taking convex combinations of approximate minimizing multipliers proves that Theta is convex. Also

|sigma_Q(g_1-lambda c)-sigma_Q(g_2-lambda c)|<=||g_1-g_2||_infinity.

Taking infima over lambda in both directions proves

|Theta(g_1)-Theta(g_2)|<=||g_1-g_2||_infinity.

Hence Theta is continuous on C(T), including when epsilon=0.

Let

B={g in C(T):<g,q>>=<f,q> for every q in Q}.

This is a nonempty closed convex set, and f belongs to B. If M=max_T f, the open supremum-norm ball of radius 1/2 about the constant M+1 lies in B, so B also has nonempty interior. For g in B and every lambda>=0,

sigma_Q(g-lambda c)>=sigma_Q(f-lambda c).

It follows that inf_{g in B}Theta(g)=Theta(f).

We next compute Theta* on every finite signed measure p on T. Directly from the definition, and substituting h=g-lambda c,

Theta*(p)
= sup_{g,lambda>=0}{<g,p>-lambda epsilon-sigma_Q(g-lambda c)}
= sup_h{<h,p>-sigma_Q(h)} + sup_{lambda>=0}lambda(<c,p>-epsilon).

The first supremum is zero for p in Q: every term is at most zero and h=0 attains zero. If p is not in Q, compact-convex separation supplies h with <h,p>>sigma_Q(h); scaling h makes the supremum infinite. The second supremum is zero when <c,p><=epsilon and infinite otherwise. Therefore

Theta*(p)=0 if p in Q and <c,p><=epsilon,

and Theta*(p)=+infinity otherwise.

Let delta_B be the convex indicator of B. It is proper, and Theta is finite and continuous at f in B, so the stated Fenchel duality theorem applies to Theta+delta_B. It gives

Theta(f)=inf_{g in B}Theta(g)
= sup_p{-Theta*(p)+inf_{g in B}<g,p>}.

For a feasible p, the definition of B gives inf_{g in B}<g,p>>=<f,p>, and equality follows by taking g=f. Only such p have finite Theta*. Hence

Theta(f)=max_{p in Q, <c,p><=epsilon}<f,p>=I_epsilon(G).

For completeness, the source's negative-measure observation is consistent with this calculation: if p is not positive, there is a nonnegative continuous u with <u,p><0. Then M+n u belongs to B and its integral against p tends to minus infinity. The explicit conjugate calculation already excludes all measures outside Q.

The continuous-function dual also has value Theta(f). Indeed, dual feasibility implies sigma_Q(h)>=sigma_Q(f-lambda c), while h=f-lambda c is dual-feasible for every lambda. Thus its infimum is exactly Theta(f). This closes both the structured strong-duality obligation and its reduction to a single nonnegative multiplier:

I_epsilon(G)=inf_{lambda>=0}{lambda epsilon+sup_{q in Q}<f-lambda c,q>}.

### 3. Conditional reduction and measurable choices

Fix lambda>=0 and define

F_lambda((x,y),v)=||x-G(v)||^2-lambda||y-v||^2.

On E, f-lambda c equals F_lambda(s,v). The function F_lambda is continuous on the compact product SxY. Its maximum over v exists, and

phi_lambda,G(s)=max_{v in Y}F_lambda(s,v)

is continuous: the absolute difference of its values at s and t is at most sup_v|F_lambda(s,v)-F_lambda(t,v)|, which tends to zero by uniform continuity.

For every admissible kernel,

integral F_lambda(s,v) dk_s(r)dmu*(s)<=integral phi_lambda,G(s)dmu*(s).

To prove the reverse inequality, take a countable dense sequence (v_j) in the nonempty compact metric space Y, allowing repetitions if needed. Continuity in v gives phi_lambda,G(s)=sup_j F_lambda(s,v_j). For each positive integer n, let j_n(s) be the smallest j for which

F_lambda(s,v_j)>phi_lambda,G(s)-1/n.

Such a j exists. The sets specifying j_n(s)=j are Borel, so v_n(s)=v_{j_n(s)} is measurable. The kernel

k_s^(n)=delta_x tensor delta_{v_n(s)},  s=(x,y),

is admissible without a cost constraint and satisfies

integral F_lambda(s,v_n(s))dmu*(s)>=integral phi_lambda,G(s)dmu*(s)-1/n.

Letting n tend to infinity proves

sup_{q in Q}<f-lambda c,q>=integral phi_lambda,G dmu*.

These selectors need not satisfy the original budget: they are used only in the unrestricted kernel supremum after Lagrange dualization. No dependence restriction beyond the source's measurable dependence on (x,y) is introduced.

Equivalently, h(s,r)=phi_lambda,G(s) is a continuous dual-feasible potential. The upper and lower inequalities just proved identify its objective with the exact unrestricted penalized supremum. Substitution into strong duality yields

I_epsilon(G)=inf_{lambda>=0}{lambda epsilon+integral phi_lambda,G dmu*}.

### 4. Lipschitz estimate and scalar optimization

Write

R_G=integral ||G(y)-x||^2dmu*(x,y),  L=L_G.

These quantities are finite and nonnegative. For a=G(y)-x and t=||v-y||, the triangle inequality and the Lipschitz property imply

||x-G(v)||^2-lambda t^2
<=||a||^2+2L||a||t-(lambda-L^2)t^2.

If lambda>L^2, maximizing the right side over all t>=0, a relaxation valid even when Y is not convex, gives

phi_lambda,G(x,y)<=||a||^2+L^2||a||^2/(lambda-L^2)
=lambda||a||^2/(lambda-L^2).

Indeed, for b>=0 and d>0 the maximum of 2bt-dt^2 over t>=0 is b^2/d, including b=0. Integrating and applying the dual representation gives

I_epsilon(G)<=inf_{lambda>L^2}{lambda epsilon+lambda R_G/(lambda-L^2)}.

Set delta=lambda-L^2>0. The expression becomes

R_G+L^2 epsilon+delta epsilon+L^2 R_G/delta.

If epsilon>0 and L^2 R_G>0, its infimum is attained at delta=L sqrt(R_G)/sqrt(epsilon), and the last two terms sum to 2L sqrt(epsilon R_G). If epsilon=0, take delta to infinity to obtain infimum R_G. If epsilon>0 and L^2 R_G=0, take delta down to zero to obtain infimum R_G+L^2 epsilon. These cases exhaust all possibilities and establish

I_epsilon(G)<=(sqrt(R_G)+L_G sqrt(epsilon))^2.

Taking the infimum over the same reconstruction class on both sides proves the second claim. If the class is empty, both outer infima are +infinity under the usual extended-real convention, so the asserted inequality still holds. No common Lipschitz bound, optimizer in G, or attained dual multiplier is required.
