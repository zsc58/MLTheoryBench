# Reference Proof

## Proof Structure

Use a state-action transition operator with correct kernel domains. Derive the constituent successor-measure series and the switching-suffix Bellman equation. Resolve the current phase, explicitly identify the next-policy action averaging in its continuation term, and apply backward induction to obtain the product weights.

## Complete Proof

### 1. Constituent successor kernels and convergence

Fix a measurable set \(X\subseteq\mathcal S\), and write \(P_X(s,a)=P(X\mid s,a)\). For a bounded measurable function \(F\) on \(\mathcal S\times\mathcal A\), define
\[
(\mathsf T_lF)(s,a)=\int P(dx\mid s,a)\int\pi_{z_l}(db\mid x)F(x,b).
\]
This operator maps bounded measurable state-action functions to bounded measurable state-action functions and satisfies \(\|\mathsf T_lF\|_\infty\leq\|F\|_\infty\). For a state-action-to-state kernel \(R\), the notation \(\mathsf T_lR\) means applying this operator to \(R(X\mid\cdot,\cdot)\) for each measurable \(X\).

Starting from the prescribed pair \((s,a)\), the probability of \(S_{j+1}\in X\) under the stationary policy \(\pi_{z_l}\) is \((\mathsf T_l^jP_X)(s,a)\). Indeed, the case \(j=0\) is the transition kernel, and each further transition first samples the next action from \(\pi_{z_l}\), giving the next application of \(\mathsf T_l\). Therefore
\[
m_\beta^{\pi_{z_l}}(X\mid s,a)
=(1-\beta)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\beta^j(\mathsf T_l^jP_X)(s,a).
\]
The series converges uniformly for \(0\leq\beta<1\), since every summand before weighting lies in \([0,1]\). It defines a measurable probability kernel: its terms are probability kernels, and its nonnegative weights sum to one. Thus the usual resolvent notation is justified by the convergent series, with no additional analytic assumption.

### 2. Switching-suffix equation and its resolution

Let \(\nu_{l:n}\) be the switching procedure initialized in phase \(l\), with the initial action prescribed rather than sampled. Write
\[
Q_l(X\mid s,a)=m_\gamma^{\nu_{l:n}}(X\mid s,a).
\]
These are measurable probability kernels. Their trajectory laws are generated by the given transition, action, and phase-update kernels, and their successor measures are normalized nonnegative sums of the corresponding finite-time laws.

For \(l<n\), condition on the first transition and the subsequent switch decision. The immediate successor contributes \((1-\gamma)P_X\). If the trajectory continues, it either retains phase \(l\) and draws the next action from \(\pi_{z_l}\), or advances to phase \(l+1\) and draws the next action from \(\pi_{z_{l+1}}\). Consequently,
\[
Q_l=(1-\gamma)P+\beta_l\mathsf T_lQ_l
 +(\gamma-\beta_l)\mathsf T_{l+1}Q_{l+1}.
\]
Here \(\beta_l=\gamma(1-\alpha_l)\) and \(\gamma-\beta_l=\gamma\alpha_l\). The final phase is absorbing, so
\[
Q_n=m_\gamma^{\pi_{z_n}}.
\]

For a fixed \(X\), iterate the current-phase term \(J+1\) times:
\[
Q_l(X\mid\cdot,\cdot)
=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\beta_l^j\mathsf T_l^j
 \big((1-\gamma)P_X+(\gamma-\beta_l)\mathsf T_{l+1}Q_{l+1,X}\big)
 +\beta_l^{J+1}\mathsf T_l^{J+1}Q_{l,X},
\]
where \(Q_{l,X}(s,a)=Q_l(X\mid s,a)\). The last term has supremum norm at most \(\beta_l^{J+1}\), which tends to zero because \(\beta_l\leq\gamma<1\).

To identify the continuation series, define the state-indexed function
\[
\overline Q_{l+1,X}(x)=\int\pi_{z_{l+1}}(db\mid x)Q_{l+1}(X\mid x,b).
\]
For each \(j\geq0\),
\[
(\mathsf T_l^j\mathsf T_{l+1}Q_{l+1,X})(s,a)
=\int(\mathsf T_l^jP)(dx\mid s,a)\overline Q_{l+1,X}(x).
\]
For \(j=0\), this is the definition of \(\mathsf T_{l+1}\). Applying \(\mathsf T_l\) to both sides gives the next value of \(j\), by associativity of nonnegative kernel integration. Thus the identity holds by induction. Summing it with weights \(\beta_l^j\) and using the constituent successor-kernel series gives
\[
Q_l(X\mid s,a)
=\frac{1-\gamma}{1-\beta_l}m_{\beta_l}^{\pi_{z_l}}(X\mid s,a)
+\frac{\gamma-\beta_l}{1-\beta_l}
 \int m_{\beta_l}^{\pi_{z_l}}(dx\mid s,a)
 \int\pi_{z_{l+1}}(db\mid x)Q_{l+1}(X\mid x,b).
\]
All integrations and series interchanges here involve nonnegative quantities. In particular, the continuation includes the next policy's action average explicitly; it is not an untyped composition of two state-action kernels.

### 3. Backward induction and coefficient collection

For \(l\leq k\), let \(C_{l:k}\) be the sequential kernel that first samples from \(m_{\beta_l}^{\pi_{z_l}}\) at its prescribed initial pair and then successively from \(\bar m_{\beta_j}^{\pi_{z_j}}\) for \(j=l+1,\ldots,k\). In particular, \(C_{l:l}=m_{\beta_l}^{\pi_{z_l}}\), and \(C_{1:k}\) is the kernel in the task.

Set
\[
a_k=\frac{1-\gamma}{1-\beta_k},\qquad
b_i=\frac{\gamma-\beta_i}{1-\beta_i}.
\]
We prove, for every \(l\), the kernel identity
\[
Q_l=\sum_{k=l}^{n}a_k\left(\prod_{i=l}^{k-1}b_i\right)C_{l:k}.
\]
For \(l=n\), \(\beta_n=\gamma\), so \(a_n=1\) and the identity is exactly \(Q_n=m_\gamma^{\pi_{z_n}}\).

Suppose the identity holds for \(l+1\). Substitute it into the continuation integral above at the intermediate pair \((x,b)\). For each \(k\geq l+1\), the definition of sequential composition gives
\[
\int m_{\beta_l}^{\pi_{z_l}}(dx\mid s,a)
 \int\pi_{z_{l+1}}(db\mid x)C_{l+1:k}(X\mid x,b)
=C_{l:k}(X\mid s,a).
\]
Hence
\[
Q_l=a_lC_{l:l}
 +\sum_{k=l+1}^{n}b_l a_k\left(\prod_{i=l+1}^{k-1}b_i\right)C_{l:k}
=\sum_{k=l}^{n}a_k\left(\prod_{i=l}^{k-1}b_i\right)C_{l:k}.
\]
This proves the induction. Taking \(l=1\) yields
\[
m_\gamma^\nu(\cdot\mid s,a)
=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1-\gamma}{1-\beta_k}
 \left(\prod_{i<k}\frac{\gamma-\beta_i}{1-\beta_i}\right)
 C_{1:k}(\cdot\mid s,a)
=\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_kC_{1:k}(\cdot\mid s,a).
\]
The argument applies to every measurable \(X\) and every prescribed initial pair, so this is equality of measures, not merely an almost-everywhere statement about initial conditions.

### 4. Boundary cases and obligation closure

Every denominator is positive because \(0\leq\beta_k\leq\gamma<1\). If \(\alpha_l=0\), then \(b_l=0\), so all later-phase contributions vanish without conditioning on an event of positive switching probability. If \(\alpha_l=1\), then \(\beta_l=0\), and the constituent series reduces to \(m_0^{\pi_{z_l}}=P\); the same proof applies. For \(n=1\), the induction base alone gives the target, with \(w_1=1\). The absorbing final phase is covered by \(\beta_n=\gamma\).

The theorem-critical obligations are all closed above: kernel typing and measurability, the constituent geometric series, the phase Bellman equation, convergence of its resolution, identification of the next-policy continuation composition, suffix induction with correct intermediate conditioning, exact coefficient collection, and all allowed endpoint cases. No specialized external result or unresolved paper-specific dependency is used.
