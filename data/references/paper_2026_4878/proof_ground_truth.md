# Reference Proof

## Obligations and scope

The proof establishes both directions under the same observation and per-round finite-state model. It verifies the converse's parameter regime, localized change of measure, exploration probabilities, information lower bound, and boundary-memory upper bound. For achievability it gives an executable static schedule, a finite-bit implementation for arbitrary bounded rewards, a simultaneous concentration event, the requisite invariants, and all four regret contributions. No paper-specific result is used as an unproved black box.

All constants below are universal. Increase the common constant `c` when necessary; both claims quantify existentially over that constant. Logarithms without a subscript are natural. Entropies are in bits.

## Converse

### Parameter legality and the degenerate horizon — step_006

If `T=1`, the assumed regret guarantee has right-hand side zero. Since the initial action is instance-independent, some arm has initial selection probability at most `1/K`. On the Bernoulli instance with that arm deterministically rewarding one and all others deterministically rewarding zero, expected regret is at least `1-1/K>0`. Thus no learner satisfies the premise at `T=1`, and the implication is vacuous.

Hence assume `T>=2`. Set

`alpha=ln(1.8)/512`, `beta=0.1`, `d=C log T log K`,

`n=floor(alpha T/(d^2 K))`, and `Delta=8d sqrt(K/T)`.

Let `a=(log 2)^2`. Because `C>=1` and `K,T>=2`, we have `d>=a`. Choose `c` at least

`max{160, 2/(alpha a^4), 1024/a^4, (16/(alpha beta))^2}`.

The horizon condition is `T>=c d^6 K`. Consequently

`alpha T/(d^2 K) >= alpha c d^4 >= 2`,

so `n>=alpha T/(2d^2 K)>=1`. Also

`n/T <= alpha/(d^2 K) <= alpha/(2a^2) < 1/2`.

Furthermore,

`Delta^2=64d^2K/T <=64/(c d^4)<=1/16`,

and

`d sqrt(KT)/n <= (2/alpha)d^3 K^(3/2)/sqrt(T) <= 2K/(alpha sqrt(c)) <= beta K/8`.

Thus every parameter condition used below follows from the stated premise with one universal `c`. In particular, the perturbations are legitimate Bernoulli instances and the floor in `n` causes no missing case.

### Hard family, prefix measurability, and exploration — step_001

Choose `S*` uniformly from the subsets of `[K]` of size `K/2`. Conditional on `S*=D`, arms in `D` have reward law `Ber(1/2)` and arms outside `D` have reward law `Ber(0)`. The seed is independent of this choice and of the reward streams. Let `N_j(T)` be arm `j`'s total pull count and define `Y_j=1{N_j(T)>=n}`.

We first derive the localized comparison used in the exploration argument. Consider two environments with the same non-anticipating learner and differing only in arm `j`, whose laws are `P` and `Q`, with `P` absolutely continuous with respect to `Q`. Let `G_n` be the sigma-field generated by the seed, all other arms' reward streams, and the first `n` rewards of arm `j`.

For any full-transcript event `E`, the event `E intersect {N_j(T)<=n}` belongs to `G_n`. To see this, replace all rewards of arm `j` after its `n`th reward by a fixed value and run the same learner with the same seed. The truncated transcript is `G_n`-measurable. The original and truncated histories agree until the first proposed `(n+1)`st pull of arm `j`: induction gives identical past observations and therefore identical next actions up to that point. The time of that pull is therefore identical in the two runs. On the event that it never occurs before the horizon, their full transcripts agree. This represents the restricted event using only the truncated transcript and proves its measurability. It applies in particular to `{N_j(T)<n}`.

For `E in G_n`, the likelihood ratio of the two restricted laws is

`L_n=product_{ell=1}^n (dP/dQ)(X_{j,ell})`.

This follows because the seed and unchanged streams have the same joint law in both environments and are independent of the changed prefix. Define

`chi^2(P||Q)=E_Q[((dP/dQ)-1)^2]`.

Then `E_Q[(dP/dQ)^2]=1+chi^2(P||Q)`, so independence gives

`E_1[L_n^2]=(1+chi^2(P||Q))^n`.

Cauchy-Schwarz and `1+x<=exp(x)` yield

`P_0(E)=E_1[1_E L_n] <= sqrt(P_1(E)) exp(n chi^2(P||Q)/2)`.

Fix a hard-family instance `D` and a good arm `j in D`. Change only arm `j` to `Ber(1/2+Delta)`, making it uniquely optimal. Write `E_j={N_j(T)<n}`. On this event, perturbed-instance pseudo-regret is at least `Delta(T-n)>=Delta T/2`. The uniform Bernoulli regret guarantee gives

`P_1(E_j) <= 2d sqrt(KT)/(Delta T)=1/4`.

For the changed arm,

`chi^2(Ber(1/2)||Ber(1/2+Delta)) = 4Delta^2/(1-4Delta^2) <=16Delta^2`.

The denominator is positive because `Delta<=1/4`. Applying the preceding prefix comparison,

`P_D(E_j) <= (1/2)exp(8nDelta^2)`.

The parameter definitions give

`8nDelta^2 <=512alpha=ln(1.8)`.

Hence `P_D(E_j)<=0.9` and, for every `D` and every `j in D`,

`P_D(Y_j=1)>=beta`.

### False positives, false negatives, and information — step_002

Let `X_j=1{j in S*}` and let `FP` and `FN` count the errors of the predictor `Y` on zero and one membership coordinates, respectively. Under a fixed base instance `D`, every bad-arm pull has gap `1/2`, while good-arm pulls have gap zero. Therefore

`E_D[FP] <= (1/n)sum_{j notin D} E_D[N_j(T)] = 2E_D[Reg_T]/n <=2d sqrt(KT)/n <=beta K/4`.

Averaging over the prior preserves the bound. The exploration result similarly gives

`E[FN] <= (K/2)(1-beta)`.

The average error of the threshold predictor is consequently

`P_e=(E[FP]+E[FN])/K <= beta/4+(1-beta)/2 = 1/2-beta/4`.

The false-positive term here is `beta/4`, not `beta/8`.

The prior entropy is `H(S*)=log_2 binom(K,K/2)`. For completeness, write `K=2m`. The inequality

`binom(2m,m)>=4^m/(2sqrt(m))`

holds at `m=1` and follows by induction because the ratio of consecutive binomial coefficients is `2(2m+1)/(m+1)`, which is at least `4sqrt(m/(m+1))`; squaring the latter comparison leaves the nonnegative difference one. Thus

`H(S*)>=K-(1/2)log_2(2K)`.

For each coordinate choose a minimum-error decoder of `X_j` from `Y_j` and let its error probability be `p_j`. It exists because both variables are binary. A constant decoder gives `p_j<=1/2`, and the threshold decoder gives `sum_j p_j/K<=P_e`.

If `E_j` denotes the decoder's binary error indicator, then `X_j` is determined by `(Y_j,E_j)`, and conversely the error indicator is determined by `(X_j,Y_j)`. Hence

`H(X_j|Y_j)=H(E_j|Y_j)<=H(E_j)=H_b(p_j)`,

where `H_b(p)=-p log_2 p-(1-p)log_2(1-p)`, with the endpoint values defined by continuity. Entropy's chain rule, reduction under conditioning, and concavity of `H_b` imply

`H(S*|Y)=H(X|Y)<=sum_j H(X_j|Y_j)<=K H_b(sum_j p_j/K)`.

Since `H_b` is increasing on `[0,1/2]`,

`H(S*|Y)<=K H_b(1/2-beta/4)`.

It follows that

`I(S*;Y)>=gamma_0 K-(1/2)log_2(2K)`,

where `gamma_0=1-H_b(1/2-beta/4)>0`.

### Boundary-memory capacity — step_003

Let `Mvec=(M_{t_1},...,M_{t_{B-1}})`. This tuple is an analytical transcript, not a requirement that the learner store all boundary states simultaneously.

Given `U` and `Mvec`, reconstruct the execution's boundaries recursively. The first boundary is determined by `(0,M_0,U)`, and each subsequent boundary by the preceding boundary time, its state in the tuple, and `U`. The last boundary is `T`. Once the start time and starting state of a batch are known, its complete action sequence is fixed. Summing these reconstructed action sequences determines every total pull count and therefore `Y`. Thus `Y=g(U,Mvec)` for a measurable function `g`.

Independence of `U` and `S*`, followed by conditional data processing, gives

`I(S*;Y)<=I(S*;Y,U)=I(S*;Y|U)<=I(S*;Mvec|U)`.

Each component of `Mvec` lies in a set of size at most `2^W`, even though its boundary time can be random. Consequently

`I(S*;Mvec|U)<=H(Mvec|U)<=H(Mvec)<=sum_{b=1}^{B-1}H(M_{t_b})<=(B-1)W<=BW`.

These finite-state entropy inequalities do not require the seed itself to have finite entropy. Comparing the information lower and upper bounds and dividing by `W>=1` proves the displayed converse. For sufficiently large `K`, the logarithmic subtraction is at most `gamma_0 K/2`, yielding the claimed asymptotic form. For smaller `K`, the displayed inequality remains valid, including when its numerator is nonpositive.

## Achievability

### Construction and deterministic accounting — step_004

Fix `K>=2`, `S in {1,...,K}`, and `T>=cK`. In this section only, `t_i` denotes a sample length, not an interaction batch boundary. Put

`H=T/(10K)`, `t_0=1`, `t_i=ceil(sqrt(H t_{i-1}))`,

`L=floor(log_2 log_2 H)`, `delta=T^-4`,

`A=log(2/delta)`, and `r(s)=sqrt(A/(2s))`.

Our choice `c>=160` ensures `H>=16` and `L>=2`. Start with incumbent arm one. For each outer iteration `i=1,...,L`, execute the following procedure.

1. Let `a_init` be the inherited incumbent. Pull it for `t_i` fresh rounds in one refresh batch and store its empirical summary. Initialize the current incumbent and its summary to this pair.
2. List `[K]` excluding `a_init` in increasing order and divide the list into consecutive blocks of size `S`, except possibly the final block. There are `J=ceil((K-1)/S)` blocks. Their cardinalities depend only on `K,S`, not on the identity of `a_init`.
3. At the start of a block, freeze the current incumbent and its summary as `(bar_a,bar_mu)`, and mark every challenger in that block active.
4. For `k=1,...,i`, execute one batch consisting of `t_k` pulls for each arm-position in the block. If that position's challenger was active at the start of the batch, pull it and compute a fresh `t_k`-sample summary. Otherwise pull `bar_a` in its reserved positions. After the batch, deactivate each sampled challenger whose summary is strictly less than `bar_mu-2r(t_k)`.
5. After slot `i`, if any challenger is active, select one with the greatest slot-`i` summary, breaking ties by arm label. Replace the incumbent and its stored summary by this challenger and its summary if that summary is at least `bar_mu+2r(t_i)`. Otherwise retain the incumbent. Proceed to the next block.

After outer iteration `L`, exploit the final incumbent for every remaining round in one final batch. The summaries have the explicit finite-precision implementation specified below.

All actions within a comparison batch depend on active flags fixed at its start; observations from that batch only affect subsequent batches. Refresh lengths and comparison-slot lengths are deterministic. Canonical block cardinalities make the lengths independent of the data even though the challenger identities change. Thus this is a static-grid procedure, provided its main schedule fits inside the horizon.

Its number of pre-exploitation rounds is exactly

`N_main=sum_{i=1}^L [t_i+(K-1)sum_{k=1}^i t_k]`.

To bound it, define `s_i=H^(1-2^-i)`. Induction gives `s_i<=t_i<=2s_i` for `1<=i<=L`. The lower bound follows directly from the recurrence. For the upper bound, `t_1<=sqrt(H)+1<=2sqrt(H)`; subsequently

`t_i<=sqrt(2)s_i+1<=2s_i`,

because `s_i>=sqrt(H)>=2`. Also the sequence `t_i` is nondecreasing, by monotonicity of the recurrence and `t_1>=t_0`.

From the definition of `L`,

`1/log_2 H <=2^-L<2/log_2 H`,

so `H/4<s_L<=H/2` and hence `H/4<t_L<=H`. For `i<=L-2`, the same bounds give `t_i<=H/8`, and therefore `t_{i+1}>=2t_i`. Furthermore `t_{L-1}<=H/2`, so `t_L>=sqrt(2)t_{L-1}`. Non-strict inequalities suffice at all endpoints.

It follows that

`sum_{k=1}^{L-1}t_k<=2t_{L-1}`,

`sum_{k=1}^L t_k<=(1+sqrt(2))t_L`,

and

`sum_{i=1}^L sum_{k=1}^i t_k <=4t_{L-1}+(1+sqrt(2))t_L <=(1+3sqrt(2))t_L<6t_L`.

Therefore

`T/40<Kt_L<=N_main<=K sum_{i=1}^L sum_{k=1}^i t_k<6Kt_L<=3T/5`.

The final batch has positive deterministic length `T-N_main`. We also record two estimates for the regret calculation:

`sum_{k=1}^i t_k<=(1+sqrt(2))t_i` for every `i<=L`,

and

`sqrt(H)<=t_i/sqrt(t_{i-1})<=2sqrt(H)` for every `1<=i<=L`.

For the second estimate, use the recurrence and `ceil(x)<=x+1`, together with `H,t_{i-1}>=1`.

The total batch count is

`1+sum_{i=1}^L(1+Ji)=1+L+J L(L+1)/2`.

Since `J<=2K/S`, `K/S>=1`, and `L=O(log log T)`, this is at most `C_2(K/S)(log log T)^2`. The construction also covers `S=1` and `S=K`; when `S=K`, there is one challenger block of size `K-1`.

### Finite-bit implementation and concentration — step_008

The finite-memory claim cannot be justified by treating arbitrary real-valued empirical means as exact machine words. Implement the source's permitted inverse-polynomial precision as follows. Set `q=T^3`. On observing a reward `R`, accumulate the integer `floor(qR)` rather than the real number `R`. A summary based on `s` samples is the accumulated integer divided by `qs`. Its difference from the corresponding exact empirical mean is at most `epsilon=1/q=T^-3`.

Every sum is an integer between zero and `qT=T^4`, requiring `O(log T)` bits. The algorithm retains only a constant number of incumbent and benchmark summaries, at most `S` challenger sums and flags, their arm labels, and a constant number of counters and indices. Denominators are determined by `q` and the sample length and need only `O(log T)` bits if stored. Schedule lengths can be recomputed from `K,T` and loop indices rather than stored as an array. Arm labels require `O(log K)<=O(log T)` bits. Active flags and the frozen block data are retained throughout a slot, and current sums can be updated round by round without changing its committed actions. All retained state therefore fits in `C_1 S log T` bits after every round.

We now show that this finite-precision implementation enjoys exactly the accuracy bounds needed by the comparison rules. Hoeffding's inequality states that for an average of `s` independent `[0,1]` observations of mean `mu`,

`P(|mean-mu|>u)<=2exp(-2s u^2)` for `u>0`.

Condition on the history at the start of a scheduled sampling slot. A sampled arm's identity and the number of its fresh samples are then fixed. Non-anticipation and the independent reward streams give the required conditional independence and boundedness, so Hoeffding applies conditionally to this slot even though its arm identity was selected adaptively.

Write `mean_q` for the quantized summary. Since `|mean_q-mean|<=epsilon`,

`P(|mean_q-mu|>r(s) | start history) <=2exp(-2s(r(s)-epsilon)^2)`.

Here `s<=T` and `r(s)>epsilon`. Because `2exp(-2s r(s)^2)=delta`, the last display is at most

`delta exp(4s r(s)epsilon) <=delta exp(4T^-3 sqrt(T A/2)) <=2delta`.

The final inequality holds for the present range `T>=160`: for example `A=log(2T^4)<=T` there, and the exponent is at most `2sqrt(2)/T^2<log 2`.

Union-bound over the fixed set of possible refresh and challenger sampling opportunities, treating an inactive opportunity as producing no estimate and no failure. There are

`L+(K-1)sum_{i=1}^L i`

such opportunities. Each occupies at least one distinct pre-exploitation round, so their number is at most `N_main<=T`. Conditional error bounds and the tower property thus imply that, with probability at least

`1-2Tdelta >=1-T^-2`,

every computed summary is within `r(s)` of the true mean of its arm. Denote this event by `G`. This argument neither assigns estimates to unsampled challengers nor conditions on a selected estimate as if it had been selected independently of its value.

At outer iteration `i`, an incumbent summary is either its refresh summary or a slot-`i` challenger summary installed at a block update. On `G`, every such summary, and every frozen copy, is consequently accurate within `r(t_i)`.

### Invariants and regret on the concentration event — step_005

Work pointwise on `G`. Let `mu*=max_a mu_a` and let `Delta_a=mu*-mu_a`.

**Best-arm survival.** If an optimal arm is being tested at slot `k<=i`, its summary is at least `mu*-r(t_k)`. The frozen benchmark summary is at most `mu_bar_a+r(t_i)<=mu*+r(t_k)`, because sample lengths are nondecreasing. Thus the optimal arm's summary is at least `bar_mu-2r(t_k)`, and the strict deactivation test cannot remove it.

**Incumbent monotonicity.** If a challenger replaces the incumbent at a block boundary, its true mean is at least

`summary_new-r(t_i) >=bar_mu+r(t_i)>=mu_bar_a`.

Thus true incumbent means never decrease. Refreshing changes a summary but not the arm, and each outer iteration inherits the previous final incumbent.

**Final incumbent accuracy at each outer iteration.** Let `a_i` be the final incumbent after outer iteration `i`. If a best arm was the initial incumbent, monotonicity keeps the incumbent optimal. Otherwise that best arm appears in a challenger block and survives through slot `i`. The selected active challenger has summary at least the best arm's summary. If an update occurs, the installed arm has gap at most `2r(t_i)`. If no update occurs, the best arm's summary is less than `bar_mu+2r(t_i)`, and the two summary errors show that the unchanged benchmark has gap less than `4r(t_i)`. Subsequent block updates cannot worsen the incumbent. Hence in all cases

`Delta_{a_i}<=4r(t_i)`.

**Survival at a later slot.** Suppose a challenger is actually sampled at slot `k>=2` in outer iteration `i`. It survived slot `k-1`, so its previous summary is at least `bar_mu-2r(t_{k-1})`. Accuracy gives

`mu_bar_a-mu_a <=r(t_i)+3r(t_{k-1})<=4r(t_{k-1})`.

Here `i>=k>=2`. The benchmark is no worse than the inherited incumbent `a_{i-1}`, which has gap at most `4r(t_{i-1})<=4r(t_{k-1})`. Therefore

`Delta_a<=8r(t_{k-1})`.

We treat first-slot and first-iteration costs separately, so this estimate is never used with a nonexistent previous outer-iteration guarantee.

Now decompose the actual pseudo-regret into the following disjoint groups. The estimates are pointwise on `G`, not statements requiring rewards to remain independent after conditioning on `G`.

1. **Incumbent refreshes.** The first refresh costs at most `t_1`. All later refreshes use the preceding final incumbent, so their total is at most

   `t_1+4sum_{i=2}^L t_i r(t_{i-1}) = O(L sqrt(H A))`.

2. **Actual challenger pulls.** First-slot pulls cost at most `(K-1)Lt_1=O(KL sqrt(H))`. At slots `k>=2`, the survival bound gives total regret at most

   `8K sum_{i=1}^L sum_{k=2}^i t_k r(t_{k-1}) = O(KL^2 sqrt(H A))`.

   These inequalities use `t_k/sqrt(t_{k-1})<=2sqrt(H)`.

3. **Benchmark-filled slots.** There are no filled tail slots in outer iteration one, since it has only one slot per block. For `i>=2`, every frozen benchmark is no worse than `a_{i-1}` and has gap at most `4r(t_{i-1})`. Bounding all potentially filled positions gives

   `4(K-1)sum_{i=2}^L [sum_{k=1}^i t_k] r(t_{i-1})`

   `<=4(1+sqrt(2))(K-1)sum_{i=2}^L t_i r(t_{i-1})`

   `=O(KL sqrt(H A))`.

4. **Final exploitation.** The last incumbent has gap at most `4r(t_L)`, and `t_L>H/4`. Hence this term is at most

   `4T r(t_L)=O(T sqrt(A/H))`.

Since `H=T/(10K)`, `A=log(2T^4)=O(log T)`, and `L=O(log log T)`, these four terms sum to at most a universal constant times

`sqrt(KT log T)(log log T)^2`.

The decomposition counts every scheduled pull: refresh, active-challenger, inactive-position filler, or final exploitation. Equal means and equality at comparison thresholds cause no exceptional case because deactivation is strict and the update is non-strict.

### Expectation and conclusion — step_007

Always `0<=Reg_T<=T`. Therefore

`E_nu[Reg_T] <= E_nu[Reg_T 1_G]+T P_nu(G^c)`

`<= O(sqrt(KT log T)(log log T)^2)+T^-1`.

The failure-event contribution is `T^-1`, not `T^-2`. It is absorbed by increasing the universal constant `C_3`. All concentration, memory, schedule, and regret estimates are uniform over every tuple of reward distributions supported on `[0,1]`, not just over Bernoulli distributions or mean vectors. Taking the supremum over that full class proves achievability. The constants can be chosen simultaneously with the converse constants, completing the integrated theorem.
