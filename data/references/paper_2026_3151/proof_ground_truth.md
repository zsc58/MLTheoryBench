# Reference Proof

## Contract and Proof Obligations

Write \(L=T-\delta\). The initial value \(V\sim p_T\) is independent of the Brownian motion \(B\). Every Euler chain uses this same initial value and the increments specified in the public task. The learned field is fixed before the grid resolution varies.

The obligations are: smoothness and uniform forward-marginal error; unique strong solvability, nonexplosion and uniform moments; path-space total-variation closeness; convergence in probability under the specified coupling; a quantitative rare-event recursion and its probability; finite moments at each finite resolution; and the weak-convergence and Wasserstein conclusions.

The local strong-solvability result is stated and justified in Step 2, including its application to an unbounded random initial value. The standard-independent globally Lipschitz Euler result is stated with its hypotheses in Step 4. Neither a specialized Euler-divergence theorem nor a paper-specific prior result is needed. The proof does not require access to a separate external-material record.

## Step 0: Exact Score and Initial Moments

Let \(\phi_t\) be the centered Gaussian density with variance \(t\). Independence gives
\[
p_t(x)=\int\phi_t(x-a)\,\mu_0(da).
\]
It is strictly positive. Gaussian differentiation under the integral, locally uniformly for \(t>0\), shows that \(p_t\) and its score are smooth. The smooth conditional-mean version is
\[
D_t(x)=\frac{\int a\phi_t(x-a)\,\mu_0(da)}{p_t(x)}.
\]
Differentiation gives \(s_t(x)=(D_t(x)-x)/t\). The posterior is supported in \([-M,M]\), so
\[
|D_t(x)|\le M,\qquad |s_t(x)|\le\frac{M+|x|}{\delta}.
\]
Also \(p_T\) has moments of every positive order, since \(|X_0+\sqrt T G|\le M+\sqrt T|G|\).

## Step 1: Construction and Score Error

Choose an even smooth cutoff \(\chi_R:\mathbb R\to[0,1]\), zero for \(|x|\le R\) and one for \(|x|\ge R+1\), and define
\[
\widehat s_t(x)=s_t(x)-\lambda\chi_R(x)x|x|^\alpha.
\]
Take \(R>0\). Although \(x|x|^\alpha\) need not be smooth at zero, its product with this cutoff vanishes in a neighborhood of zero and is smooth everywhere. Thus the learned field is jointly smooth on the stated time interval.

Uniformly over \(t\in[\delta,T]\),
\[
\mathbb E_{p_t}|\widehat s_t-s_t|^2
\le\lambda^2\mathbb E\left[(M+\sqrt T|G|)^{2+2\alpha}\mathbf1_{\{M+\sqrt T|G|>R\}}\right].
\]
The dominating Gaussian moment is integrable, so the right side tends to zero as \(R\to\infty\). Choose \(R\) large enough that it is at most \(\varepsilon\). The radius will also be chosen to satisfy Step 3; this does not change the bound's validity.

## Step 2: Strong Solvability and Uniform Moments

On the fully active tail,
\[
x\widehat s_t(x)\le\frac{x^2+M|x|}{\delta}-\lambda|x|^{2+\alpha}.
\]
The negative term dominates the others at infinity. Compactness on the remaining cylinder therefore gives positive constants \(C,c\), independent of \(t,x\), with
\[
x\widehat s_t(x)\le C-c|x|^{2+\alpha}.
\]
These constants may depend on the fixed data, parameters and chosen radius, but not on a grid resolution.

### Local Strong-Solvability Result

Let \(d:[0,L]\times\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) be deterministic and jointly continuous. Assume that for every \(n<\infty\) there is \(K_n<\infty\) such that
\[
|d(u,x)-d(u,y)|\le K_n|x-y|
\quad\text{for all }u\in[0,L],\quad |x|,|y|\le n.
\]
Let \(A\) be an almost surely finite real random variable independent of a standard Brownian motion \(B\). Use the usual completed, right-continuous augmentation of the filtration generated by \(A\) and \(B\); independence ensures that \(B\) is still Brownian in this filtration. Then
\[
Q_u=A+\int_0^u d(r,Q_r)\,dr+B_u
\]
has a maximal continuous adapted strong solution, pathwise unique up to its lifetime. The solution either exists throughout \([0,L]\), or has an explosion time \(\tau\le L\) with
\[
\limsup_{u\uparrow\tau}|Q_u|=\infty.
\]
No boundedness or moment assumption on \(A\) is needed for this local conclusion. If exit probabilities from intervals \((-n,n)\) before time \(L\) tend to zero, the maximal solution is nonexplosive and hence is a unique strong solution on the entire horizon.

For completeness, the additive-noise construction gives this result directly. Put \(\pi_n(x)=\max(-n,\min(x,n))\) and \(d_n(u,x)=d(u,\pi_n(x))\). Joint continuity and the stated local Lipschitz bound make \(d_n\) bounded and globally Lipschitz in space. For each continuous Brownian path and finite initial value, solve
\[
Q^n_u=A+B_u+\int_0^u d_n(r,Q^n_r)\,dr.
\]
On any interval of length \(r\) with \(rK_n<1\), successive Picard iterates are a contraction in the uniform norm on continuous functions with the prescribed starting value. Their successive differences are bounded by a geometric series, so they converge uniformly to the unique solution. A finite partition of \([0,L]\) constructs \(Q^n\) throughout the horizon. The iterates are measurable functions of the initial value and Brownian path up to the current time, so the limit is adapted and strong. This construction works for every finite initial value, including an unbounded random \(A\).

When \(|A|<n\), the solutions for radii \(n\) and \(m>n\) agree until the radius-\(n\) solution first reaches \(\{-n,n\}\): while both remain in that interval their drifts are \(d\), and the local Lipschitz inequality and Grönwall force their difference to vanish. Continuity then gives agreement at the first exit. Since \(A\) is finite almost surely, these compatible solutions can be patched over increasing radii. Exit times are defined to be zero when \(|A|\ge n\); their increasing limit is the maximal lifetime, with an empty exit set interpreted as an infinite exit time. If a path remained bounded before a purported explosion time, continuity of \(B\) and boundedness of \(d\) on the corresponding compact cylinder would give a finite continuous limit and continuation using a larger truncation. Thus failure to exist on the whole horizon entails the stated explosion alternative. The same local difference argument proves pathwise uniqueness for arbitrary solutions up to their common lifetime.

### Application and Lyapunov Estimates

Put \(b(u,x)=\widehat s_{T-u}(x)\). Steps 0 and 1 give a deterministic jointly smooth drift. In particular, for every \(n\),
\[
\sup_{0\le u\le L,\ |x|\le n}|\partial_x b(u,x)|<\infty,
\]
which verifies the local Lipschitz hypothesis uniformly in time. The initial value \(V\) is finite almost surely and independent of \(B\). The result just stated therefore gives a pathwise unique maximal strong solution to
\[
dZ_u=b(u,Z_u)\,du+dB_u,\qquad Z_0=V.
\]

For integer \(m\ge1\), set \(V_m(x)=(1+x^2)^m\). Its generator is
\[
\mathcal L_u V_m(x)
=2m(1+x^2)^{m-1}x b(u,x)
+m(1+x^2)^{m-1}
+2m(m-1)x^2(1+x^2)^{m-2}.
\]
The coercive bound implies \(\mathcal L_uV_m\le C_mV_m\), with \(C_m\ge0\), uniformly over \(u\in[0,L]\). Stop at the first exit \(\tau_n\) from \((-n,n)\), with \(\tau_n=0\) when the initial value is outside. Stopped Itô's formula and Grönwall give
\[
\sup_{u\le L}\mathbb E V_m(Z_{u\wedge\tau_n})
\le e^{C_mL}\mathbb E V_m(V).
\]
Indeed, before exit the stochastic integrand is bounded, so the stopped stochastic integral has zero expectation; the initial term is integrable by Step 0. For \(m=1\), continuity at exit, including the initial-outside case, implies
\[
\mathbb P(\tau_n\le L)\le\frac{e^{C_1L}\mathbb E V_1(V)}{1+n^2}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The explosion alternative therefore rules out explosion. Fatou's lemma for general \(m\) yields
\[
\sup_{u\le L}\mathbb E(1+Z_u^2)^m<\infty.
\]
For every real \(q>0\), choose \(2m\ge q\) and use \(|x|^q\le1+|x|^{2m}\). This proves all required uniform moments.

For the exact process, the deterministic drift \(d(u,x)=s_{T-u}(x)\) is jointly smooth by Step 0, so it satisfies the same local Lipschitz hypothesis. It uses the same finite independent initial value \(V\) and Brownian motion. The local result therefore also gives a pathwise unique maximal strong exact solution \(U\). Its envelope gives
\[
x s_{T-u}(x)\le\frac{x^2+M|x|}{\delta}\le C'(1+x^2).
\]
Substitution into the displayed generator formula gives \(\mathcal L^U_uV_m\le C'_mV_m\). The same stopped estimate and exit-probability argument show that \(U\) is a unique nonexplosive strong solution, with continuous paths on \([0,L]\) and all finite-horizon moments.

## Step 3: Path-Space Total Variation

Use the same \(V,B\) for \(U,Z\). The two drifts agree on \([-R,R]\), so pathwise uniqueness makes the solutions agree until the exact solution exits that interval. Consequently
\[
\operatorname{TV}(\mathcal L(Z_\cdot),\mathcal L(U_\cdot))
\le\mathbb P\left(\sup_{u\le L}|U_u|\ge R\right).
\]
This coupling inequality follows by bounding the difference of the probabilities of any measurable path event by the probability that the coupled paths differ.

The exact process is nonexplosive and continuous on a compact interval, so its path supremum is finite almost surely. The exit probability therefore tends to zero as \(R\to\infty\). Choose a single radius satisfying both this bound \(\le\eta\) and Step 1's error bound. Steps 1 and 2 apply to the field at this final radius.

## Step 4: Euler Convergence by Buffered Localization

For \(H>1\), let \(\pi_H(x)=\max(-H,\min(x,H))\) and \(b_H(u,x)=b(u,\pi_H(x))\). Compact smoothness of \(b\) makes \(b_H\) bounded and globally Lipschitz in space uniformly in time. It also makes it uniformly continuous in time uniformly in space. The common initial value is square-integrable and independent of \(B\).

The standard-independent Euler result used here is the following: for a deterministic bounded drift on a finite time horizon, globally Lipschitz in space uniformly in time and uniformly continuous in time uniformly in space, a square-integrable initial value independent of Brownian motion, and Euler approximations using that same initial value and Brownian increments, deterministic meshes with maximal step tending to zero give convergence in probability of the maximum grid-point error to zero. The SDE has a unique global strong solution under these hypotheses, also by Step 2's construction.

Applying this result gives
\[
\max_{0\le j\le K}|Y_{K-j}^{H,K}-Z^H_{u_j}|\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{in probability},\qquad u_j=T-t_{K-j}.
\]
Here \(Z^H\) solves the SDE with drift \(b_H\), and \(Y^{H,K}\) is its Euler chain. The drift, initial-value and coupling hypotheses have just been verified. The source geometric grids satisfy the required mesh condition, as checked in Step 5.

Fix \(\zeta>0\). Nonexplosion and continuity of \(Z\) allow \(H\) so large that
\[
\mathbb P\left(\sup_{u\le L}|Z_u|\ge H-1\right)<\zeta.
\]
On the complementary event, uniqueness gives \(Z^H=Z\) throughout the horizon. If, in addition, the truncated Euler grid error is less than one, its states all remain in \((-H,H)\). The raw and truncated recursions then agree by induction from the common initial value. For any \(r>0\), failure of raw endpoint accuracy is thus contained in the exit event, the event that the truncated grid error is at least one, or the event that its endpoint error exceeds \(r\). Hence
\[
\limsup_{K\to\infty}\mathbb P(|Y_0^{(K)}-Z_L|>r)\le\zeta.
\]
Letting \(\zeta\downarrow0\) proves the asserted convergence in probability. No grid-uniform raw Euler moment estimate is used.

## Step 5: Geometric Grid and Rare-Event Amplification

Set \(\ell=\log(T/\delta)>0\) and
\[
t_j=\delta e^{j\ell/K},\qquad h_j=t_j(e^{\ell/K}-1).
\]
There are positive constants \(a,b\), depending only on \(\delta,T\), such that
\[
\frac aK\le h_j\le\frac bK
\]
for all grid indices and all sufficiently large \(K\). Indeed \(z\le e^z-1\le ze^z\) for \(z\ge0\), and \(\delta\le t_j\le T\). These bounds also verify that the maximal step tends to zero.

For a step from \(Y_{j+1}=y\) in the active tail, on \(|\xi_j|\le1\), the triangle inequality and the exact-score envelope give
\[
|Y_j|\ge\frac{\lambda a}{K}|y|^{1+\alpha}
-\left(1+\frac b{\delta K}\right)|y|
-\frac{bM}{\delta K}-\sqrt{\frac bK}.
\]
Choose \(A\) sufficiently large. For all sufficiently large \(K\) and every \(|y|\ge AK^{1/\alpha}\), the displayed subtracted terms are bounded by a fixed multiple of \(|y|\), whereas the positive term is at least \(\lambda a A^\alpha|y|\). Increasing \(A\) therefore ensures
\[
|Y_j|\ge\frac{c_0}{K}|Y_{j+1}|^{1+\alpha},\qquad c_0=\frac{\lambda a}{4}>0.
\]
Also take \(K\) large enough that \(AK^{1/\alpha}\ge R+1\), and choose \(A\) so that \(c_0A^\alpha>1\).

The factor \(1/K\) cannot be discarded for the raw states. Define instead
\[
z_j=\frac{|Y_j|}{K^{1/\alpha}},\qquad \beta=c_0^{1/\alpha}A>1.
\]
Then \(z_j\ge c_0z_{j+1}^{1+\alpha}\). If \(z_K\ge A\), this inequality preserves \(z_j\ge A\), so every iterate remains in the fully active tail. Equivalently \(w_j=c_0^{1/\alpha}z_j\) satisfies \(w_j\ge w_{j+1}^{1+\alpha}\). After \(n\) reverse steps,
\[
z_{K-n}\ge c_0^{-1/\alpha}\beta^{(1+\alpha)^n}.
\]

Define
\[
E_K=\{V\in[AK^{1/\alpha},AK^{1/\alpha}+1]\}
\cap\bigcap_{j=0}^{K-1}\{|\xi_j|\le1\}.
\]
For \(y\ge0\), the Gaussian mixture density obeys
\[
p_T(y)\ge\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi T}}\exp\left(-\frac{(y+M)^2}{2T}\right).
\]
Integrating over the unit shell gives fixed positive constants \(C_1,C_2\) with shell probability at least \(C_1e^{-C_2K^{2/\alpha}}\). Within each grid the standardized Brownian increments are independent standard Gaussians, independent of \(V\). With \(p_\xi=\mathbb P(|N(0,1)|\le1)>0\), it follows that, for a fixed \(C_3\) and all sufficiently large \(K\),
\[
\mathbb P(E_K)\ge C_1e^{-C_2K^{2/\alpha}}p_\xi^K
\ge e^{-C_3(K^{2/\alpha}+K)}.
\]
No independence across different grid resolutions is asserted or needed.

On this event,
\[
|Y_0^{(K)}|\ge K^{1/\alpha}c_0^{-1/\alpha}\beta^{(1+\alpha)^K}.
\]
Thus for every fixed \(q>0\),
\[
\mathbb E|Y_0^{(K)}|^q
\ge e^{-C_3(K^{2/\alpha}+K)}K^{q/\alpha}c_0^{-q/\alpha}
\beta^{q(1+\alpha)^K}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Taking logarithms proves the limit: the positive term \(q\log(\beta)(1+\alpha)^K\) dominates the polynomial cost \(C_3(K^{2/\alpha}+K)\). This holds, in particular, along \(K=2^m\).

## Step 6: Finite-Grid Moments and Distributional Conclusions

The constructed field has the uniform polynomial envelope
\[
|\widehat s_t(x)|\le C_*(1+|x|^{1+\alpha}).
\]
For each fixed finite grid, every Euler step is consequently bounded in absolute value by a fixed constant times \(1+|Y_{j+1}|^{1+\alpha}+|\xi_j|\). If the previous state has moments of all positive orders, so does the next state. Starting from \(V\), finite induction proves all such moments for every finite-grid endpoint. Thus its \(W_p\) distance from every \(\nu\in\mathcal P_p\) is finite at each finite resolution.

Convergence in probability under Step 4's coupling implies weak convergence of the endpoint laws to \(\mathcal L(Z_L)\).

Finally fix \(p\ge1\) and \(\nu\in\mathcal P_p(\mathbb R)\). For any coupling \((A_K,A')\) of \(\mathcal L(Y_0^{(K)})\) and \(\nu\), Minkowski gives
\[
(\mathbb E|A_K-A'|^p)^{1/p}
\ge(\mathbb E|Y_0^{(K)}|^p)^{1/p}
-(\mathbb E_\nu|x|^p)^{1/p}.
\]
Taking the infimum over couplings and applying Step 5 with \(q=p\) proves \(W_p(\mathcal L(Y_0^{(K)}),\nu)\to\infty\).

All obligations are closed. The same fixed learned field provides the simultaneous conclusions, and no unresolved private proof dependency remains.
