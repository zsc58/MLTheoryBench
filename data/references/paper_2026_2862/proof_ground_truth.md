# Reference Proof

## Setup, Boundary Cases, and Integrability

Fix either method and let \(\mathcal F_k\) be the sigma-algebra generated by its samples before iteration \(k\). Write
\[
h_k=\nabla f(x^k),\quad r_k=\|h_k\|,\quad F_k=f(x^k)-f^*,\quad e_k=\bar g_k-h_k,\quad a=(\rho-1)/B,\quad s=\sigma/\sqrt B.
\]
The initial point and parameters are deterministic. Fresh samples make the centered oracle gradients conditionally independent given \(\mathcal F_k\), with conditional mean zero. Expanding their squared average gives
\[
\mathbb E[\|e_k\|^2\mid\mathcal F_k]
=\frac1{B^2}\sum_{i=1}^B\mathbb E[\|g(x^k,\xi_i^k)-h_k\|^2\mid\mathcal F_k]
\le ar_k^2+s^2.
\tag{1}
\]
Cross terms vanish by conditional independence and zero means. For each summand, unbiasedness gives \(\mathbb E\|g-h\|^2=\mathbb E\|g\|^2-\|h\|^2\), yielding the stated estimate.

If \(L_0=L_1=0\), the global descent inequality says \(f(y)\le f(x)+\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle\). A nonzero gradient would make the right-hand side tend to minus infinity along its negative direction, contradicting the lower bound. Thus every gradient is zero and both targets are immediate, regardless of the oracle draws. Henceforth assume at least one smoothness constant is positive.

The clipped step has length at most \(\eta c\), and the normalized step at most \(\eta\). For \(L_1>0\), the respective step-size bounds give \(\eta c\le1/(9L_1)\) and \(\eta\le1/L_1\). For \(L_1=0\), descent is global. Therefore the descent inequality applies to both methods.

All expectations used below are finite, without an extra bounded-gradient assumption. For fixed finite \(N\), the iterates lie in the deterministic closed ball of radius \(N\eta c\) or \(N\eta\) around \(x^0\). The continuous function \(f\) is bounded on that ball. When \(L_1>0\), applying descent at \(x\) in the direction \(-\nabla f(x)\) with length \(1/L_1\), and using the lower bound, gives \(\|\nabla f(x)\|\le2L_1(f(x)-f^*)+L_0/L_1\). When \(L_1=0<L_0\), the step \(-\nabla f(x)/L_0\) gives \(\|\nabla f(x)\|^2\le2L_0(f(x)-f^*)\). Thus the gradients at the iterates are uniformly bounded for this horizon; (1) and growth ensure the necessary second moments.

## Elementary Clipping Properties

The map \(C_c\) is the Euclidean projection onto the closed ball of radius \(c\). To verify nonexpansiveness directly, set \(p=C_c(u)\). For every \(z\) in the ball,
\(\langle u-p,z-p\rangle\le0\): it is immediate if \(u\) is in the ball, and otherwise follows because \(u-p\) is a nonnegative multiple of \(p\) and \(\langle p,z\rangle\le c^2\). Applying this inequality to \(p=C_c(u)\) and \(q=C_c(v)\), and adding, gives
\[
\|p-q\|^2\le\langle u-v,p-q\rangle\le\|u-v\|\|p-q\|.
\]
Hence \(\|C_c(u)-C_c(v)\|\le\|u-v\|\).

We also need a relative comparison. For \(h\) with \(r=\|h\|\), define \(\alpha=\min\{1,c/r\}\) when \(r>0\), and \(\alpha=1\) when \(r=0\). Then
\[
\|C_c(u)-\alpha h\|\le2\alpha\|u-h\|.
\tag{2}
\]
For \(r\le c\), nonexpansiveness proves the stronger bound with coefficient one. For \(r>c\), put \(t=\|u\|\) and write \(C_c(u)=\beta u\), taking \(\beta=1\) when \(u=0\). The triangle inequality gives
\[
\|C_c(u)-\alpha h\|\le\alpha\|u-h\|+|\beta-\alpha|t.
\]
If \(t\ge c\), the second term equals \(c|1-t/r|\le\alpha\|u-h\|\). If \(t<c\), it equals \((1-c/r)t\le(c/r)(r-t)\le\alpha\|u-h\|\), where the first inequality is equivalent to \(t\le c\). This proves (2), including \(u=0\).

## Clipped Method: Source Alignment and Small-Gradient Estimates

Condition on \(\mathcal F_k\), abbreviate \(h=h_k\), \(r=r_k\), \(u=\bar g_k\), and put \(v=C_c(u)\), \(q=ar^2+s^2\). The descent inequality gives
\[
F_k-F_{k+1}\ge\eta\langle h,v\rangle-\frac{\eta^2}{2}(L_0+L_1r)\|v\|^2.
\tag{3}
\]
Here \(a\le1/72\).

For the source's large-gradient event argument, suppose \(r\ge\max\{c,6\sqrt2s\}\). Let \(A=\{\|e_k\|\le3\sqrt q\}\). If \(q>0\), Markov gives \(\mathbb P(A^c\mid\mathcal F_k)\le1/9\), using denominator \(9q\). If \(q=0\), (1) implies \(e_k=0\) almost surely and the same probability bound holds without division. On \(A\),
\[
\|e_k\|\le3\sqrt{1/72+1/72}\,r=r/2,
\quad \langle h,u\rangle\ge r^2/2,
\quad \|u\|\le3r/2\le2r.
\]
Since \(r\ge c\), the clipping multiplier is at least \(c/(2r)\), so \(\langle h,v\rangle\ge cr/4\) on \(A\). On its complement, \(\langle h,v\rangle\ge-cr\). Therefore
\[
\mathbb E[\langle h,v\rangle\mid\mathcal F_k]
\ge(8/9)(cr/4)-(1/9)cr=cr/9.
\]
Also \((L_0+L_1r)c^2\le(L_0+L_1c)cr\). Substitution in (3) gives the fully derived large-gradient estimate
\[
F_k-\mathbb E[F_{k+1}\mid\mathcal F_k]\ge\eta cr/18.
\tag{4}
\]

When \(r<c\), \(C_c(h)=h\). Polarization in (3), the step-size bound, and nonexpansiveness give
\[
F_k-\mathbb E[F_{k+1}\mid\mathcal F_k]
\ge\frac\eta2r^2-\frac\eta2\mathbb E[\|v-h\|^2\mid\mathcal F_k]
\ge\frac\eta2(1-a)r^2-\frac\eta2s^2
\ge\frac\eta{18}r^2-\frac\eta2s^2.
\tag{5}
\]
In the first inequality the nonnegative term \(\eta(1-\eta(L_0+L_1r))\mathbb E\|v\|^2/2\) was discarded. Equality \(r=c\) may be assigned to either case.

## Clipped Method: Uniform Aggregation and Rate

To cover all noise regimes without assuming that the conditional objective decrement is nonnegative below a threshold, use the same polarization argument with \(C_c(h)=\alpha h\). For every \(r\ge0\),
\[
\alpha(L_0+L_1r)\le L_0+L_1c,
\qquad \eta\alpha(L_0+L_1r)\le1/9.
\]
Polarization gives the exact identity
\[
\langle h,v\rangle=\frac{\alpha^2r^2+\|v\|^2-\|v-\alpha h\|^2}{2\alpha}.
\]
Using (2) and (1) in (3), and discarding the nonnegative remaining \(\|v\|^2\) term, yields
\[
D_k:=F_k-\mathbb E[F_{k+1}\mid\mathcal F_k]
\ge\frac{\eta\alpha}{2}r_k^2-2\eta\alpha(ar_k^2+s^2)
\ge\frac\eta4\phi(r_k)-2\eta s^2,
\tag{6}
\]
where \(\phi(r)=\min\{r^2,cr\}=\alpha r^2\). The last inequality uses \(1/2-2a\ge17/36\ge1/4\) and \(\alpha\le1\). It also holds at \(r=0\).

Take full expectations in (6) before summation. Telescoping and \(F_N\ge0\) give
\[
\frac1N\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathbb E\phi(r_k)\le M:=\frac{4F_0}{\eta N}+8s^2.
\tag{7}
\]
Define the random indicators \(I_k=\mathbf1_{\{r_k\ge c\}}\). Their placement inside expectations is essential. Equation (7) bounds each of the nonnegative quantities
\[
\frac1N\sum_k\mathbb E[c r_k I_k]\le M,
\qquad
\frac1N\sum_k\mathbb E[r_k^2(1-I_k)]\le M.
\]
Cauchy-Schwarz, first for each random variable and then across the finite sum, gives
\[
\frac1N\sum_k\mathbb E[r_k(1-I_k)]
\le\sqrt{\left(\frac1N\sum_k\mathbb E[r_k^2(1-I_k)]\right)
\left(\frac1N\sum_k\mathbb E[1-I_k]\right)}\le\sqrt M.
\]
Consequently
\[
\min_{k<N}\mathbb E r_k\le\frac1N\sum_k\mathbb E r_k
\le\sqrt M+M/c
\le\sqrt{\frac{4F_0}{\eta N}}+\frac{4F_0}{\eta cN}+\sqrt8s+\frac{8s^2}{c}.
\]
Every coefficient is no larger than the corresponding coefficient in the public clipped bound: \(4\le18\), \(\sqrt8\le6\sqrt2\), and \(8\le9\). This proves part 1, including \(F_0=0\), \(s=0\), and either empty gradient regime.

## Normalized Method

Condition on \(\mathcal F_k\) and write \(u=\bar g_k\), \(h=h_k\), \(r=\|h\|\), \(t=\|u\|\), and \(e=u-h\). Local descent and \(\|R_\lambda(u)\|\le1\) give
\[
F_{k+1}-F_k\le-\eta\frac{\langle h,u\rangle}{t+\lambda}+\frac{\eta^2}{2}(L_0+L_1r).
\]
Expand \(\langle h,u\rangle=t^2+\langle h-u,u\rangle\). Since \(t^2/(t+\lambda)=t-\lambda t/(t+\lambda)\ge t-\lambda\), Cauchy-Schwarz and \(t\ge r-\|e\|\) imply
\[
F_{k+1}-F_k\le-\eta r+2\eta\|e\|+\eta\lambda+\frac{\eta^2}{2}(L_0+L_1r).
\]
The absorption inequality is written without division by \(L_0+L_1r\):
\[
\eta(L_0+L_1r)\le r+\lambda.
\]
Indeed, when \(L_0+L_1\lambda>0\), the step-size condition and
\[
(r+\lambda)(L_0+L_1\lambda)-\lambda(L_0+L_1r)=L_0r+L_1\lambda^2\ge0
\]
prove it. When both constants vanish it holds directly. Thus, even at \(L_0=r=0\),
\[
F_{k+1}-F_k\le-\frac\eta2r+2\eta\|e\|+\frac{3\eta\lambda}{2}.
\]
Conditional Cauchy-Schwarz and (1) yield \(\mathbb E[\|e\|\mid\mathcal F_k]\le\sqrt{ar^2+s^2}\le\sqrt a\,r+s\). With \(a\le1/64\),
\[
\mathbb E[F_{k+1}\mid\mathcal F_k]-F_k
\le-\frac\eta4r_k+2\eta s+\frac{3\eta\lambda}{2}.
\]
Taking full expectations and summing gives
\[
\frac\eta4\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathbb E r_k
\le F_0-\mathbb E F_N+N\left(2\eta s+\frac{3\eta\lambda}{2}\right)
\le F_0+N\left(2\eta s+\frac{3\eta\lambda}{2}\right).
\]
Divide by \(\eta N/4\) and bound the minimum by the average to prove part 2. When \(\sigma=0\), the terms containing \(s\) vanish in both public bounds; the normalized bound retains its stated \(6\lambda\) term.
