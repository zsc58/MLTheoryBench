# Reference Proof

## Scope and source corrections

This proof establishes the public theorem for its entire stated parameter range. It does not assume square summability of the stepsizes. In particular, the range 0<alpha<=1/2 is not discarded.

The source's selection of a basis column through an eigenvalue comparison does not justify treating that selected column as a uniform spherical direction. The spectral argument below avoids that inference using elementary determinant estimates on the same covariance recursion. It proves all uniform inverse moments needed here, but does not claim the source's separate sharper inverse-minimum-eigenvalue monotonicity or product inequalities. Those sharper assertions are not obligations of this task.

The source's compact-support differentiability proposition is also not applied to Gaussian measures. Every residual-field identity and descent estimate used below is derived directly. Gaussian gradient domination is proved with the normalization F=(d/2)SW_2^2 throughout.

## 1. Gaussian preservation and the trace bound

Let A_k denote the sigma-field generated by P_1,...,P_k. In formulas involving matrices below, use L_k for the update matrix to avoid confusing it with the filtration. Suppose sigma_k=N(0,Sigma_k), with Sigma_k positive definite. For a unit direction theta, put q_theta=theta^T Sigma_k theta. Its projected transport to N(0,1) is multiplication by q_theta^(-1/2). For P=P_{k+1}=[theta_1,...,theta_d], write

D_k=diag(q_{theta_1}^(-1/2),...,q_{theta_d}^(-1/2)),

L_k=(1-gamma_k)I+gamma_k P D_k P^T.

The update is linear and

Sigma_{k+1}=L_k Sigma_k L_k^T.

All eigenvalues of L_k are positive because 0<gamma_k<=1. Thus every iterate is a nondegenerate centered Gaussian.

Orthogonality and marginal transport give

integral ||T_{sigma_k,P}(x)||^2 d sigma_k(x)=sum_i integral |t_{theta_i}(theta_i^T x)|^2 d sigma_k(x)=d.

Here gamma_0=1, so Tr(Sigma_1)=d. For k>=1, convexity of the squared norm gives

Tr(Sigma_{k+1})<=(1-gamma_k)Tr(Sigma_k)+gamma_k d<=d.

Consequently, for k>=1, lambda_max(Sigma_k)<=d and lambda_min(Sigma_k)<=1. Coupling and projection inequalities also give W_2^2(sigma_k,mu)<=4d, SW_2^2(sigma_k,mu)<=4, and

0<=F(sigma_k)<=2d.

For completeness, the projection inequality follows by projecting any coupling (X,Y), averaging E_theta|theta^T(X-Y)|^2=E||X-Y||^2/d, and taking the infimum over couplings. The Wasserstein bound follows from ||X-Y||^2<=2||X||^2+2||Y||^2.

## 2. Uniform inverse-eigenvalue control without square summability

Define the deterministic number

delta=det(Sigma_0)(d/Tr(Sigma_0))^d>0.

At the first, full update, let q_i=theta_i^T Sigma_0 theta_i. Then

det(Sigma_1)=det(Sigma_0)/product_i q_i>=delta,

because sum_i q_i=Tr(Sigma_0) and AM-GM bounds their product by (Tr(Sigma_0)/d)^d.

Now fix k>=1 and a realized basis. Write q_i=theta_i^T Sigma_k theta_i and a_i=1-gamma_k+gamma_k q_i^(-1/2). Since sum_i q_i=Tr(Sigma_k)<=d, AM-GM gives product_i q_i<=1. Concavity of the scalar logarithm gives

sum_i log a_i>=-(gamma_k/2)sum_i log q_i>=0.

The a_i are precisely the eigenvalues of L_k. Hence

det(Sigma_{k+1})=det(Sigma_k)product_i a_i^2>=det(Sigma_k).

It follows that det(Sigma_k)>=delta for every k>=1. Each of the other d-1 eigenvalues is at most d, so

m_k:=lambda_min(Sigma_k)>=eta:=delta/d^{d-1}>0.

The empty product when d=1 equals one. Also delta<=1 by AM-GM applied to the eigenvalues of Sigma_0, so eta<=1. We have therefore proved, pathwise,

eta I<=Sigma_k<=d I,  k>=1.

In particular, for every positive integer p,

sup_{k>=1} E[m_k^(-p)]<=eta^(-p)<infinity.

This argument uses neither square summability nor any distributional assertion about a selected basis column.

The source's auxiliary finite spherical-moment consequence also has the following elementary corrected form. If Q is positive semidefinite with Tr(Q)<=d, then 0<=theta^T Q theta<=d for every unit theta. Thus E|theta^T Q theta-1|^r<=max(1,d-1)^r for integer r>=2. For uniform theta, E(theta^T Q theta-1)=Tr(Q)/d-1<=0. The source's particular alternating-binomial upper bound is not used.

## 3. Gaussian comparison and gradient domination

First consider centered Gaussians with positive-definite covariances Sigma and Lambda. Put

q_theta=theta^T Sigma theta, r_theta=theta^T Lambda theta,

H=d E_theta[(1-sqrt(r_theta/q_theta))theta theta^T],  G(x)=Hx.

All these integrals are finite. The matrix H is symmetric. The scalar identity and inequality

(sqrt(q)-sqrt(r))^2<=(1-sqrt(r/q))(q-r)

follow because the difference between the right and left sides is sqrt(r/q)(sqrt(q)-sqrt(r))^2. Averaging gives

F(sigma)<=(1/2)Tr(H(Sigma-Lambda)).

For any coupling (X,Y) of the two Gaussian laws, symmetry of H implies

Tr(H(Sigma-Lambda))=E< H(X+Y),X-Y>.

Cauchy-Schwarz and ||u+v||^2<=2||u||^2+2||v||^2 therefore imply

F(sigma)^2<=(1/2)E||X-Y||^2 (E||HX||^2+E||HY||^2).

Let R=lambda_max(Lambda)/lambda_min(Sigma). Since Lambda<=R Sigma in Loewner order,

E||HY||^2=Tr(H Lambda H)<=R Tr(H Sigma H)=R||G||_sigma^2.

Taking the infimum over couplings proves

F(sigma)^2<=(1/2)W_2^2(sigma,mu)(1+R)||G||_sigma^2.

This proves the general Gaussian inequality directly, without the source proof's normalization slips or its problematic nonsymmetric trace comparison.

Next suppose Sigma and Lambda are co-diagonalizable and all their eigenvalues lie in [m,M]. In their common eigenbasis write their diagonal entries as s_i^2 and t_i^2, and put Gamma=Sigma-Lambda. Coordinatewise Cauchy-Schwarz gives a lower bound sum_i(s_i-t_i)^2 on the cost of every coupling; the common-standard-Gaussian coupling attains it. Thus

W_2^2=sum_i(s_i-t_i)^2.

Also

SW_2^2=E_theta(sqrt(theta^T Sigma theta)-sqrt(theta^T Lambda theta))^2

>=E_theta(theta^T Gamma theta)^2/(4M).

The spherical identity is

E_theta(theta^T Gamma theta)^2=[2Tr(Gamma^2)+(Tr Gamma)^2]/[d(d+2)].

To verify it for d>=2, sign symmetry kills odd coordinate products. Rotational symmetry gives E theta_1^4=3 E theta_1^2 theta_2^2, and expanding (sum_i theta_i^2)^2=1 gives E theta_1^2 theta_2^2=1/[d(d+2)]. Expansion in an eigenbasis of Gamma proves the formula. For d=1 the same formula is immediate.

Finally,

Tr(Gamma^2)=sum_i(s_i-t_i)^2(s_i+t_i)^2>=4m W_2^2.

These estimates in particular imply the source's comparison

W_2^2<=C_d SW_2^2,  C_d=d(d+2)M/m.

Combining this comparison with the preceding general Gaussian inequality and cancelling F when F>0 gives

F<=(C_d/d)(1+M/m)||G||_sigma^2.

For d>=2 this implies the source's looser constant (C_d/2)(1+M/m). For d=1, direct calculation gives F=(1/2)||G||_sigma^2, so that same stated inequality holds. The case F=0 needs no cancellation.

Apply this to Sigma_k and Lambda=I. Define Mbar_k=max(1,lambda_max(Sigma_k)) and

B_k=[d(d+2)/2](Mbar_k/m_k)(1+Mbar_k/m_k).

Then

F(sigma_k)<=B_k||G_k||_{sigma_k}^2.

The fields G_k are the averaged sliced residual fields defined above. Since Mbar_k<=d and m_k>=eta, the B_k are A_k-measurable and bounded by a deterministic finite number. In particular, for every positive integer p there is c_p<infinity such that

sup_k E[B_k^p]<=c_p.

## 4. Residual identities and conditional descent

For a fixed Gaussian sigma and a sampled basis P, let G_P=Id-T_{sigma,P}, and let G=E_P G_P. Orthogonality yields

||G_P||_sigma^2=sum_i W_2^2(sigma^{theta_i},mu^{theta_i}).

Each fixed column of an independent Haar basis has the uniform spherical marginal, so

E_P G_P=G,  E_P||G_P||_sigma^2=2F(sigma).

Jensen gives ||G||_sigma^2<=2F(sigma), and expanding the square also gives

2F(sigma)=||G||_sigma^2+E_P||G_P-G||_sigma^2.

These identities concern fixed columns and do not average a column selected through an eigenvalue extremum.

Here is a direct upper Taylor inequality. For X distributed as sigma and any square-integrable map S, the pair (theta^T S(X),t_theta(theta^T X)) is a coupling of the projected updated law and the projected target. Expanding its squared cost and averaging over theta gives

F(S#sigma)<=F(sigma)+<G,S-Id>_sigma+(1/2)||S-Id||_sigma^2.

All terms are integrable by the finite second moments and bounded Gaussian transport coefficients. Set S=Id-gamma_k G_{P_{k+1}}, condition on A_k, and use independence of the next basis. This proves

E[F(sigma_{k+1})|A_k]<=(1+gamma_k^2)F(sigma_k)-gamma_k||G_k||_{sigma_k}^2.

No compact-support differentiability theorem is required.

## 5. Truncation to a deterministic recursion

Write z_k=E F(sigma_k), and fix a deterministic g_k in (0,1]. Let E_k={B_k<=g_k^(-1)}. On E_k, Gaussian gradient domination gives ||G_k||^2>=g_k F(sigma_k). Consequently,

E||G_k||^2>=g_k z_k-g_k E[F(sigma_k)1_{E_k^c}].

Using F(sigma_k)<=2d and Markov's inequality gives

E[F(sigma_k)1_{E_k^c}]<=2d c_p g_k^p.

Together with conditional descent, this implies the slightly looser source-form recursion

z_{k+1}<=(1+gamma_k^2-gamma_k g_k)z_k+4d c_p gamma_k g_k^p,

for every positive integer p. The relaxation uses g_k<=1. Every constant is independent of k.

## 6. The first rate

Fix 0<alpha<2/3 and 0<epsilon<min(alpha,1-alpha). Set

g_k=(k+1)^(-epsilon),  s=alpha+epsilon<1,

a=1-2^{-(alpha-epsilon)}>0.

For k>=1,

gamma_k^2-gamma_k g_k<=-a(k+1)^(-s).

Choose an integer p>(1-alpha)/epsilon. Put b=alpha+p epsilon>1. The recursion becomes

z_{k+1}<=(1-a/(k+1)^s)z_k+K/(k+1)^b.

We verify the required elementary scalar estimate rather than leaving a sequence-lemma citation as an obligation. Put r=b-s>0. A barrier D(k+1)^(-r) closes induction for all sufficiently large k: choose D>=2K/a, and use

(k+1)^(-r)-(k+2)^(-r)<=r(k+1)^(-r-1).

Because s<1, eventually (aD-K)(k+1)^(-r-s)>=rD(k+1)^(-r-1). Enlarging D covers the finite initial prefix. Hence z_k=O(k^(-r)). Since

r=epsilon(p-1)>1-alpha-epsilon,

we obtain

z_k<=C k^{-(1-alpha-epsilon)},  k>=1.

The choice of p and the resulting constant may depend on epsilon, as allowed.

## 7. The second rate

Fix 2/3<alpha<1. Set g_k=(k+1)^{-(1-alpha)}, so gamma_k g_k=1/(k+1). Choose an integer p>=alpha/(1-alpha); then g_k^p<=gamma_k. Using z_k<=2d to bound the gamma_k^2 z_k term gives

z_{k+1}<=(1-1/(k+1))z_k+K/(k+1)^{2alpha}.

Multiplication by k+1 gives

(k+1)z_{k+1}<=kz_k+K(k+1)^{1-2alpha}.

Summing and using 1<2alpha<2 yields

kz_k<=z_1+K sum_{j=2}^k j^{1-2alpha}<=C k^{2-2alpha}.

Therefore

z_k<=C k^{-(2alpha-1)},  k>=1.

All constants depend only on the permitted fixed parameters. The case d=1 is included; indeed the first full update already equals the target. No claim is required at alpha=2/3. Every theorem-critical obligation of the public task is closed.
