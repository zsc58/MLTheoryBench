# Scoring Guide

- **0:** No correct idea or relevant valid method.
- **1-2:** Nontrivial relevant ideas, but no complete correct proof step or useful conclusion.
- **3-7:** Some correct core steps or conclusions, but important steps are missing.
- **8-9:** Complete and essentially correct, with only readily repairable errors or omitted cases.
- **10:** Fully correct.

# Core Steps

1. **Moment-matching construction (3 points).** Establishes dimension-independent Gaussian polynomial control in the correct scalar or two-dimensional domain, proves suitable anti-concentration and uniform positive depth, and realizes the required parameter moment vector by an actual probability law. Quantifies the planted weight at least a_0(k+1)^{-B k^2}, verifies every mixed moment, and handles odd-coordinate symmetry and k=1. A valid geometric route obtains strict directional witnesses, selects finitely many by compactness, and proves that their finite convex hull contains a neighborhood of the target. An unspecified interior perturbation without a quantitative planted weight is incomplete; membership only in a closed convex hull does not establish exact finite realization.
2. **Low-degree limitation (2 points).** Subtracts f(0), reduces the point-mass mixture to the univariate scale polynomial with zero constant term, proves the single-sample O(alpha sqrt(k)) bound, and gives a complete independent-product tensorization argument yielding (1+C^2 alpha^2 k)^m-1. Does not assume f(0)=0 and E_Q f=0 simultaneously. Justifies the constant-advantage regime and the finite-moment and polynomial-space hypotheses.
3. **Null soundness (2 points).** Proves a simultaneous spherical incoherence event with failure at most m^2 exp(-c n), uses a deterministic relative-perturbation bound valid for arbitrarily correlated allowed perturbations, and obtains a strict pair singular-value lower bound greater than 1/2. A logarithmic-coherence estimate with only polynomial confidence does not by itself establish the exponential null guarantee.
4. **Planted completeness and complexity (2 points).** Uses independent rerandomization and binomial concentration to obtain two retained line samples with error at most exp(-c m alpha(1-p)). Handles zero observations according to the public definition, bounds the normalized perturbations for nonzero samples, and proves acceptance. Reports O(m^2) pair checks and O(nm^2) arithmetic runtime, or a correctly justified improvement.
5. **Integrated conclusion (1 point).** Combines the three assertions with universal constant dependencies and the stated domains. Keeps the two planted laws and the independent-product and corrupted-observation experiments distinct. Gives separate null and planted error guarantees and their common lower bound without claiming exponential-in-n planted completeness from a constant expected planted count.

# Evaluator Notes

Accept a complete valid alternative proof. Do not require the reference proof's incidental presentation choices or its particular small-ball parameters. No quantitative bound on the number of convex witnesses or on a random-polytope sample size is part of the public theorem. The reference proof establishes the needed finite convex realization directly and does not invoke the specialized quantitative random-polytope theorem. Merely citing that specialized result does not close this obligation under the audit contract; a derivation of the needed consequence or a reduction to admissible standard background is required. The public task provides no intermediate proof identities or anti-concentration hints.

Treat omitted numerical constants as acceptable only when universality and the parameter regime are correctly justified. A final assertion of k^{-O(k^2)} must be supported by a quantitative construction and interpreted consistently at small k. Central symmetry alone does not justify vanishing of every mixed monomial with an odd coordinate exponent. A source-specific prior result or an unreconstructed nonstandard external dependency is not a substitute for establishing a theorem-critical obligation. A standard independent external theorem may be used when its relevant statement and hypotheses are correctly supplied and checked.

The algorithm's planted error is controlled by m alpha(1-p), not by n alone. Do not penalize a solution for pointing out that constant m alpha(1-p) provides fixed confidence rather than asymptotically vanishing error. The null bound can be vacuous when m is exponentially large; no additional upper restriction on m is required by the stated finite-sample inequality. Undefined normalization at zero, missing quantitative convex realization, unjustified tensorization, or incorrect robustness/confidence arguments are major gaps when they affect a claimed conclusion. There are no unresolved theorem-critical private reference-proof obligations.
