# Problem

## Sampling and operator notation

Let \(d,n,m\) be positive integers. Let \(\mathcal M\) be the space of Borel probability measures on \(\mathbb R^d\), equipped with the Borel sigma-algebra generated by weak convergence. A latent random measure \(\mu\) has law \(P\), and \(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_n\) are independent copies. Conditional on these measures, all observations \(X_{ij}\), \(1\le i\le n\), \(1\le j\le m\), are independent, with \(X_{ij}\) having conditional law \(\mu_i\). Only the array \((X_{ij})\) is observed. Write
\[
\widehat\mu_i=\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m\delta_{X_{ij}}.
\]
For a single latent measure, \(\widehat\mu^{(\ell)}\) denotes its empirical measure from \(\ell\) conditionally independent observations.

Let \(\mathcal E\subseteq\mathcal M\) be a measurable domain and let \(\Phi:\mathcal E\to H\) be a fixed measurable map into a real separable Hilbert space. Whenever \(\mathbb E_P\|\Phi(\mu)\|<\infty\), define the Bochner mean and the population-centered map by
\[
a_P=\mathbb E_P\Phi(\mu),\qquad
\bar\Phi_P(\nu)=\Phi(\nu)-a_P.
\]
Define the population covariance by
\[
\Sigma_P=\mathbb E_P[\bar\Phi_P(\mu)\otimes\bar\Phi_P(\mu)].
\]
Here \((u\otimes v)h=\langle u,h\rangle v\). Write \(\mathcal S_2(H)\) for the Hilbert space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators on \(H\), with
\[
\|A\|_{HS}^2=\sum_j\|Ae_j\|^2
\]
for an orthonormal basis \((e_j)\) of \(H\).

## 1. Covariance upper bound

For this part, assume that \(\mu\) and \(\widehat\mu^{(\ell)}\) belong to \(\mathcal E\) almost surely for every positive integer \(\ell\). Work with a fixed centered feature map: \(\mathbb E_P\Phi(\mu)=0\), so \(\bar\Phi_P=\Phi\). Assume there is one \(R>0\) such that
\[
\mathbb E_P\|\Phi(\mu)\|^4\le R,
\qquad
\mathbb E_P\|\Phi(\widehat\mu^{(\ell)})\|^4\le R
\quad\text{for every positive integer }\ell.
\]
The given feature map is used in the covariance construction; the population center is not replaced by a sample mean. Define
\[
\widehat\Sigma=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\bar\Phi_P(\widehat\mu_i)\otimes\bar\Phi_P(\widehat\mu_i),
\qquad
r_m(\Phi)=\left(\mathbb E_P\|\Phi(\mu)-\Phi(\widehat\mu^{(m)})\|^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]
Expectations include both the latent measure and the conditional sampling.

Prove that
\[
\mathbb E_P\|\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma_P\|_{HS}
\le R^{1/2}n^{-1/2}+2R^{1/4}r_m(\Phi).
\]

## 2. Minimax lower bound

For \(p=1,2\), let
\[
\mathcal M_p=\left\{\nu\in\mathcal M:\int\|x\|^p\,d\nu(x)<\infty\right\},
\]
with their inherited Borel sigma-algebras. Use the following measurable maps on their indicated domains.

- For \(\Phi_A\), take \(\mathcal E_A=\mathcal M_1\), \(H_A=\mathbb R^d\), and
  \[
  \Phi_A(\nu)=\int x\,d\nu(x).
  \]
  In particular, \(\Phi_A(N(b,S))=b\).

- For \(\Phi_B\), take \(\mathcal E_B=\mathcal M_2\), \(\gamma_d=N(0,I_d)\), and \(H_B=L^2(\gamma_d;\mathbb R^d)\). Let \(T_\nu\) be the \(\gamma_d\)-almost-everywhere unique optimal transport map from \(\gamma_d\) to \(\nu\) for squared Euclidean cost: it minimizes
  \[
  \int\|T(x)-x\|^2\,d\gamma_d(x)
  \]
  over measurable maps with \(T_\#\gamma_d=\nu\), where \(T_\#\gamma_d(B)=\gamma_d(T^{-1}(B))\). Set \(\Phi_B(\nu)=T_\nu-\operatorname{Id}\). For a Gaussian measure with diagonal positive covariance matrix \(S\),
  \[
  [\Phi_B(N(b,S))](x)=(S^{1/2}-I_d)x+b,
  \]
  where \(S^{1/2}\) is the positive square root.

- For \(\Phi_C\), take \(\mathcal E_C=\mathcal M_2\) and
  \[
  H_C=L^2(\sigma_{d-1}\otimes\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]),
  \]
  where \(\sigma_{d-1}\) is the uniform probability measure on the unit sphere. For \(0<t<1\), set
  \[
  [\Phi_C(\nu)](\theta,t)
  =\inf\{z\in\mathbb R:\nu(\{x:\theta^\top x\le z\})\ge t\}.
  \]
  Values at \(t=0,1\) can be assigned arbitrarily since these are \(L^2\) equivalence classes. For \(s\ge0\),
  \[
  [\Phi_C(N(b,s^2I_d))](\theta,t)
  =\theta^\top b+\sqrt2\,s\,\operatorname{erf}^{-1}(2t-1),
  \]
  where \(\operatorname{erf}(z)=\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^z e^{-u^2}\,du\).

For each of these specified maps, define
\[
\mathcal D_\Phi(R)=
\left\{P:\ P\text{ is a probability law on }\mathcal E_\Phi,
\quad\mathbb E_P\|\Phi(\mu)\|^4\le R\right\}.
\]
The moment in this class is that of the specified map \(\Phi\), while the covariance parameter \(\Sigma_P\) uses \(\bar\Phi_P\). The fixed-zero-mean convention and the empirical-measure moment assumption of part 1 are not additional restrictions on this class.

An estimator \(T\) is a Borel measurable function from \((\mathbb R^d)^{n\times m}\) into \(\mathcal S_2(H_\Phi)\). It may depend on the specified map and on \(d,n,m,R\), but not on the unknown law \(P\), its latent measures, or its population feature mean.

For each \(\Phi\in\{\Phi_A,\Phi_B,\Phi_C\}\), find an explicit constant \(C_{\Phi,d}>0\), depending only on the map and \(d\), and prove that for all positive integers \(n,m\),
\[
\inf_T\sup_{P\in\mathcal D_\Phi(R)}
\mathbb E_P\|T((X_{ij}))-\Sigma_P\|_{HS}
\ge C_{\Phi,d}n^{-1/2},
\]
whenever \(R\ge4(d^2+2d)\) for \(\Phi_A,\Phi_B\), and whenever \(R\ge17\) for \(\Phi_C\).
