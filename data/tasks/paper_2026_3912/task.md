# Problem

## Setup

Let $n,d\ge1$ be integers. Let $\alpha\in(0,1)$ and $\alpha_\tau,\alpha_m\in[0,1]$ satisfy $\alpha+\alpha_\tau+\alpha_m\le1$. Let $\Pi$ be a nonempty finite catalog of deployable policies, and let $\mathcal J$ be a finite set of constraint indices, possibly empty. A world is $u=(z,\tau,m)$, where $z\in\mathbb R^d$ and the response and member-experience variables $\tau,m$ range over fixed measurable spaces. Each policy has a real-valued measurable value function $V(\pi;u)$ and real-valued measurable constraint functions $g_j(\pi;u)$, $j\in\mathcal J$. Feasibility means $g_j\le0$ for every $j$. All random quantities below are measurable.

For a nonnegative nominal dual-price vector $\lambda$, define
\[
L(\pi;u,\lambda)=V(\pi;u)-\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\lambda_jg_j(\pi;u).
\]
For every nominal pair $(u_0,\lambda_0)$ used below, where $u_0=(z_0,\tau_0,m_0)$, assume that $z\mapsto L(\pi;(z,\tau_0,m_0),\lambda_0)$ is affine on $\mathbb R^d$ for every $\pi\in\Pi$. Denote its coefficient by $w_\pi(u_0,\lambda_0)\in\mathbb R^d$. The component gradients introduced below are assumed to exist as finite vectors at the nominal points where they are evaluated.

For a given nominal pair $(u_0,\lambda_0)$, abbreviate $w_\pi=w_\pi(u_0,\lambda_0)$ and define
\[
\Phi(e)=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}|\langle w_\pi,e\rangle|,
\qquad
W=\operatorname{conv}\bigl(\{w_\pi:\pi\in\Pi\}\cup\{-w_\pi:\pi\in\Pi\}\bigr).
\]

### Calibration

Let $\mathcal H$ be the sigma-field generated by the training data and the fitted forecasting and nominal dual-price computation rules. These rules are fixed before calibration. For each calibration block $b=1,\ldots,n$ and the next planning block $b=*$, let
\[
u_{0,b}=(z_{0,b},\tau_{0,b},m_{0,b}),\qquad \lambda_{0,b}\ge0
\]
be the nominal outputs of those rules, computed from information available before that block's realized future is observed. These nominal outputs may vary across blocks. Let $z_b\in\mathbb R^d$ be the realized inventory vector, and put
\[
e_b=z_b-z_{0,b},\qquad w_{\pi,b}=w_\pi(u_{0,b},\lambda_{0,b}).
\]
Define
\[
S_b=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}|\langle w_{\pi,b},e_b\rangle|\quad (b=1,\ldots,n),
\qquad
S_{\mathrm{next}}=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}|\langle w_{\pi,*},e_*\rangle|.
\]
Assume that $S_1,\ldots,S_n,S_{\mathrm{next}}$ are exchangeable conditional on $\mathcal H$.

Let $r=\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$. Define $\hat q$ as the $r$th order statistic of $S_1,\ldots,S_n$ when $r\le n$, and as $+\infty$ when $r=n+1$.

For component calibration, fix a component miscoverage level $\alpha_{\mathrm{comp}}\in(0,1)$. At the nominal world for calibration block $b$, define
\[
a_{\pi,V,b}=\nabla_zV(\pi;u_{0,b}),\qquad
 a_{\pi,g_j,b}=\nabla_zg_j(\pi;u_{0,b}),
\]
\[
S_b^V=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}|\langle a_{\pi,V,b},e_b\rangle|,
\qquad
S_b^{g_j}=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}|\langle a_{\pi,g_j,b},e_b\rangle|.
\]
Let $r_{\mathrm{comp}}=\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha_{\mathrm{comp}})\rceil$. Define $\hat q^V$ and $\hat q^{g_j}$ as the $r_{\mathrm{comp}}$th order statistics of their respective $n$ component calibration scores when $r_{\mathrm{comp}}\le n$, and as $+\infty$ when $r_{\mathrm{comp}}=n+1$.

### Coverage and selection

For the next planning block, use $u_0=u_{0,*}$ and $\lambda_0=\lambda_{0,*}$, and let $u_*=(z_*,\tau_*,m_*)$ be its realized world. Define the nominal predictions by
\[
\hat V(\pi)=V(\pi;u_0),\qquad \hat g_j(\pi)=g_j(\pi;u_0).
\]
Let $\rho_V(\pi),\rho_{g,j}(\pi)\in[0,\infty)$ be finite response/member-experience radii available at selection time, and set
\[
\Delta_V(\pi)=\hat q^V+\rho_V(\pi),\qquad
\Delta_{g,j}(\pi)=\hat q^{g_j}+\rho_{g,j}(\pi).
\]
Assume that the event
\[
E=\bigl\{|V(\pi;u_*)-\hat V(\pi)|\le\Delta_V(\pi)\text{ for every }\pi\in\Pi\bigr\}
\cap
\bigl\{g_j(\pi;u_*)\le\hat g_j(\pi)+\Delta_{g,j}(\pi)\text{ for every }\pi\in\Pi,\ j\in\mathcal J\bigr\}
\]
has probability at least $1-\alpha-\alpha_\tau-\alpha_m$. In particular, the value bound is required even when $\mathcal J$ is empty.

Define the robust-feasible catalog
\[
\mathcal F=\{\pi\in\Pi:\hat g_j(\pi)+\Delta_{g,j}(\pi)\le0\text{ for every }j\in\mathcal J\}.
\]
If $\mathcal F$ is empty, there is no robust-selector policy output. Otherwise, for a finite optimization tolerance $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}\ge0$, a robust-selector output is any measurable choice $\hat\pi\in\mathcal F$, made from information available before $u_*$ is observed, satisfying
\[
\hat V(\hat\pi)-\Delta_V(\hat\pi)
\ge
\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}\{\hat V(\pi)-\Delta_V(\pi)\}-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}.
\]
The inequalities defining $\mathcal F$ and approximate optimization use the extended-real order when a conformal radius is infinite. In particular, a finite value minus $+\infty$ is $-\infty$, and $-\infty-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}=-\infty$.

## Task

Prove the following integrated theorem.

1. For every fixed nominal pair $(u_0,\lambda_0)$ and every $e\in\mathbb R^d$,
\[
\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\left|L(\pi;(z_0+e,\tau_0,m_0),\lambda_0)-L(\pi;u_0,\lambda_0)\right|=\Phi(e).
\]
Moreover, $\Phi$ is the support function
\[
\sigma_W(e):=\sup_{v\in W}\langle v,e\rangle
\]
of $W$. Among all finite-valued lower-semicontinuous sublinear functions $B:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R$ satisfying
\[
\left|L(\pi;(z_0+e,\tau_0,m_0),\lambda_0)-L(\pi;u_0,\lambda_0)\right|\le B(e)
\quad\text{for every }\pi\in\Pi\text{ and }e\in\mathbb R^d,
\]
$\Phi$ is pointwise smallest. Equality $B=\Phi$ for all $e$ holds exactly when
\[
\{v\in\mathbb R^d:\langle v,e\rangle\le B(e)\text{ for every }e\in\mathbb R^d\}=W.
\]

2. The split-conformal radius satisfies
\[
\Pr\{S_{\mathrm{next}}\le\hat q\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]

3. On $E$, every robust-selector output is feasible in the realized world. Whenever $\mathcal F\ne\varnothing$, let
\[
\pi_*^{\mathrm{rob}}\in\arg\max_{\pi\in\mathcal F}V(\pi;u_*)
\]
be a realized-value comparison policy. Then, on $E$, every $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}$-optimal robust-selector output obeys
\[
V(\pi_*^{\mathrm{rob}};u_*)-V(\hat\pi;u_*)
\le 2\sup_{\pi\in\Pi}\Delta_V(\pi)+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}.
\]
The right-hand side is interpreted in the extended reals. The common event on which these feasibility and regret statements hold has probability at least $1-\alpha-\alpha_\tau-\alpha_m$.
