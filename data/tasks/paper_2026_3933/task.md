# Problem

## Setup

Let n>=1, let X be a standard-Borel space, and let Z_i=(X_i,Y_i), i=1,...,n+1, take values in Z=X times R under an arbitrary joint law. Assume the realized pairs are pairwise distinct almost surely. Write E for their unordered multiset.

Fix measurable, permutation-equivariant true weight functions w_i(z_1,...,z_{n+1})>=0 whose sum is one. For almost every distinct-atom multiset e and any enumeration z_1,...,z_{n+1} of e, w_i(z_1,...,z_{n+1}) is the conditional probability that Z_{n+1}=z_i given E=e. Permutation equivariance means that reordering the tuple reorders the weights with their atoms. Fix versions on all tuples, including null multisets and repeated-atom tuples, that remain measurable, permutation-equivariant, nonnegative, and sum to one. Those versions are used for candidate responses as well. Write w_i^*=w_i(Z_1,...,Z_{n+1}).

Let M_1 be the space of finite-support probability laws on Z. A fixed predictive procedure G is a measurable map from M_1 times X to the space D of CDFs on R, where D has the sigma-field generated by its evaluation maps. Write G[mu,x](y) for its value at threshold y. For a positive finite measure nu, write P_nu=nu/nu(Z).

For a finite-support nonnegative measure mu, x in X, and c>0, define

Pi_l[mu,x;c](y)=inf_{y' in R} G[P_{mu+c delta_(x,y')},x](y),

Pi_u[mu,x;c](y)=sup_{y' in R} G[P_{mu+c delta_(x,y')},x](y).

The band Pi[mu,x;c] is the set of all CDFs H satisfying Pi_l[mu,x;c](y)<=H(y)<=Pi_u[mu,x;c](y) for every real y. The same pointwise convention defines every band below; the envelopes themselves need not be CDFs.

### Calibration terminology

For CDFs H and K, write H<=st K when H(t)>=K(t) for every real t. A Borel upper set U in D satisfies: H in U and H<=st K imply K in U. For a random CDF F, define

A(F)={F^(-1)(U): U is a Borel upper set in D},

with events interpreted modulo null sets. This is a sigma-lattice: it contains the empty and full events and is closed under countable unions and intersections, but need not be closed under complements. Write sigma(F) for the ordinary sigma-field generated by F.

For a sigma-lattice A and bounded real random variable T, E[T|A] denotes its sigma-lattice conditional expectation: the unique, up to almost-sure equality, minimizer of E[(T-H)^2] over square-integrable real H satisfying {H>t} in A modulo null sets for every real t. Conditioning on sigma(F), in contrast, means ordinary conditional expectation.

For each deterministic nu in M_1, draw (X,Y) with law nu and put F_nu=G[nu,X]. The following are separate properties of G, each required to hold for every such nu:

- **In-sample probabilistic calibration:** for every u in (0,1),
  P_nu(F_nu(Y)<=u)<=u<=P_nu(F_nu(Y-)<u),
  where H(y-)=lim_{t increasing to y} H(t), and P_nu here denotes probability in the experiment (X,Y)~nu.
- **In-sample isotonic calibration:** for every y in R,
  E_nu[1{Y>y}|A(F_nu)]=1-F_nu(y) almost surely.
- **In-sample auto-calibration:** for every y in R,
  E_nu[1{Y>y}|sigma(F_nu)]=1-F_nu(y) almost surely.

### Known-weight band

For a candidate response y' in R, let

w^*(y')=(w_i(Z_1,...,Z_n,(X_{n+1},y')))_{i=1}^{n+1},

tilde_mu^{y'}=sum_{i=1}^n w_i^*(y') delta_{Z_i}+w_{n+1}^*(y') delta_{(X_{n+1},y')}.

Define

L*(y)=inf_{y' in R} G[P_{tilde_mu^{y'}},X_{n+1}](y),

U*(y)=sup_{y' in R} G[P_{tilde_mu^{y'}},X_{n+1}](y),

and let Pi*[X_{n+1}] be the band between L* and U*. Set

F*=G[P_{tilde_mu^{Y_{n+1}}},X_{n+1}].

### Weight-uncertainty band

For the confidence assertions only, additionally assume that w^*(y') does not depend on y' in R, almost surely; denote this vector by w^*.

Fix alpha in (0,1). Let hat w_i be finite nonnegative measurable estimated weights, and let L_i,U_i be measurable bounds with 0<=L_i<=U_i<infinity. They may depend on the full sequence and alpha. Their realized values are held fixed throughout the optimizations below. Define

W_alpha={w in [0,infinity)^{n+1}: L_i hat w_i<=w_i<=U_i hat w_i for every i, and sum_i w_i>0}.

For w in W_alpha and y' in R, set

mu_w^{y'}=sum_{i=1}^n w_i delta_{Z_i}+w_{n+1}delta_{(X_{n+1},y')}.

Define

L_w(y)=inf_{y' in R} G[P_{mu_w^{y'}},X_{n+1}](y),

U_w(y)=sup_{y' in R} G[P_{mu_w^{y'}},X_{n+1}](y),

L^{WU,alpha}(y)=inf_{w in W_alpha} L_w(y),

U^{WU,alpha}(y)=sup_{w in W_alpha} U_w(y).

Let Pi^{WU,alpha} be the band between these last two envelopes. Zero coordinates, including w_{n+1}=0, are allowed because the total mass is positive. If W_alpha is empty, use inf(empty)=+infinity and sup(empty)=-infinity, so Pi^{WU,alpha} is empty.

Assume the band-membership events {F* in Pi*[X_{n+1}]} and {F* in Pi^{WU,alpha}} are measurable in the completed probability space.

For the finite-sample confidence assertion assume P(w^* in W_alpha)>=1-alpha for each n. Alternatively, for the asymptotic assertion assume

liminf_{n->infinity} P(w^* in W_alpha)>=1-alpha.

In the asymptotic assertion the setup and applicable assumptions hold at each n, and alpha is fixed.

## Task

1. Prove F* in Pi*[X_{n+1}] almost surely.
2. Prove each of the following implications under its respective in-sample calibration assumption on G:
   - For every u in (0,1), P(F*(Y_{n+1})<=u)<=u<=P(F*(Y_{n+1}-)<u).
   - For every y in R, E[1{Y_{n+1}>y}|A(F*)]=1-F*(y) almost surely.
   - For every y in R, E[1{Y_{n+1}>y}|sigma(F*)]=1-F*(y) almost surely.
3. Under the additional assumptions for the uncertainty band, prove P(F* in Pi^{WU,alpha})>=1-alpha in the finite-sample regime and liminf_{n->infinity}P(F* in Pi^{WU,alpha})>=1-alpha in the asymptotic regime.
