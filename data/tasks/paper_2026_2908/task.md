# Problem

## Setup

Let X=(X_1,...,X_d) be a finite vector of covariates, with index set V={1,...,d}, and let Y be real-valued. Each covariate has a standard Borel state space, equipped with its Borel sigma-field; ordinary real-valued and discrete covariates are included. Fix one augmented deployment structural causal model over (X,Y,E), with an acyclic DAG G. The environment node E is a source and there is no edge E->Y. Its nonempty set of environment values is denoted by \(\mathcal E\). For each \(e\in\mathcal E\), let \(P_e\) be the law of (X,Y) obtained by fixing E=e in this same model. Thus the laws are generated by a common environment-indexed SCM, not by unrelated models.

Structural assignments are measurable and use independent exogenous noises. The environment may change assignments only at children of E; all other assignments and their noise laws are unchanged. Any additional parent edges used by these interventions are included in G, which remains acyclic. Assume the global Markov property for the common augmented model and for the SCMs obtained by fixing the environment and performing the specified replacements: d-separation implies the corresponding conditional independence.

All graph sets below are computed in G. For a node, \(\operatorname{PA}_G\), \(\operatorname{CH}_G\), and \(\operatorname{DE}_G\) denote its parents, children, and descendants, with descendants excluding the node itself. For a set of nodes these operators denote unions of the nodewise sets. For \(T\subseteq V\), write \(X_T\) for the corresponding subvector; \(X_\varnothing\) is the constant empty vector. Define
\[
C=\operatorname{CH}_G(Y),\qquad
C_{\mathrm{int}}=C\cap\operatorname{CH}_G(E),\qquad
D=C_{\mathrm{int}}\cup\operatorname{DE}_G(C_{\mathrm{int}}),\qquad
D^c=V\setminus D,
\]
and the covariate-index set
\[
S=\left[\operatorname{PA}_G(Y)\cup(C\setminus D)
\cup\operatorname{PA}_G(C\setminus D)\right]\cap V.
\]
In particular, only covariate parents of retained children are included in S, not Y itself.

A subset \(T\subseteq V\) is invariant when there is one measurable conditional probability kernel for the law of Y given \(X_T\) that is a version of this conditional law under every \(P_e\). Conditional laws and means are interpreted up to the corresponding environment's covariate-marginal null sets. For invariant T define the measurable predictor
\[
f_T(x)=\mathbb E[Y\mid X_T=x_T]
\]
using that common kernel. Write \(f_S\) for the stable-blanket conditional-mean predictor associated with the set S above.

Let \(\ell:\mathbb R\times\mathbb R\to[0,\infty]\) be measurable and strictly consistent for the mean on its finite-expectation domain: for every law F with \(\mathbb E_F|Y|<\infty\) and every \(q\in\mathbb R\) for which both expected losses are finite,
\[
\mathbb E_F[\ell(Y,\mathbb E_FY)]
\leq \mathbb E_F[\ell(Y,q)],
\]
with equality if and only if \(q=\mathbb E_FY\).

For every measurable real-valued predictor f define its nonnegative, possibly infinite risk by
\[
R_e(f)=\mathbb E_e[\ell(Y,f(X))].
\]
Assume, for every \(e\in\mathcal E\),
\[
\mathbb E_e|Y|<\infty,\qquad
R_e(f_S)<\infty,\qquad
\mathbb E_e\!\left[\ell\!\left(Y,\mathbb E_e[Y\mid X]\right)\right]<\infty.
\]
Predictor comparisons and suprema use the extended nonnegative order when a competing predictor has infinite risk. No supremum is assumed to be attained.

At least one of the following conditions holds:

1. **Condition I.** For every \(e\in\mathcal E\) there exists \(e'\in\mathcal E\) such that \(X_S\) has the same marginal law under \(P_e\) and \(P_{e'}\), and under \(P_{e'}\), Y is independent of \(X_D\) conditional on \(X_{D^c}\).
2. **Condition II.** The graph satisfies
\[
C\cap\operatorname{DE}_G(C_{\mathrm{int}})\subseteq C_{\mathrm{int}},
\]
and for every \(e\in\mathcal E\) there exists \(e'\in\mathcal E\) obtained from e by replacing each assignment at \(j\in C_{\mathrm{int}}\) with \(X_j=\widetilde\varepsilon_j\). The replacement noises are mutually independent and independent of E and all other exogenous noises; constant replacements are allowed. Every other structural assignment and its noise law remains unchanged from e. This independence concerns the replacement mechanisms, not independence from descendants generated afterward.

## Task

Prove that for every measurable real-valued predictor f,
\[
\sup_{e\in\mathcal E}R_e(f)
\geq
\sup_{e\in\mathcal E}R_e(f_S).
\]
Consequently, \(f_S\) is a minimizer of the worst-case risk over all measurable real-valued predictors.
