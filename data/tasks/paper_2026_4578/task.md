# Problem

## Setup

Fix an integer \(K\ge2\) and a mean vector \((\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_K)\in\mathbb R^K\), neither depending on the horizon. The means are unknown to the learner. The reward on the \(s\)-th pull of arm \(a\) is
\[
Y_{a,s}=\mu_a+\varepsilon_{a,s},
\]
where all \(\varepsilon_{a,s}\) are independent and identically distributed according to one common non-degenerate, mean-zero, 1-sub-Gaussian law. Here 1-sub-Gaussian means \(\mathbb E e^{\lambda\varepsilon_{a,s}}\le e^{\lambda^2/2}\) for every \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\). The learner observes only the reward of the arm it selects, after making that selection.

Let \(\mu^*=\max_a\mu_a\), \(S^*=\{a:\mu_a=\mu^*\}\), \(r=|S^*|\), and \(\Delta_a=\mu^*-\mu_a>0\) for \(a\notin S^*\). Write \(N_{a,t}\) for the number of pulls of arm \(a\) in the first \(t\) rounds. When \(N_{a,t}\ge1\), define its empirical mean by
\[
\widehat\mu_{a,t}=\frac{1}{N_{a,t}}\sum_{s=1}^{N_{a,t}}Y_{a,s}.
\]
Let \(\mathcal F_t\) be the history generated by the selected arms and their observed rewards through round \(t\).

For each integer horizon \(T\ge K\), choose a deterministic multiplier \(\sigma_T>1\) such that
\[
\sigma_T\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad \sigma_T(\log T)^2/T\longrightarrow0.
\]
First pull each arm once, so \(N_{a,K}=1\). For \(t=K,\ldots,T-1\), conditionally on \(\mathcal F_t\), draw independent
\[
\theta_{a,t+1}\sim N\!\left(\widehat\mu_{a,t},\frac{\sigma_T}{N_{a,t}}\right),\qquad a=1,\ldots,K,
\]
using fresh algorithmic randomness independent of the reward streams, and pull an arm attaining the maximum. Ties have probability zero.

## Task

Prove that this procedure is stable. Namely, as \(T\to\infty\),
\[
\frac{N_{a,T}}{T}\xrightarrow{p}\frac1r\quad(a\in S^*),
\qquad
\frac{N_{a,T}}{\sigma_T\log(T/\sigma_T)}\xrightarrow{p}\frac{2}{\Delta_a^2}\quad(a\notin S^*).
\]
Equivalently, for all sufficiently large horizons, the deterministic normalizers
\[
N^*_{a,T}=\begin{cases}
T/r,&a\in S^*,\\
2\sigma_T\log(T/\sigma_T)/\Delta_a^2,&a\notin S^*
\end{cases}
\]
satisfy \(N^*_{a,T}\to\infty\) and \(N_{a,T}/N^*_{a,T}\to1\) in probability for every arm. Values of these normalizers at finitely many earlier horizons may be assigned arbitrarily positive values.
